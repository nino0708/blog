## A Public Space Sunk into the Ground

A few steps from the south exit of Musashino-Sakaei Station, the entrance to Musashino Place draws visitors downward into the earth. The building, completed in March 2011, has four floors above ground and three below, and was designed by kw+hg architects — a practice led by Yasuko Kawaharada and Takehiko Higa — for the City of Musashino. The exterior gives little away: its street presence is restrained, sitting neatly beside the station without the assertiveness common to civic landmarks. Inside, however, curved walls open into voids that cascade between levels, pulling natural light down to the lower floors through a series of interconnected atriums. The building accommodates four distinct programmes under one roof — a public library, a support centre for teenagers, a lifelong learning centre and a civic activity support centre — connected by corridors and open voids that allow each function to coexist without rigid boundaries.

## Curves Below the City

The organisational logic of Musashino Place rests on the curved wall. Where most buildings use straight partitions to define rooms, kw+hg architects worked with continuous curves that blur the edges between functions, encouraging visitors to wander rather than move purposefully from room to room. The deepest floors include soundproofed spaces for music and rehearsal, addressing the practical challenge of running a quiet library and noisy activity spaces inside the same structure. Light from the atriums reaches floors that might otherwise feel buried, and the library's reading areas retain a sense of openness despite sitting metres below street level. The building attracted attention in Japanese architectural media on completion, and its model of layering multiple civic functions in a compact urban site has been referenced in discussions of public building design. The station-adjacent position in Musashino City makes the facility one of the most accessible civic buildings in the Tokyo metropolitan area.

## Summary

Musashino Place was completed in 2011, designed by kw+hg architects for the City of Musashino, with four floors above ground and three below. The building's defining move is to sink its principal spaces underground while keeping them bright and open through interlocking curved voids. As a civic building that refuses both monumentality and anonymity, it offers a quietly persuasive argument for what a neighbourhood public facility can be — well worth stepping into on a walk through the Musashino-Sakaei area.
