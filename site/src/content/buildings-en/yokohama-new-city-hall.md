## A 155-Metre Tower for Japan's Second-Largest City

On 31 January 2020, a 32-story, 2-basement-level tower was completed at Hommachi 6-50-10 in Yokohama's Naka-ku. The new Yokohama City Hall stands 155.4 metres tall with a total floor area of approximately 142,582 m², designed and built by a joint venture of Takenaka Corporation and Nishimatsu Construction. For a Japanese municipal office building, it is unusually large — a consequence of consolidating administrative functions that had been spread across the ageing 1959 city hall and several satellite offices into a single high-rise.

Yokohama began seriously planning a replacement for its old city hall in the 2010s, opting for a PFI (private finance initiative) scheme to include commercial and hotel floors alongside the government offices. The result is a mixed-use high-rise with public administration occupying the mid-rise floors and private tenants in the lower and upper sections — a model that reduced the city's financial burden while generating activity in the Kannai redevelopment area.

## Seismic Isolation, CASBEE S-Rank, and BCP Design

The building sits on a large seismic isolation system installed in its basement, designed to keep the building functional as a business continuity planning (BCP) hub after a major earthquake. On-site power generation and emergency water storage allow critical city operations to continue even if surrounding infrastructure is disrupted.

For environmental performance, the building earned an S-rank from CASBEE (Comprehensive Assessment System for Built Environment Efficiency) — the highest tier. High-efficiency HVAC, daylight harvesting, and intelligent lighting controls collectively push the building's energy performance significantly below that of a conventional office tower of comparable size.

## Summary

Thirty-two floors above ground, two below, 155.4 m tall, 142,582 m² in total — Yokohama's new city hall, completed in January 2020 at Hommachi 6-50-10, is a seismically isolated, CASBEE S-ranked public high-rise designed and built by Takenaka Corporation and Nishimatsu Construction. It consolidates Yokohama's city government in a single landmark tower within walking distance of Sakuragicho and Bashamichi stations, bringing the administration of Japan's second-largest city under one roof for the first time.
