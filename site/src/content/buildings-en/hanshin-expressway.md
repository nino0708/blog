## A Network Born in the Economic Miracle

The Hanshin Expressway is a network of urban toll roads centered on the Osaka and Kobe metropolitan areas, with sections extending into Kyoto and Nara Prefectures. Its first route — a portion of Route 1 (the Loop) in central Osaka — opened on May 31, 1964, in time for the Tokyo Olympics that autumn. The network has since grown to approximately 260 kilometers of elevated and tunnel roadway, all operated by Hanshin Expressway Company (formerly the Hanshin Expressway Public Corporation).

The system is organized around two main clusters: the Osaka routes, which include the loop line circling central Osaka and several radial routes reaching toward the suburbs, and the Kobe routes, which connect Osaka's urban core to central Kobe and the port areas. Route 3 (the Kobe Route) forms the main link between the two cities, while Route 5 (the Bay Shore Route) runs along the reclaimed land bordering Osaka Bay, serving container terminals, industrial facilities, and the coastal developments at Rinku Town.

## Elevated Infrastructure and the Earthquake Test

A striking feature of the Hanshin Expressway is the degree to which it passes over existing city streets, rivers, and canals rather than occupying new right-of-way. The resulting elevated structures have become inseparable from the visual character of central Osaka — a constant presence in the urban field of view, threading between office towers and threading along the Yodo River embankment.

That infrastructure was severely tested by the Great Hanshin Earthquake of January 1995, which toppled a long stretch of the Kobe Route's elevated columns in one of the most photographed images of the disaster. Reconstruction was completed within about two years, and the rebuilt sections incorporate significantly improved seismic design. The earthquake fundamentally changed how Japan approaches the seismic retrofitting of elevated expressways, and the Hanshin network has since undergone broad-based reinforcement.

## Summary

The Hanshin Expressway, opened in 1964 and now spanning approximately 260 kilometers across four prefectures, is the arterial road network of the Hanshin metropolitan region. Its elevated structures have shaped the visual character of central Osaka and Kobe for more than six decades, and the system continues to carry the region's commuter, freight, and port traffic. The Bay Shore Route along Osaka Bay offers a dramatic vantage point over the coastal industrial landscape — a reminder of how much of the region's economic life runs just below.
