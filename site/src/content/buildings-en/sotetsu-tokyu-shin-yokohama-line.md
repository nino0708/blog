## A New Spine Through Yokohama

On 18 March 2023 two railway companies opened a combined 12.1-kilometre underground corridor that reshaped commuting in the greater Yokohama area. Tokyu Corporation's Shin-Yokohama Line (5.8 km, Hiyoshi to Shin-Yokohama) and Sagami Railway's Shin-Yokohama Line (6.3 km, Shin-Yokohama to Hazawa-Yokohama-Kokudai-Mae) were built by the Japan Railway Construction, Transport and Technology Agency under an infrastructure-separation arrangement: JRTT owns the tunnels and stations, while Tokyu and Sotetsu operate the trains. Total construction cost came to approximately ¥319.6 billion.

The corridor introduced two new stations. Shin-Tsunashima, in Kohoku Ward, Kawasaki, is a deep underground stop adjacent to the existing Tokyu Toyoko Line station at Tsunashima, giving the neighbourhood a second access point and direct travel toward Shibuya. Shin-Yokohama serves as the pivotal interchange: Sotetsu trains surface at Hazawa-Yokohama-Kokudai-Mae and run underground to Shin-Yokohama, where passengers can transfer to the Tokaido Shinkansen for the first time without changing trains again at Yokohama or Kikuna. The opening day also saw through-service begin across four railway systems: Sotetsu Main Line and Izumino Line trains now run directly onto Tokyu tracks, continuing through the Toyoko Line to the Tokyo Metro Fukutoshin Line, Tobu Tojo Line, and to the Meguro Line, Toei Mita Line, Tokyo Metro Namboku Line, and Saitama Rapid Railway.

## Shin-Yokohama as Urban Hub

The strategic value of the line centres on Shin-Yokohama. Before 2023, Sotetsu passengers travelling to the Shinkansen terminal had to transfer at Yokohama or Kikuna; the new line provides a one-seat ride. Combined with the Sotetsu–JR Direct Line opened in 2019, Hazawa-Yokohama-Kokudai-Mae station now connects Sotetsu's network to both JR (via Musashi-Kosugi to Shinjuku) and Tokyu (via Shin-Yokohama to Hiyoshi and beyond). This dual exit transformed what had been a lightly-used new station into a genuine transfer hub.

## Summary

The Sotetsu–Tokyu Direct Line opened 18 March 2023, linking western Kanagawa to Tokyo's core. The Tokyu Shin-Yokohama Line (5.8 km) and Sotetsu Shin-Yokohama Line (6.3 km) together cost approximately ¥319.6 billion and were built under an infrastructure-separation model. Two new stations — Shin-Tsunashima and Shin-Yokohama — now sit on a network that runs without transfer from Ebina to Urawa-Misono, drawing Yokohama's northern suburbs into direct, unbroken contact with central Tokyo.
