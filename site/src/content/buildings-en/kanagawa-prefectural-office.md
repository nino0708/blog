## King of the Nihon Odori

Standing at the head of the Nihon Odori boulevard in Naka Ward, Yokohama, the Kanagawa Prefectural Government Main Building was completed in 1928. The structure rises approximately 49 metres, with five floors above ground, one below, and a four-storey tower section at the top. Its base is clad in brick-toned tile and white stone, with an Art Deco framework beneath a traditional copper-tiled roof — an arrangement that exemplifies the Imperial Crown Style fashionable in early Showa Japan. The design originated from the first-prize entry of a competition, submitted by Karo Obi, and was developed into final plans by the prefectural Department of Home Affairs.

Yokohama residents affectionately call it "King." Together with the Yokohama Customs Building ("Queen") and the Yokohama City Opening Memorial Hall ("Jack"), it forms the celebrated "Three Towers of Yokohama." Viewed from Nihon Odori, the tapering crown of the building rises emphatically against the sky, anchoring the visual axis that connects the boulevard to the port.

## A Building That Reflects Its Era

The Imperial Crown Style placed a modern steel-and-concrete frame beneath Japanese-style roofing and decorative elements — a fusion promoted in the late Taisho and early Showa periods to blend Western technology with national identity. The Kanagawa Prefectural Government Building is considered one of the earliest and most accomplished examples of the style. Its construction came just five years after the Great Kanto Earthquake of 1923, and the rebuilding of the prefectural seat carried both practical urgency and symbolic weight.

The exterior has aged gracefully over nearly a century. In December 2019, the national government designated it an Important Cultural Property — a rare distinction for a building still in daily administrative use. Parts of the interior, including staircases and original fittings from the 1920s, survive to give visitors a sense of the building's age.

## Summary

The Kanagawa Prefectural Government Main Building was completed in 1928, stands roughly 49 metres tall, and comprises five above-ground floors, one basement level, and a four-storey tower. Known as "King" of the Three Towers of Yokohama, it has been a fixture of the city's harbour front for nearly a century and continues to serve as a working government office. A walk along Nihon Odori, with its plane trees and wide pavement, makes an ideal approach to this quietly remarkable building.
