## A Tower Rising Over the Tosabori River

The Kitahama stands at 1-7-5 Koraibashi, Chuo Ward, Osaka, in the heart of the Kitahama financial district along the Tosabori River. Completed in 2009, this 54-story, one-basement-level supertall condominium reaches 209.35 m and houses 484 units. Designed by Kajima Design, the slender rectangular form is clad in a glass curtain wall that shifts in appearance throughout the day — pale and luminous in the morning, turning amber in the evening light. Looking up from one of the bridges over the Tosabori River, the tower surges past the surrounding low- and mid-rise cityscape in a way that makes its height genuinely surprising. The neighborhood around Kitahama Station (Osaka Metro Sakaisuji Line and Keihan Railway) retains a remarkable number of Meiji and Taisho era stone and brick buildings — former banks and securities offices — whose restrained horizontal scale throws the tower's vertical ambition into sharp relief, making a walk here feel like a conversation between eras.

## A Home in Osaka's Historic Financial Quarter

Kitahama has been the economic heart of Osaka since the Edo period, when it was home to Japan's rice commodity exchange and formed the financial spine of the country's commercial capital. The Meiji era brought a stock exchange and waves of banking institutions that still define the neighborhood's character. When a mid-2000s surge in central-city repopulation coincided with a nationwide boom in supertall condominium construction, Kitahama's unusual mix of financial heritage and riverside location made it a compelling setting for a landmark residential tower. The Kitahama was among the tallest residential buildings in Osaka Prefecture when it opened, and upper-floor residents can enjoy views said to extend to Osaka Bay and the Rokko mountains, with the canals and bridges of Osaka's historic waterway network spread below.

## Summary

Completed in 2009, 54 stories, 209.35 m — The Kitahama is a defining supertall condominium of Osaka, designed by Kajima Design, its 484 units occupying the rare convergence of a historic financial district and a riverside promenade. Walk the Tosabori riverbank and look up: the slim tower, framed by old stone buildings and the river below, tells the story of a city still reshaping itself.
