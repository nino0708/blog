## A Residential Tower in the Heart of Old Finance

The Kitahama rises 209.35 metres over 54 floors in the Koraibashi district of Chuo Ward, Osaka. Completed in May 2009, the slender residential tower stands on a site in Kitahama — the neighbourhood that housed the Osaka Securities Exchange and shaped the financial culture of modern Osaka. Design was shared by three firms: Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei, Nihon Sekkei and Kajima Design.

The tower's height places it among the tallest residential buildings in Osaka Prefecture. Its narrow footprint means the structure reads as a single sharp vertical element against the Osaka skyline — especially noticeable from across the Tosaborigawa River, where the river's surface doubles the reflection of the tower on calm mornings.

## Kitahama: Finance District Turned Residential Quarter

During the Meiji and Taishō eras, Kitahama was the nerve centre of Osaka's economy. Banks, brokerages and trading houses clustered here around the original Osaka Securities Exchange building. Over the postwar decades the district gradually opened up to mixed uses, and by the 2000s high-end residential development had become part of the area's identity.

The Kitahama's 2009 completion represented the district's embrace of that residential shift, bringing world-class residential heights to a neighbourhood still surrounded by the fine-grain architecture of old Osaka finance. Walking the riverside path along Tosaborigawa and looking up at the 54-storey tower against the surrounding low-rise cityscape is one of the more memorable urban contrasts Osaka has to offer.

## Summary

Completed in 2009, 54 storeys, 209.35 metres. The Kitahama stands as one of Osaka's tallest residential towers, planted in the historic financial quarter of Kitahama beside the Tosaborigawa River. A stroll along the riverbank at dusk, with the tower reflected in the water, captures something essential about the layered, ever-changing character of Osaka's city centre.
