## A Thousand Panels, One Façade

TOYAMA Kirari stands at the Nishimachi intersection in the heart of Toyama city, its six storeys clad in 1,000 panels of glass, aluminium and white granite arranged in a shifting geometric pattern. Since the building opened in 2015, the façade has acted as a kind of sundial for the district: the granite catches the morning light as a cold, granular shimmer; the metal panels glow orange at dusk. The building sits at a measured height relative to its neighbours, integrating into the existing commercial streetscape rather than asserting dominance over it. Kengo Kuma and Associates approached the cladding as a contemporary reinterpretation of the shoji screen and latticed transom of traditional Japanese architecture — familiar materials reordered into a new grammar of surface.

## Library Below, Gallery Above

The interior stacks two public institutions in a single frame: the main branch of the Toyama City Library occupies the lower floors, while the Toyama Glass Art Museum fills the upper galleries. Visitors enter through a shared lobby and can move between borrowing books and viewing international glass sculpture without passing through any transition. Cedar-wood louvres line the central atrium, extending the material contrast of the exterior into the interior and softening the light that filters through the glass panels. Toyama has long been a centre of studio glass-making in Japan, and the museum consolidates that heritage in a civic setting. Since opening, the building has served as both a daily neighbourhood amenity and a cultural destination, strengthening the walkability of the city centre along the tramway corridors.

## Summary

TOYAMA Kirari opened in 2015, six floors above ground, designed by Kengo Kuma and Associates for Toyama City. The 1,000-panel façade and the stacked library-museum programme have established the building as the new civic face of central Toyama. For anyone travelling to the city of glass craft, the building is steps from the tramline — a quiet, light-filled place that repays more than a passing glance.
