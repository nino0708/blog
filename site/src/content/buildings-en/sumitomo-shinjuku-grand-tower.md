## A 195-Meter Tower on the Edge of Nishi-Shinjuku

Most visitors who picture Nishi-Shinjuku's skyline think immediately of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building or the cluster of corporate towers that line the broad avenues near the west exit. But cross Ome Kaido and walk a little further west, and a different building comes into view — the Sumitomo Fudosan Shinjuku Grand Tower, completed in October 2011. With 40 floors above ground and a height of approximately 195 meters, it was designed by Nikken Sekkei in steel-frame construction.

What makes this tower distinctive is its mixed-use programme. Rather than a pure office block, the building integrates office floors with residential units within a single structure — an approach that reflected the thinking of early 2010s urban development in central Tokyo, where live-work proximity was increasingly seen as both a planning goal and a selling point.

## How a Local Association Built a Skyscraper

The project was organized by the Nishi-Shinjuku 8-chome Naruko District Urban Redevelopment Association — a grouping of local landowners who pooled their land rights to enable a comprehensive redevelopment. This cooperative model, in which scattered low-rise plots are consolidated into a single high-rise parcel, is the standard vehicle for urban renewal in Japan and has been used across central Tokyo for decades.

The resulting building stands along Ome Kaido with a clean rectangular profile that rises well above its surroundings. Its location slightly removed from the original Nishi-Shinjuku high-rise cluster illustrates how that cluster has expanded outward over time, absorbing adjacent urban fabric block by block.

## Summary

The Sumitomo Fudosan Shinjuku Grand Tower was completed in 2011 with 40 floors above ground and a height of approximately 195 meters. Built through a landowner-organized redevelopment association and designed by Nikken Sekkei, it stands as an example of how Tokyo's high-rise development has extended beyond the original subcenter zone into surrounding neighborhoods.

Extend your Nishi-Shinjuku walk along Ome Kaido and you will find this tower where the high-rise city meets the quieter residential streets beyond.
