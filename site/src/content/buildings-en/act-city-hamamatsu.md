## The Instrument City's Tallest Landmark

On the north side of JR Hamamatsu Station in Chuo Ward, Hamamatsu City, Shizuoka Prefecture, Act Tower stands 45 storeys tall with its highest point reaching 212.77 metres. Completed in July 1994, it is the centrepiece of the ACT CITY Hamamatsu complex and the tallest structure in Shizuoka Prefecture. For more than thirty years since its completion, the tower has defined the station-area skyline of a city known worldwide as the home of Yamaha, Kawai Musical Instruments and Roland.

The building's facade is composed of vertical stripes that evoke the profile of a harmonica — an intentional reference to the musical instrument manufacturing heritage that shapes Hamamatsu's civic identity. Design was led by Nihon Sekkei and Mitsubishi Estate, with Dai-ichi Life Insurance and Mitsubishi Estate as the developers. The total gross floor area reaches approximately 150,978 square metres. The upper floors of the tower house a hotel, and the observation floor at the summit offers panoramic views across the Hamamatsu cityscape and Enshu-nada Bay.

## A Station-Front Redevelopment at Regional Scale

ACT CITY Hamamatsu was conceived as a major redevelopment of a large site on the north side of Hamamatsu Station. Works began in August 1991 and were completed in July 1994, requiring approximately three years of construction. Beyond the tower itself, the complex integrates concert halls of varying capacities, exhibition facilities, a shopping mall and publicly operated civic spaces — a mixed-use ensemble that went beyond the typical station-front formula for a regional city of its time.

Hamamatsu's economy has long been driven by manufacturing that punches well above the weight of its population: alongside automotive production, the musical instrument and music industry has been a defining force. The decision to anchor the redevelopment's cultural programme in concert halls and music facilities reflects a rare alignment between a city's economic identity and the ambitions of a public-private regeneration project — a model that still repays attention.
