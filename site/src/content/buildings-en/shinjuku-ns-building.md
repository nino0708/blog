## A Glass Roof Crowning West Shinjuku

In Nishi-Shinjuku, Tokyo, the Shinjuku NS Building rises 30 floors and 133 meters above the dense grid of high-rise offices that define this part of the city. Completed in September 1982, it was jointly developed by Nippon Life Insurance and Sumitomo Realty — the "NS" in its name standing for the initials of both companies. Nikken Sekkei, one of Japan's leading architecture firms, served as designer.

The building arrived as the second generation of Nishi-Shinjuku skyscrapers, following earlier neighbors such as the [Shinjuku Mitsui Building](/en/buildings/shinjuku-mitsui-building/) and the [Shinjuku Sumitomo Building](/en/buildings/shinjuku-sumitomo-building/). With a total floor area of approximately 166,767 square meters spanning basement level 3 to floor 30, it stands as a substantial presence in the already formidable skyline of West Shinjuku. Its curtain-wall facade, however, gives little away: the real drama is inside.

## The Full-Height Atrium Experiment

Step through the entrance and the building reveals its true character. A continuous atrium rises through all 30 above-ground floors, capped by a glass roof that floods the interior with natural light throughout the day. At the center of this open shaft stands a large Seiko clock — a fixture that has become something of a landmark for visitors to Nishi-Shinjuku over the decades.

When the building was designed in the late 1970s, most high-rise office towers in Japan stacked their functions around a closed service core. The decision to sacrifice rentable floor space in favor of a shared civic void was an ambitious departure. The resulting space feels closer to an interior public square than a conventional office lobby, inviting pedestrians to linger in a way that plain-faced corporate towers rarely do. More than forty years on, while several Nishi-Shinjuku towers have entered phases of renewal or redevelopment, the NS Building's atrium continues to hold its character intact.

## Summary

Completed in 1982, the Shinjuku NS Building is a 30-story, 133-meter office tower with a total floor area of around 166,767 square meters. Jointly developed by Nippon Life Insurance and Sumitomo Realty and designed by Nikken Sekkei, its most distinctive feature remains the full-height atrium that draws daylight deep into the building. When walking through Nishi-Shinjuku's canyon of towers, it is worth stepping inside to experience what an earlier generation of architects imagined when they asked what public space could mean inside a skyscraper.
