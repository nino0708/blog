## An Octagonal Icon in the Imperial Forest

Nestled within the wooded grounds of Kitanomaru Park in Chiyoda City, just north of the Imperial Palace, Nippon Budokan opened on 3 October 1964 — a week before the Tokyo Olympics began. The building was designed by architect Mamoru Yamada, who drew on the octagonal Yume-dono hall at Horyuji Temple in Nara. The resulting silhouette — an eight-sided arena capped with a brass orb — is unmistakable from any angle. Construction was carried out by Takenaka Corporation, and the structure rises approximately 42 metres above the park floor.

The venue made its global debut at the 1964 Tokyo Olympics as the site for judo competition, the first time judo appeared as an official Olympic discipline. With a seating capacity of approximately 14,471, the arena bore witness to that historic moment. Judo, kendo, karate, and sumo championships have been held here ever since, cementing Budokan's role as the spiritual home of Japanese martial arts.

## From Martial-Arts Hall to Concert Legend

In 1966, just two years after the building opened, the Beatles performed three concerts on the Budokan stage. The choice of a martial-arts venue for a rock band sparked fierce debate at the time, yet the performances became a landmark moment in Japanese music history and established Budokan as a premier concert destination. The phrase "playing Budokan" became shorthand for the pinnacle of an artist's career in Japan, and countless headline acts have followed.

The octagonal plan is well suited to both disciplines. The near-equal sightlines from every direction have allowed the arena to serve two radically different purposes for more than sixty years. The building has undergone several renovation cycles since the 2010s, with seismic upgrades and infrastructure renewals keeping it in active service.

## Summary

Nippon Budokan opened in 1964, stands approximately 42 metres tall, and holds around 14,471 spectators. Mamoru Yamada's octagonal roof and brass orb have presided over Kitanomaru Park for more than six decades, witnessing world-class martial-arts bouts and legendary concerts in equal measure. If you are walking the grounds of the Imperial Palace or Kitanomaru Park, look for the arena's distinctive silhouette emerging from the trees.
