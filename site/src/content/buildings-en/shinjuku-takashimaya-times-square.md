## A Department Store Complex That Remade Shinjuku's South Exit

In 1996, a large-scale commercial complex opened directly connected to the south exit of JR Shinjuku Station. Shinjuku Takashimaya Times Square, designed jointly by Nikken Sekkei and Nihon Sekkei, rises 14 stories above and one below ground, integrating a Takashimaya department store with Tokyu Hands, Junkudo Books, and Kinokuniya Bookstore under one roof. Its opening reshaped the character of Shinjuku's south exit almost overnight.

Shinjuku has long been Tokyo's most complex transit hub, with the east and west exits developing their own distinct commercial and office clusters after the war. The mid-1990s marked a turning point: land formerly held by the Japan Railway Settlement Corporation around the south exit came open for private development. Times Square emerged from this wave of redevelopment at Sendagaya 5-24-2 in Shibuya, right on the former National Railways land. The adjacent Shinjuku Southern Terrace pedestrian promenade was developed at almost the same time.

## A Vertical Commercial City at the Station

The "Times Square" branding covers a cluster of interconnected buildings. The main Takashimaya building and the adjacent Times Square Building — a seven-story structure housing Tokyu Hands — share a low-rise podium that ties them together. The complex stacks department store floors, one of Japan's largest bookstores, home improvement retail, restaurants, and a cinema in a single tower, creating what could be described as a vertical commercial city where each tenant occupies whole floors rather than sharing them with others.

The connection to Shinjuku Station's south concourse — across the Koshu Kaido expressway viaduct — gives the complex direct access to hundreds of thousands of daily commuters passing through one of the world's busiest stations.

## Summary

Fourteen floors above ground, one below, opened in 1996 — Shinjuku Takashimaya Times Square at Sendagaya 5-24-2 in Shibuya transformed the south exit of Shinjuku Station into a vertical retail destination. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and Nihon Sekkei, it fused a full-scale department store with the city's largest bookstores and specialist retailers into a single complex that has anchored Shinjuku's south side for nearly three decades.
