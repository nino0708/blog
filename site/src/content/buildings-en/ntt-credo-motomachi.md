## Hiroshima's First 150-Meter Skyscraper

At 6-78 Motomachi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima City, the NTT Credo Motomachi Building stands 35 floors and 150 meters above the Kamiyacho intersection, just south of Hiroshima Castle. Completed in 1994 and developed by NTT Credo, the tower was the first building in Hiroshima — and among the first in the Chugoku region — to reach 150 meters, bringing a new scale of high-rise development to a city still writing its postwar story.

The complex is divided into a hotel tower housing the Rihga Royal Hotel Hiroshima in its upper floors and a lower commercial annex. The glass curtain wall and stone cladding give the exterior a restrained, formal presence suited to its position as a business and hospitality anchor in central Hiroshima. From the hotel's upper floors, guests look across Hiroshima Castle's moat and green grounds — a direct juxtaposition of a modern skyscraper with one of the city's key historical landmarks.

## From Reconstruction to High-Rise: Hiroshima's Urban Trajectory

The Motomachi district lies immediately south of Hiroshima Castle's grounds, on land that held military facilities before the Second World War and was rebuilt as a commercial and civic area during postwar reconstruction. NTT Credo's development of the site drew on land tied to NTT's legacy infrastructure in the city, converting an idle parcel into a mixed-use anchor for central Hiroshima.

The building's 150-meter height went unmatched in Hiroshima for many years. Its position at the Kamiyacho intersection places it at the junction of two main tram lines and the approach to the Hondori shopping arcade, making it a natural orientation point for anyone navigating central Hiroshima. The Rihga Royal's presence also established a high-end hospitality base that has supported the city's role as a venue for international conferences and diplomatic visits.

## Summary

The NTT Credo Motomachi Building was completed in 1994 with 35 floors at 150 meters. Developed by NTT Credo, it was the first skyscraper in Hiroshima to reach that height and introduced a large-scale hotel tower to the city center. A walk from the Kamiyacho tram stop north along the castle moat passes the base of this tower and continues into Hiroshima's green historic heart — a short route that places the city's rebuilt postwar urban form in direct conversation with its prewar castle district.
