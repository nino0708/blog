## A Private High-Rise at the Heart of Japan's Government District

Nagatacho is a name that immediately evokes Japan's political center — the National Diet Building, the Prime Minister's official residence, the headquarters of major parties. Yet quietly anchored in a corner of this district at 2-13-10 Nagatacho stands Prudential Tower, a 38-story, 3-basement-level building completed in 2002, rising to approximately 158 meters. Mori Building was the developer, with Taisei Corporation serving as both architect and builder. The project was recognized with the 46th BCS Award in 2005. The nearest station, Akasaka-Mitsuke, is roughly a minute's walk away — uncommonly convenient for a district defined more by government than by daily footfall.

The building stacks uses vertically: retail on the 1st and 2nd floors, office space from the 3rd through the 24th, and rental apartments (Prudential Tower Residence) on the 26th floor and above. That a building combining shops, offices, and residences should stand here, in the shadow of the Diet and adjacent to the PM's residence, speaks to a dimension of Nagatacho that the political-center image tends to obscure.

## The Name, the Place, and the Quiet of Nagatacho

The name "Prudential" traces to Prudential Life Insurance of Japan, the local subsidiary of U.S.-based Prudential Financial, which has offices in the building. An American company's name on a skyscraper in Japan's political capital makes for an incongruous-sounding combination — yet it fits the broader pattern of Chiyoda, where financial institutions, insurers, and multinational firms have long clustered within walking distance of government buildings.

Walking the slopes of Nagatacho, one passes between official buildings that anchor national policy and then encounters towers like this one — privately built, mixed in use, named after a private company. The BCS Award-winning exterior carries a composed restraint that suits its setting. The layering of official and commercial in the same streetscape, without one overwhelming the other, is part of what makes Chiyoda's urban fabric distinctive and worth exploring on foot.

## Summary

Prudential Tower, completed in 2002, is a 38-story mixed-use high-rise (approx. 158 m) in Nagatacho, Chiyoda. Developed by Mori Building and designed-built by Taisei Corporation, it combines office floors below with rental residences above, and received the 46th BCS Award (2005) for its architectural quality. Named for Prudential Life Insurance, it is a telling example of how private development has woven itself into Japan's governmental quarter over the postwar decades. The walk from Akasaka-Mitsuke up the hill toward the Diet building passes this tower — worth pausing for a look.
