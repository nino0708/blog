## A Glass Pavilion on Oita's Waterfront

The Oita Prefectural Art Museum (OPAM) opened on 24 April 2015 in the heart of Oita City, at 2-1 Kotobuki-machi. The building was completed on 31 October 2014, with internal preparation running until spring of the following year. Designed by Shigeru Ban Architects and built by the Kajima–Umebayashi joint venture, it covers a total floor area of 17,084 m² across four floors above ground and one below, with a site area of 12,064 m².

## The Bamboo Lattice and Its Meaning

The most striking feature of OPAM is its exterior: a diagonal glass lattice that evokes the bamboo weaving traditions of Oita Prefecture. Full-height glass panels filter natural light into the galleries, allowing sunlight to shift across the interiors throughout the day without overwhelming the artwork. At the center of the building is a two-storey atrium, an open public space that blurs the boundary between the museum and the city outside.

Shigeru Ban received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2014, the same year OPAM was completed. His approach here follows themes found across his career — using transparency to draw the outdoors inside, treating a building as something the public inhabits rather than merely enters. For OPAM, that sensibility is well suited to the museum's location in a compact city center where the street and the building should feel continuous.

## Oita's Cultural Investment

OPAM was developed by Oita Prefecture as part of a broader effort to strengthen cultural infrastructure in the prefectural capital. Its location close to JR Oita Station makes it accessible to both residents and visitors traveling from Beppu, Yufuin, and elsewhere in the region. Since opening, the museum has hosted major exhibitions and attracted visitors from across Kyushu.

## Summary

Completed in October 2014 and opened in April 2015, OPAM covers 17,084 m² in four above-ground floors. Its bamboo-lattice glass facade, designed by Pritzker laureate Shigeru Ban, gives Oita City a distinctive cultural landmark that is well worth a detour on any trip to Kyushu.
