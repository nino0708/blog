## The Gate to Hokkaido, Rising Above Its Own Tracks

Kita 5-jo Nishi 2-chome, Chuo Ward, Sapporo. South of JR Sapporo Station, where the elevated tracks of the Hakodate Main Line pass overhead and the former at-grade platforms once spread across the land below, JR Tower rises 173 metres into the northern sky. The building was completed on 5 February 2003 and opened to the public on 6 March of the same year. Designed by Nihon Sekkei, the tower has 38 floors above ground and four below.

The development is inseparable from a major reconstruction of Sapporo Station itself. In 1988, the at-grade section of the Hakodate Main Line was elevated to allow better interchange with the subway and to unlock the former surface track corridor for redevelopment. JR Hokkaido used that freed land to build a vertically integrated city around its own station: a Daimaru department store, the Stellar Place, Paseo, Apia, and Esta retail complexes, an office wing, the JR Tower Hotel Nikko Sapporo occupying floors 23 to 34, and the T38 observation deck at the very top. Total floor area across the complex approaches 350,000 square metres.

## Hokkaido's Tallest, the Station's Backbone

At 173 metres, JR Tower is the tallest building in Hokkaido. The T38 observation deck on the 38th floor commands views across the Ishikari Plain on clear days, with the Hidaka mountain range visible in the distance. The hotel, observation floor, and upper restaurant form a vertical sequence that turns arrival by train into an immediate encounter with the city's highest point — a deliberate spatial compression of the "welcome to Hokkaido" moment.

The building's connection to Sapporo Station's covered concourse is a functional asset in a city where winter temperatures can drop well below zero and snowfall is heavy from November through March. The underground pedestrian network linking JR Tower to the surrounding retail wings means that movement across the station precinct can be managed entirely indoors, a feature that distinguishes northern Japanese city centres from their counterparts in milder climates.

JR Hokkaido's decision to develop its own land as a vertical mixed-use tower follows the model established by JR Central in Nagoya with the JR Central Towers in 1999. The two projects share a logic: a railway company with large landholdings adjacent to its busiest station invests in a high-density complex that serves as both real estate and infrastructure, drawing passengers, tourists, and daily users into a single building.

## Summary

JR Tower, completed in 2003, is a 38-floor, 173-metre building and Hokkaido's tallest structure. Developed by JR Hokkaido on the site of Sapporo Station's former at-grade tracks, it packages a hotel, department store, retail complex, offices, and observation deck into a single tower that defines the city's skyline.
