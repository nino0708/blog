## The Last Piece of Sunshine City

Sunshine City Prince Hotel opened in April 1980 as the final component of the Sunshine City complex in Ikebukuro's eastern district. Designed by Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei, the tower rises 37 floors above ground and four below, reaching a height of approximately 130 metres, with a total floor area of 86,910 square metres. Its completion marked the full realisation of the Sunshine City development — one of Japan's most ambitious post-war urban redevelopment projects — which had been rolling out phase by phase since the late 1970s.

## A Heavy History, A New Skyline

The site carries considerable historical weight. The land once housed Sugamo Prison, known to the world after World War II as the detention facility for Class-A war crimes suspects tried by the International Military Tribunal for the Far East. The prison was demolished following its closure in 1971, and the New Urban Development Centre (later renamed Sunshine City Corporation) led the transformation of the grounds. Sunshine 60, the neighbourhood's iconic skyscraper, opened in 1978; the World Import Mart and Alpa commercial building followed in subsequent years. Prince Hotels took on the hotel operations, establishing a full-service city hotel with convention facilities and restaurants in the lower floors. The hotel's underground connections to Sunshine City's extensive subterranean shopping network made it one of Ikebukuro's most accessible destinations. While Sunshine 60 drew attention for its all-glass curtain wall, the Prince Hotel tower added a more restrained presence that rounded out the complex's skyline.

## Summary

Sunshine City Prince Hotel was completed in 1980 with 37 floors and a height of approximately 130 metres. As the final structure in the Sunshine City development, it stands on the former Sugamo Prison grounds in a part of Ikebukuro that has been shaped by one of the most dramatic reversals in Tokyo's post-war urban history. The tower and the wider complex remain defining features of Ikebukuro's eastern skyline today — worth seeking out alongside Sunshine 60 for anyone tracing the city's redevelopment story.
