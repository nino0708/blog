## A Stadium Built by Supporters

Panasonic Stadium Suita — officially the Suita City Football Stadium — was completed on 30 September 2015 and opened for use in October of that year at 4-30 Senri Bampaku-koen, Suita City, Osaka. Designed and built by Takenaka Corporation, the building rises six floors to a maximum height of 40.33 metres, with a total floor area of 66,509 m². It serves as the home ground of Gamba Osaka and accommodates approximately 40,000 spectators. The venue is used exclusively for football and rugby, with no running track separating the seats from the pitch.

## Fan-Funded Construction

What sets Suita Stadium apart from other large Japanese sports venues is its funding model. The stadium was built almost entirely without public money, relying instead on donations from Gamba Osaka supporters, businesses, and organizations. Approximately 14 billion yen was raised through this campaign. The project was widely described at the time as a rare case in Japanese football of a supporter-driven home ground — a model that drew attention across the sport in Japan and was held up as evidence of the depth of fan culture that had grown around Jリーグ.

Takenaka Corporation's design features steeply raked stands that place spectators close to the pitch, increasing the sense of involvement during matches. The steep angle of the seating is deliberate: in a football-only venue without an athletics track, the design can prioritize the viewing experience over multi-purpose flexibility.

## A New Layer in Bampaku-koen

The stadium stands within Senri Bampaku-koen, the park that occupies the former site of Expo 1970. The Taiyo no To (Tower of the Sun) by Taro Okamoto still stands at the park's center, and the site continues to draw visitors through its green spaces and cultural facilities. The addition of a major sports venue brings a new layer of activity to a site already associated with large public gatherings — connecting the park's legacy of international events to a modern stadium culture.

Panasonic acquired the naming rights effective January 2018, giving the venue its current commercial name.

## Summary

Completed in September 2015 and spanning 66,509 m² across six floors, Panasonic Stadium Suita stands in Suita's Bampaku-koen as Japan's first supporter-funded football stadium. Its steep stands bring fans close to the pitch, and its setting in the park of the 1970 World Exposition gives every match a backdrop with a story. Worth a visit the next time you are in Osaka.
