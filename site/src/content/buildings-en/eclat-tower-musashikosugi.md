## A Tower Wrapped in Green

A few minutes from the south exit of Musashikosugi Station, a tower comes into view whose façade is threaded with bands of living green. Eclat Tower Musashikosugi was completed in 2013 — 39 floors above ground and 2 below — in Kosugicho 3-chome, Nakahara Ward, Kawasaki. The project was developed as a joint venture by Tokyu Corporation, Tokyu Land Corporation, Marubeni, and Tepco Real Estate, with construction carried out by a joint enterprise of Nishimatsu Construction and Sato Kogyo.

What sets the building apart is its wall-planting system, which lines balconies across the full height of the tower. The result is a continuous green surface that softens the concrete mass and echoes the street trees below. That system won the 2013 Good Design Award — recognition that the greenery represented a genuine environmental commitment rather than a decorative afterthought. The building holds 326 dwellings in total, and within Musashikosugi's growing cluster of high-rise towers, it remains the one most readily identified by its colour.

## Redevelopment and Its Counterweight

Musashikosugi underwent one of the most dramatic transformations of any station neighbourhood in greater Tokyo during the 2000s and 2010s. The convergence of the Tokyu Toyoko, Meguro, JR Nambu, and Yokosuka lines created a powerful transit hub, and the former industrial plots around Kosugicho 3-chome were steadily claimed by high-rise redevelopment. Eclat Tower Musashikosugi was built within that wave, joining a skyline that had grown rapidly from low-rise factory blocks.

The wall-planting choice reads as a response to that density — an attempt to restore, within the building's own skin, some of the green cover that intensive development tends to remove. The Good Design Award citation acknowledged that logic, and subsequent projects in the area have continued to treat planted surfaces as a part of the design vocabulary.

## Summary

Eclat Tower Musashikosugi was completed in 2013, standing 39 floors above ground with 2 basement levels and 326 dwellings, developed by a four-company joint venture anchored by the Tokyu Group. Its wall-planting façade, recognised with the 2013 Good Design Award, makes it one of the more visually distinctive towers in a neighbourhood defined by rapid change. Walk south from Musashikosugi Station to see the green crown rising above the rooftops — an unusual counterpoint to the glass and concrete surrounding it.
