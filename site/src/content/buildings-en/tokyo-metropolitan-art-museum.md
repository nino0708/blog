## Below the Trees of Ueno Park

In Ueno Park, Taito City, Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum stands within walking distance of the Tokyo National Museum. The building was completed in September 1975 and is one of the defining works of architect Kunio Maekawa. Its exterior is clad in a warm brick-red tile that settles comfortably among the trees, and much of the exhibition space is located underground — a deliberate design move that keeps the building profile low and the park's canopy undisturbed.

The museum has a longer history than the present building suggests. Its predecessor, the Tokyo Prefectural Art Museum, opened in 1926 to a design by Shin'ichiro Okada. When ageing fabric and accumulated additions made a full replacement necessary, work on the new structure began in 1972. Maekawa's design had nothing in common with the old building except its location: the same corner of Ueno Park that had housed public art for nearly half a century.

## Maekawa in Ueno

Kunio Maekawa is closely associated with Ueno Park: the Tokyo Bunka Kaikan concert hall (1961), just a short walk north, is also his work. The two buildings share a disciplined use of raw concrete and a respect for the wooded setting. The Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum, however, shifts from concrete to brick-red tile — the material gives the facade a warmth and texture that changes with the angle of the sun. Early morning and late afternoon light draw out different tones from the surface.

The museum hosts a rotating programme of exhibitions, from major curated shows to open public submissions that have been a Tokyo institution for generations. Recent renovations have modernised the ventilation and accessibility infrastructure, allowing the building to continue serving audiences half a century after its opening.

## Summary

Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum was completed in 1975, designed by Kunio Maekawa Architects. Its brick-red tile exterior and largely subterranean galleries make it one of the more distinctive public buildings in Ueno Park. With a programme that runs throughout the year and an entrance that connects naturally to the park, it is a place worth entering on almost any visit to the area.
