## Natural Grass and Open Air at Hiroshima Station

Mazda Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima was completed in March 2009 and opened for play on April 10, 2009, replacing the Hiroshima Municipal Baseball Stadium that had served the Hiroshima Toyo Carp since 1957. The new venue stands at 2-3-1 Minami-kanie, Minami Ward, Hiroshima City, a site adjacent to JR Hiroshima Station, bringing the Carp to one of the most accessible locations in the city. Designed by Masuoka Architectural Office and built by Takenaka Corporation, the ballpark accommodates approximately 32,000 spectators in an open-air configuration with a natural grass playing field. Covered seating was kept to the lower decks, leaving the upper reaches of the stands exposed to the elements—a deliberate choice that connects spectators to Hiroshima's sky in a way that enclosed venues cannot replicate.

## A Ballpark That Redefined the Fan Experience

At a time when most major Japanese stadiums were domed or heavily covered, Mazda Stadium's commitment to natural grass and an open roof stood out. The infield and outfield dimensions were set to allow generous foul territory and varied sight angles, and the sloped seating bowl was engineered for unobstructed views from every section. Several seating areas—including spaces where fans could lie on a lawn-style terrace—expanded the range of ways visitors could engage with the game. The brick-and-steel exterior in warm tones references the city's postwar reconstruction aesthetic. Its placement steps from Hiroshima Station integrated it into the urban fabric rather than isolating it on a suburban site, and surrounding commercial activity grew alongside the venue in the years that followed.

## Summary

Opened in 2009 with a capacity of approximately 32,000, Mazda Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima brought natural grass and an open-air atmosphere back to the heart of the city. Walking out of Hiroshima Station on a game day, surrounded by red Carp jerseys converging on the gates, is one of the more vivid ways to feel this city's connection to its baseball team.
