## A 1,672-Meter Wing Over Osaka Bay

Five kilometers off the Osaka coast, an artificial island carries one of the world's longest passenger terminal buildings. The Kansai International Airport terminal stretches 1,672 meters end to end — a distance that would cover more than fifteen city blocks. The airport opened on September 4, 1994, as the world's first international airport built entirely on a man-made island. The lead architect was Renzo Piano, working jointly with Nikken Sekkei and four Japanese construction firms: Obayashi, Takenaka, Shimizu, and Ando.

The terminal building rises 4 floors above ground and 1 below, reaching a height of approximately 36 meters, with a total floor area of 303,943 square meters. Its most dramatic element is the stainless-steel roof, which runs the full length of the building in a gentle tube-like arc, like a vast wing laid flat over the concourse. The ceiling surface is formed by more than 4,000 individual stainless-steel panels, each geometrically precise. Natural light enters from both ends of the building, keeping the long concourse from feeling enclosed.

## Sinking Ground, Rising Engineering

Building an airport on a freshly reclaimed island presented a structural problem no project had faced at this scale: the soft seabed of Osaka Bay would settle unevenly under the weight of millions of tons of landfill and concrete, causing the ground — and the building on top of it — to tilt and warp over time.

The design team's answer was a foundation of more than 900 steel piles combined with individual hydraulic jacks built into each column base. Each jack can be adjusted independently, allowing the building's floor levels to be corrected as the island settles. Since opening, the island has sunk by more than ten meters in places — far beyond initial projections — but the terminal has remained level thanks to this system. The scale of the anti-settlement engineering, applied continuously for three decades, stands as one of the most ambitious structural achievements in modern airport construction.

## Summary

The Kansai International Airport terminal opened in September 1994: 1,672 meters long, 303,943 square meters in floor area, and approximately 36 meters tall at its highest point. Renzo Piano's flowing stainless-steel roof and the hydraulic settlement-correction system beneath the floor both represent engineering ambition that went well beyond what any previous airport had attempted. The terminal continues to serve as the main international gateway to the Kansai region, three decades after the island it stands on was pulled up from the sea.
