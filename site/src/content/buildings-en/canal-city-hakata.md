## A City Within the City

Exit Hakata Station and walk ten minutes south, and Canal City Hakata announces itself by sound before sight: the rush of water, music echoing across an open atrium. The complex opened in 1996 on a site close to the boundary between Hakata and Fukuoka's downtown Tenjin district, covering a total floor area of approximately 234,000 square metres. At its heart is a roughly 180-metre-long artificial canal, flanked by curving buildings painted in bold colours, their tiers of balconies looking down onto the water below.

The design came from The Jerde Partnership, a Los Angeles firm whose work on projects such as Horton Plaza in San Diego and Universal CityWalk had established a template for what became known as "experiential retail" — commercial spaces designed less as containers for shops and more as destinations in themselves. Canal City applied that approach to the specifics of Fukuoka: the canal becomes a communal space, a stage for fountain shows and outdoor performances that give visitors a reason to stay, wander, and return.

## What the Complex Contains

Beyond the canal and the commercial floors, Canal City Hakata holds a multiplex cinema (United Cinemas Canal City 13), a live theatre (Canal City Theatre), two hotels (Grand Hyatt Fukuoka and ANA Crowne Plaza Fukuoka), and office towers. The programme reflects an ambition to bring the functions of an entire urban neighbourhood into a single complex — shopping, eating, staying, working, performing, watching — and to do so in a central location rather than a suburban fringe.

The walkability of Canal City's position between Hakata Station and Tenjin has sustained high visitor numbers across its three decades of operation. Fountain shows remain a regular evening attraction. A second phase (Canal City East Building) was added in 2012, extending the complex further along the canal. The project's scale and the theatricality of its public spaces have made it one of the most visited destinations in Kyushu.

## Summary

Canal City Hakata opened in 1996 as a mixed-use complex of approximately 234,000 m² in central Fukuoka City. Its artificial canal, flanked by The Jerde Partnership's curving, colourful buildings, was one of the earliest examples of large-scale experiential retail design in Japan. Shops, a cinema, theatres, two hotels and offices make it a self-contained urban district that has remained a focal point of Fukuoka tourism for nearly three decades.
