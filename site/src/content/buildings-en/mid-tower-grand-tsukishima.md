## A Tower Rising from Tsukishima's Backstreets

In October 2020, a 32-story residential tower was completed in the Nishinaka-dori district of Tsukishima, Chuo Ward, Tokyo. Mid Tower Grand stands 121.00 metres (maximum height 122.15 m) and was born from the Tsukishima 1-chome Nishinaka-dori Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project — a formal mechanism for replacing ageing, densely packed urban blocks.

Tsukishima is a reclaimed island in Tokyo Bay known across Japan for its *monjayaki* (Tokyo-style savoury pancake) restaurants along the Nishinaka-dori shopping street. While the adjacent waterfront areas of Kachidoki and Harumi were being transformed by landmark towers like [Deux Tours Kachidoki](/en/buildings/deux-tours-kachidoki/) and [The Tokyo Towers](/en/buildings/the-tokyo-towers/), Tsukishima retained much of its low-rise shitamachi character. The construction of Mid Tower Grand marks one chapter in the gradual transformation of that character.

## Three Developers, One Redevelopment Framework

The Type 1 Urban Redevelopment scheme involves compulsory reorganisation of land rights in designated blocks, replacing a mix of low-rise wooden buildings with fire-resistant, code-compliant structures. Mitsui Fudosan Residential, Marubeni, and Taisei Corporation served as joint sellers; Sato Sogo Keikaku and Taisei Corporation handled design; and Taisei Corporation carried out construction — an arrangement in which a major general contractor handled design and build together.

The 503-unit tower is conspicuous among Tsukishima's low-rise surroundings, its 32 stories visible from the Nishinaka-dori restaurant lane and acting as a new spatial reference point in the neighbourhood. The building completes a year (2020) that also saw [Grand City Tower Tsukishima](/en/buildings/grand-city-tower-tsukishima/) under planning nearby, underlining how much redevelopment pressure the island carries.

## Summary

Mid Tower Grand was completed in October 2020 with 32 floors above ground (one basement level), a height of 121 metres, and 503 residential units. It emerged from a Type 1 Urban Redevelopment project in Tsukishima 1-chome and was developed jointly by Mitsui Fudosan Residential, Marubeni, and Taisei Corporation.

Walking west from Tsukishima Station through the Nishinaka-dori monjayaki district, you can look up and find the tower framing the end of the street — a reminder that Tokyo's renewal reaches even its most characterful neighbourhoods.
