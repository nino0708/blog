## Japan's First Intercity Highway

The Meishin Expressway runs from Komaki Interchange (IC) in Aichi Prefecture to Nishinomiya IC in Hyogo Prefecture. A section between Ritto and Amagasaki opened on July 16, 1963, marking Japan's first stretch of intercity expressway. Full operation between Komaki and Nishinomiya was achieved on July 1, 1965. The Japan Highway Public Corporation undertook the construction, applying lessons drawn from studies of West Germany's Autobahn network, which at the time represented the most advanced highway engineering in the world. The road was equipped with a full-length center divider separating opposing lanes — a standard that was novel on Japanese roads at the time.

Traffic demand from the opening day exceeded initial projections. The expressway cuts through Aichi, Gifu, Shiga, Kyoto, Osaka, and Hyogo prefectures, following the historic Tokaido and Tozan corridors. By connecting the manufacturing heartlands of the Tokai region — Nagoya and its surrounding industrial belt — with the port city of Kobe and the commercial center of Osaka, the expressway compressed travel times and delivery schedules in ways that fueled the country's high-growth economy.

## Backbone of Japan's Postwar Recovery

Before the Meishin opened, the journey between the Tokai and Kansai regions by road was slow and unreliable. The expressway replaced that uncertainty with consistent travel times, particularly important for freight carriers serving the factories and warehouses along the corridor. In 1968, the opening of the Tomei Expressway linked the Meishin to Tokyo, completing Japan's first long-distance highway axis. This east-west connection — Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe — formed the structural backbone of Japan's postwar industrial geography, a geography that remains largely intact today.

The expressway is now jointly managed by NEXCO Central Japan (from Komaki to the Maibara area) and NEXCO West Japan (from there to Nishinomiya). The parallel Shin-Meishin Expressway, which branches off the Meishin at Maibara, offers additional capacity through the same corridor and reduces congestion during peak periods.

## Summary

The Meishin Expressway opened in stages, reaching its full alignment between Komaki IC and Nishinomiya IC in July 1965. Built by the Japan Highway Public Corporation, it was Japan's first full intercity expressway and a technological milestone influenced by European highway design. Its opening accelerated postwar Japan's shift toward road-based commerce and travel, and its subsequent connection with the Tomei Expressway created the east-west highway spine the country still depends on. Driving or riding its full length is a journey through the landscape of Japan's modern economy.
