## The Road That Started It All

The Meishin Expressway (Route E1) is a 190-kilometer toll expressway that links Komaki Junction (JCT) in Aichi Prefecture to Nishinomiya Interchange (IC) in Hyogo Prefecture. It was Japan's first true long-distance expressway, with the initial section between Ritto and Amagasaki opening on July 16, 1963, and the full route completing on October 26, 1965. Construction was carried out by the Japan Highway Public Corporation, the predecessor of the current operators NEXCO Central Japan (covering the Aichi, Gifu, and Shiga sections) and NEXCO West Japan (covering Kyoto, Osaka, and Hyogo).

Before the Meishin opened, motor traffic between Nagoya and Osaka had to navigate National Route 1 — the modern successor to the old Tokaido — through a succession of towns and traffic signals. The expressway cut travel times dramatically and changed the economics of freight movement between the Chukyo and Hanshin industrial zones, two of Japan's most productive manufacturing regions. The route handles hundreds of millions of vehicle passages each year and remains one of the country's most heavily trafficked corridors.

## Backbone of the Tokaido Corridor

The Meishin connects at its eastern end with the Tomei Expressway near Nagoya, forming a continuous expressway axis from Tokyo all the way to Kobe that is often simply called the "Tomei-Meishin." The Tomei completed its full length in 1969, four years after the Meishin, making the older road the founding segment of Japan's national expressway network.

In recent decades the parallel Shin-Meishin Expressway (Route E25) has been built along much of the same corridor to relieve congestion on the original route, particularly around the Tennozan Tunnel bottleneck near Kyoto. The two routes now work in tandem, together providing a highly redundant and resilient connection across the heartland of Japan's manufacturing economy.

## Summary

Completed in 1965, the Meishin Expressway spans 190 kilometers from Aichi to Hyogo and stands as the founding road of Japan's expressway network. Its historical significance as the first long-distance toll expressway is matched by its continued role as a vital artery linking the Chukyo and Hanshin metropolitan areas. Driving along its Shiga Prefecture section, with views of Lake Biwa on the horizon, offers a sense of traveling along one of the most historically important roads in modern Japan.
