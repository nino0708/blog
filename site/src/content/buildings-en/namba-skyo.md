## A New Axis for Namba

In Namba 5-chome, Chuo Ward, Osaka, a 31-storey tower rose to 150 metres in September 2018, taking the place of the old Nankai Kaikan Building that had long anchored the north side of Nankai Namba Station. Named Namba Skyo, the building was designed by Obayashi Corporation and developed by Nankai Electric Railway — the same company whose trains arrive directly beneath the structure. The total floor area is approximately 84,000 square metres.

The predecessor building, Nankai Kaikan, had opened in 1932 and served for decades as a cultural venue and social gathering point at the heart of Namba. As the building aged, Nankai Electric Railway began planning its replacement, eventually announcing the name "Namba Skyo" — blending Namba and the English word "sky" — in November 2017.

## Stacking Uses Above a Terminal Station

What sets Namba Skyo apart from a straightforward office development is its tight integration with Nankai Namba Station below. Offices, retail, a conference hall, and medical facilities are stacked vertically above the concourse level, making it one of the cleaner examples of station-integrated mixed-use redevelopment in Osaka.

At 150 metres, the tower stands well above its immediate surroundings, framing views over the Takashimaya shopping complex, the Sennichimae entertainment district, and the broader grid of Namba. The decision by a railway operator to invest in a high-rise on its own terminal land reflects a pattern visible across major Japanese urban railways, where station-adjacent redevelopment has become a primary strategy for diversifying revenue beyond the tracks.

## Summary

Namba Skyo was completed in September 2018, reaching 31 storeys and 150 metres above Nankai Namba Station. Designed by Obayashi Corporation and developed by Nankai Electric Railway, it replaced the Nankai Kaikan Building with a mixed-use tower that added a significant block of office floor space to the historically commercial Namba district.
