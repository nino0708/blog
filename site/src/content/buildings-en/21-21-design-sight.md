## Buried in the Lawn of Midtown Garden

21_21 DESIGN SIGHT opened on March 30, 2007, in a corner of Tokyo Midtown Garden in Roppongi. Designed by Tadao Ando Architect & Associates in collaboration with Nikken Sekkei, the building has two stories above ground and one below, with a total floor area of 1,932 square meters. Ando's signature board-formed concrete walls and a steeply folded, horizontal roof settle so low into the garden's lawn that from the outside, the building reads almost as a topographical feature—only the sharp crease of the roof gives it away.

The museum was founded by fashion designer Issey Miyake, product designer Naoto Fukasawa, and graphic designer Taku Satoh. Its mandate: to examine everyday design through thematic exhibitions, without a permanent collection.

## A Vertex of the Roppongi Art Triangle

The term "Roppongi Art Triangle" describes the three cultural venues clustered in this neighborhood: 21_21 DESIGN SIGHT, Mori Art Museum, and the National Art Center, Tokyo. Within that triangle, 21_21 holds the design angle—its exhibitions consistently ask visitors to reconsider how ordinary objects, materials, or systems are made and used.

Descending into the building's underground gallery, the folded roof becomes a ceiling that channels daylight through narrow skylights. The space feels larger than its footprint suggests. Exhibitions here are tightly themed and tightly curated; the small scale keeps the density of ideas high.

## Summary

21_21 DESIGN SIGHT, completed in 2007 at two stories above ground with a total floor area of 1,932 square meters, is one of Tokyo's most architecturally particular small museums. Tadao Ando's low concrete form dissolves into Midtown Garden's lawn, its interior lit by slivers of sky. Visit for the architecture, stay for whichever exhibition is running—and walk the garden to see the roof's folded geometry from every angle.
