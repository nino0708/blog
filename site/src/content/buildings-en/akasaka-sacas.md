## A Broadcaster's Neighbourhood in the Heart of Akasaka

Akasaka Sacas sits directly above Akasaka Station on the Tokyo Metro Chiyoda Line in Minato City. Its name combines "Akasaka" with the Japanese verb *sakasu* — "to make bloom" — and the complex opened on 20 March 2008. At its centre stands the Akasaka Biz Tower: 39 above-ground floors, 3 basement floors, and a height of 179 metres. Designed by Kume Sekkei and built by a joint venture led by Obayashi Corporation and Kajima Construction, the tower was completed in January 2008.

The project originated as TBS Holdings' "TBS Akasaka 5-chome Development Plan," a scheme to replace ageing studio and office buildings with an integrated precinct combining broadcasting, theatre, retail, and housing. Construction began in February 2005, and the complex took its current form in roughly three years.

## Multiple Functions Around a Single Plaza

Akasaka Sacas is not a single tower but a group of buildings arranged around a central open space. The TBS Broadcasting Center (20 above-ground floors) continues as one of Tokyo's main television production and news bases. Adjacent to it, the TBS Akasaka Act Theater — the Akasaka Creative Theater — seats 1,324 across two levels and hosts musicals, stage plays, and comedy events throughout the year. The Akasaka The Residence (21 floors, 133 rental units) adds a residential component to the mix, so that work, performance, and daily life coexist within the same precinct.

The roughly 1,200-square-metre Sacas Plaza ties the buildings together. Outdoor events and programming tie-ins take place there regularly, letting the broadcast world spill into street-level life. The station-connected location feeds office workers through at midday and theatre-goers in the evening, so the same space reads very differently at different hours.

## Summary

Akasaka Sacas opened in March 2008 as a cultural and mixed-use district anchored by TBS's headquarters in Minato City. The Akasaka Biz Tower (39F, 179m, designed by Kume Sekkei) defines the skyline, while the theatre, plaza, and residential building around it show what a broadcaster's approach to city-making looks like.
