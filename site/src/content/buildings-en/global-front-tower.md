## A Pentagon on the Waterfront

Walk east from Tamachi Station along the canal and a tower with an unfamiliar outline comes into view. Global Front Tower, completed in January 2016, rises 34 storeys to approximately 120 metres in Shibaura, Minato City. What makes it stand out in a neighbourhood already accustomed to tall buildings is its floor plan: a pentagon rather than the rectangle or circle typical of high-rise residential towers.

The five-sided geometry is not merely cosmetic. Each face of the building meets the others at a slightly different angle, which means corner units and even mid-floor units receive a more varied relationship with the canal, Tokyo Bay, and the city skyline. The building's 883 units benefit from that variety, and the exterior gains an angular rhythm that makes the tower identifiable from a distance.

## Shibaura's Long Transformation

The Shibaura district, on the bay-facing side of Tamachi Station, spent much of the 20th century as an industrial zone. Warehouses and factories gave way to redevelopment from the 1990s onward, and the neighbourhood has since accumulated a mix of office towers, residential blocks, and canal-side promenades. Global Front Tower occupies a site in that evolving fabric, alongside msb Tamachi to the north and Shibaura Island to the south.

The pentagonal profile adds an architectural note to a streetscape that might otherwise consist of generic rectilinear towers. From the canal bank, the building reads as a quietly distinctive presence — evidence that even a standard residential programme can yield a singular form when the geometry is handled with intention.

## Summary

Global Front Tower was completed in January 2016 as a 34-storey, approximately 120-metre residential tower with 883 units in Shibaura, Minato City. Its pentagonal floor plan sets it apart visually and spatially from nearby towers, capturing the ongoing bay-side residential evolution between Tamachi and the waterfront. A walk along the Shibaura canal reveals the building's full silhouette and the neighbourhood's layered history of industrial and residential change.
