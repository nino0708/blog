## A Geometric Landmark on Aoyama-dori

Standing along Aoyama-dori in Minami-Aoyama, Minato City, Spiral opened in October 1985 as a mixed-use cultural facility developed by Wacoal, the Kyoto-based fashion company. Its architect, Fumihiko Maki, later received the Pritzker Prize — and Spiral is widely regarded as one of his defining works. The facade combines circular and rectilinear elements around a central atrium, presenting a composition of intersecting straight lines and curves that immediately distinguishes the building from its neighbors on Aoyama-dori. It became a celebrated example of Postmodern architecture in Japan and was featured repeatedly in international architectural journals throughout the 1980s and beyond.

## A Building That Opens to the Street

What makes Spiral distinct is its permeability: from the street, the interior life of the building is visible, inviting passersby to explore. Inside, the composition echoes a nautilus shell, with sightlines and circulation flowing naturally from the ground floor upward through the gallery and event spaces. The gallery hosts exhibitions that cross between contemporary art, design, and fashion — a fitting program for Minami-Aoyama, one of Tokyo's most design-conscious neighborhoods. The multi-purpose Spiral Hall below the gallery accommodates concerts, theatrical performances, and corporate presentations. Even the plaza in front of the main entrance is activated for events, extending the building's social role onto the street itself.

Pairing a visit with [Omotesando Hills](/buildings/omotesando-hills/) nearby offers a vivid contrast between two generations of Aoyama architecture — Maki's layered geometry from 1985 versus Tadao Ando's trench-cut complex from 2006.

## Summary

Spiral was completed in 1985 to the design of Fumihiko Maki, bringing a gallery, event hall, and restaurant together under one geometrically inventive roof in Minami-Aoyama. The building remains a touchstone of Japanese Postmodern architecture and a lively cultural hub four decades after it opened. When walking Aoyama-dori, let the patterned facade draw you in — it rewards a closer look.
