## A Nine-Story Electronics Complex at the Gateway to Akihabara

On September 16, 2005, a nine-story, six-basement-level building sixty metres tall opened steps from Akihabara Station's east exit. Named Yodobashi Camera Multimedia Akiba, it covers a total floor area of roughly 61,400 m² — the chain's largest store at the time — with electronics, cameras, PCs, food, and restaurants stacked across its floors. The investment in facilities was reported at around 12 to 13 billion yen. Its opening reshaped the visual character of Akihabara overnight.

Akihabara has reinvented itself many times: postwar black-market stalls gave way to radio parts, then consumer electronics, then personal computers and anime goods. In the 2000s, the decommissioned Akihabara freight-yard site was redeveloped into complexes such as Akihabara UDX. Yodobashi Akiba emerged from the same wave of urban renewal, built on land east of Akihabara Station that had been held by the former Japan Railway Settlement Corporation. Its position at a stop shared by JR lines and two Tokyo Metro lines makes it accessible from across the metropolitan area.

## Six Basement Floors and a Vertical Retail Stack

What sets this building apart is its structure. Despite standing only nine floors above ground, it channels six basement levels mainly into parking — roughly 400 spaces — while stacking each of the above-ground floors with a different retail category: cameras, computers and peripherals, audio and instruments, smartphones, games and toys, topped by a restaurant floor. Rather than mixing tenants across a horizontal floor plate, the building assigns an entire level to each category, creating a vertical retail tower where the anchor tenant occupies everything.

## Summary

Nine floors above ground, six below, 60 m tall, and roughly 61,400 m² in total — Yodobashi Camera Multimedia Akiba opened in September 2005 as a vertical urban retail complex directly adjacent to Akihabara Station. Standing at Kandahanaokacho 1-1 in Chiyoda, the building remains one of the defining landmarks of the electronics district, a modern anchor for a neighbourhood that has never stopped reinventing itself.
