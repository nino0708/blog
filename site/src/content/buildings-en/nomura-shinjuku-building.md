## A Pioneer of Nishi-Shinjuku

On the site of the former Yodobashi Water Purification Plant in Nishi-Shinjuku, west of Shinjuku Station, the Shinjuku Nomura Building was completed in June 1978. The tower reaches approximately 210 metres across 50 storeys above ground and four below, designed by Yasui Architects & Engineers and built by Kajima Corporation. It was developed to serve as the head office of Nomura Real Estate and Nomura Real Estate Holdings, and is widely known as simply the Nomura Building in the neighbourhood.

The 1970s saw a concentration of high-rise construction in Nishi-Shinjuku as the sub-centre district took shape on reclaimed land cleared for urban renewal. The Nomura Building was part of that wave, joining the Keio Plaza Hotel, Shinjuku Mitsui Building and others in establishing the distinctive skyline that now defines western Shinjuku. At the time of its completion, 50 storeys was a remarkable scale for Japan, and the approximately 210-metre tower captured the ambition of a period of rapid economic growth.

## Nearly Five Decades on the Skyline

The Shinjuku Nomura Building has remained a prominent feature of the Nishi-Shinjuku skyline for close to fifty years. Its height places it comfortably among the mid-tier of Shinjuku's high-rise cluster even today, and it has not retreated into the background as newer, taller towers have risen around it. The lower floors of the building contain restaurants and retail tenants that serve the daily needs of the surrounding office district.

The building is a short walk from Shinjuku Station's West Exit, well connected to the underground passageways and bus terminal that tie together the sub-centre's pedestrian network. Its long-standing role as a corporate headquarters gives it a continuity of occupancy that many speculative office towers in the area do not share.

## Summary

The Shinjuku Nomura Building was completed in June 1978: 50 storeys, approximately 210 metres tall, anchoring the Nomura Real Estate group's presence in Nishi-Shinjuku for nearly half a century. It stands as one of the founding towers of a sub-centre development that began in earnest in the 1970s and continues to evolve today.
