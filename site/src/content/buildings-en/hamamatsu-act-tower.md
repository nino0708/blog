## Shizuoka's Tallest Tower

Hamamatsu Act Tower, completed in 1994 in Itayacho, Chuo Ward, Hamamatsu City, is the tallest building in Shizuoka Prefecture at 213 metres and 45 above-ground floors. With four basement levels, the tower was designed by Kume Sekkei as part of the Hamamatsu Station North Exit Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project, integrating offices, a hotel, and commercial facilities into a single high-rise structure directly connected to JR Hamamatsu Station. On the flat expanse of the Enshu Plain, a 213-metre tower reads as an unmistakable landmark from considerable distance — visible even briefly from Tokaido Shinkansen trains passing through the city.

The choice to concentrate the functions of a modern urban core into a single mixed-use supertall rather than distribute them across a spread of mid-rise buildings was deliberate. The result was a tower that immediately became the defining element of Hamamatsu's skyline.

## Manufacturing City, Urban Ambition

Hamamatsu is best known as the manufacturing heartland of Japan's musical instrument and motorcycle industries, with Yamaha and Suzuki headquartered in the city. The Act Tower's completion in 1994 added a different dimension: a demonstration that this mid-sized industrial city could build and sustain a major urban complex at a scale commensurate with Japan's larger metropolitan centres.

The tower sits at the geographic midpoint between Tokyo, Nagoya, and Osaka — Hamamatsu's longstanding advantage as a transit city. That centrality has helped the Act Tower's hotel and office components maintain relevance across three decades, with the complex continuing to serve as the city's principal venue for conferences and large-scale events. The Act City Hamamatsu complex surrounding the tower extends this function further, combining concert halls and event spaces in a single integrated precinct.

## Summary

Completed in 1994, 45 floors above ground with four basement levels and a height of 213 metres, Hamamatsu Act Tower was designed by Kume Sekkei as the centrepiece of Hamamatsu's station-area redevelopment. As Shizuoka Prefecture's tallest structure, it rises unmistakably above the Enshu Plain — a moment on the Tokaido Shinkansen where this mid-sized industrial city announces itself in a single vertical stroke.
