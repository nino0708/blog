## A Mixed-Use Tower That Set the Bar in Hiroshima

Just a one-minute walk from the Shukkeien-mae tram stop in Hiroshima's central Naka Ward, Urban View Grand Tower has anchored the Kamihatchobori neighborhood since its completion in 2004. At 43 stories and 166 meters, it was the tallest building in Hiroshima Prefecture when it opened, and its profile — a slim tower rising from a wider commercial podium — became a recognizable feature of the cityscape north of Hiroshima Castle. Urban Corporation developed the project, and construction was carried out by Penta-Ocean Construction.

The building's program stacks retail space on the lower floors, offices in the mid-section, and 295 privately owned condominiums on the upper floors. This vertical layering in a compact urban footprint anticipated the direction of central-area redevelopment that would follow in subsequent years.

## Kamihatchobori and the Hiroshima High-Rise Moment

Kamihatchobori has long been a district of government offices, financial institutions, and legal firms — a quieter counterpart to the busier commercial axis of Hatchobori and Kamiyacho. Urban View Grand Tower's arrival gave this area a visual anchor that announced Hiroshima's ambition to build tall, something that had previously been limited by the city's post-war scale of reconstruction.

For much of the 2000s, the tower held the record as Hiroshima's tallest structure, a distinction that passed only as the next generation of high-rises arrived in the 2010s and 2020s. The Hiroshima Prefectural government's guide to landmark buildings cites it alongside Motomachi Credo as one of the defining structures of the downtown core.

## Summary

Urban View Grand Tower was completed in 2004 with 43 floors above ground and 1 basement floor, reaching 166 meters and briefly holding the title of Hiroshima's tallest building. Its mixed-use formula — retail, offices, and condominiums stacked in one tower — established a model that shaped later developments in the city. When you stroll through Kamihatchobori on the way to Shukkeien Garden, look north along the streets and the tower's slender silhouette is there to guide your sense of the city.
