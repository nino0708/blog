## The Sumitomo stake in Shiodome

At 1-9-2 Higashi-Shimbashi, Minato City, the Shiodome Sumitomo Building rises 27 floors to 128.1 metres. Completed in 2004, it was jointly developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development and Sumitomo Life Insurance, with Nikken Sekkei handling the design. The building occupies the Sumitomo Group's assigned block within Shiodome Siosaite — a large-scale mixed-use district that rose on the site of the former Shiodome Freight Station after Japan National Railways was privatised in 1987.

The programme is mixed in a way typical of Shiodome's development: offices take the majority of floors, while hotel, retail, and food-and-beverage outlets fill the lower levels. This formula — anchor office floors over a hospitality and retail base — became the signature template for Shiodome's cluster of towers. Nearby stand the Shiodome Tower (Kajima, 2003), the Shiodome Media Tower (Kyodo News), and Jean Nouvel's Dentsu Building at 213 metres; their near-simultaneous completion in 2003–2004 gave the district its present density in barely two years.

## A district assembled block by block

Shiodome Siosaite divided its parcels among different developers, resulting in a patchwork of architectural identities beneath a broadly shared height profile. At 128.1 metres, the Sumitomo Building occupies the middle tier of Shiodome's skyline — smaller than the Dentsu Building, but consistently visible from the platforms of Hamamatsucho and Shimbashi stations.

At ground level, public plazas connect the block to the pedestrian network leading south toward Hamarikyu Garden — one of Edo's surviving tidal gardens and a rare expanse of greenery in this part of the city. The juxtaposition of dense twenty-first-century towers beside a seventeenth-century shogunal garden is Shiodome's defining visual tension. The Sumitomo Building participates in that contrast simply by standing in place, as every building in this district does.

## Summary

The Shiodome Sumitomo Building was completed in 2004: 27 floors, 128.1 metres. A product of Sumitomo Realty and Sumitomo Life Insurance working together, it typifies the mixed-use formula that shaped Shiodome. Walk south from Shimbashi Station, pass through the public plazas between towers, and look across to Hamarikyu Garden. Few parts of Tokyo make early-2000s ambition quite so tangible.
