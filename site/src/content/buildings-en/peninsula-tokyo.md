## Elegance at the Heart of Tokyo

The Peninsula Tokyo opened on 1 September 2007 at 1-8-1 Yurakucho, Chiyoda, rising 24 floors above ground with four basement levels. It is the first Japanese outpost of The Peninsula Hotels, a brand founded in Hong Kong in 1928 and long associated with understated luxury. The site previously housed the Nichigeki Theatre building and the Nikkatsu Hotel, properties that had defined the Hibiya–Yurakucho streetscape for generations. The Peninsula inherited that civic weight and settled quietly into one of the most storied corners of the Japanese capital.

The location is exceptional by any measure. To the north lies the Imperial Palace Outer Garden; to the southeast, the shopping streets of Ginza; to the west, Hibiya Park and the Imperial Theatre. Few addresses in Tokyo place a single building in view of all three at once.

## A Horizontal Presence in a Vertical City

Where many Tokyo hotels compete on height, The Peninsula Tokyo distinguishes itself through the quality of its streetscape relationship. A generous motor court along Yurakucho-dori allows guests to arrive unhurried, while the limestone-toned façade reads more like a refined civic building than a commercial tower. Each guestroom includes a private terrace, a feature rare in Tokyo's dense urban fabric, framing views of the park or the cityscape depending on orientation.

At 24 floors the building sits below the skyline of neighboring developments such as Tokyo Midtown Hibiya. Yet within the human-scale context of Yurakucho — a district still scaled to the 1950s entertainment quarter that once occupied it — The Peninsula reads as the anchor it was intended to be.

## Summary

The Peninsula Tokyo, opened in 2007, is a 24-floor luxury hotel occupying one of central Tokyo's most geographically privileged addresses. Nearly two decades in, the Hong Kong-born brand has become a fixture of the Hibiya streetscape, a point of reference between the park, the palace, and the Ginza. If you are walking the district, a pause at the Yurakucho intersection to take in the façade costs nothing and rewards the eye.
