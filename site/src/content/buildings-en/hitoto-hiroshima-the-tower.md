## A New Landmark on a Historic Campus Site

In 2020, a 53-story, 178-meter residential tower rose from a site in Hiroshima's Naka Ward that had for decades been home to the main campus of Hiroshima University. hitoto Hiroshima The Tower, developed jointly by five companies — Mitsubishi Jisho Residence, Mitsui Fudosan Residential, JR West Properties, Totate Urban Development, and Hiroshima Electric Railway — became the tallest building in the combined Chugoku, Shikoku, and Kyushu regions upon completion. Its 665 units occupy a seismic-isolation structure designed by Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei and built by Maeda Corporation, standing at 1-1-66 Higashi-Senda-cho, Naka Ward.

The five-company consortium reflected both the scale of the project and the importance of rooting it in local partners: JR West Properties connected the site to the city's rail network, while Hiroshima Electric Railway brought tram-network expertise to the development mix.

## A Campus Reborn as Urban High-Rise

Hiroshima University relocated its main campus to Higashi-Hiroshima in 1991, leaving a large central Hiroshima parcel without a clear purpose for years. The Senda district had long been associated with learning and cultural life; the university's presence shaped the quiet, residential character of the streets around it. When the redevelopment plans emerged, questions arose about how to honor that history while meeting contemporary housing needs.

hitoto Hiroshima The Tower's response was a tower surrounded by open ground-level space and greenery, allowing the public realm around the building to remain accessible and park-like. At 178 meters — roughly 6.5 times the height of Hiroshima Castle's keep — the tower transformed the visual scale of the Senda neighborhood permanently. Hiroshima Electric Railway trams pass near the site, and from the tram window, the 53-story building briefly fills the skyline in a way that makes clear just how thoroughly the former campus has been reinvented.

## Summary

hitoto Hiroshima The Tower was completed in 2020 with 53 floors above ground and 1 below, reaching 178 m as the tallest residential building across Chugoku, Shikoku, and Kyushu at the time of completion. Five companies co-developed the 665-unit seismic-isolation tower on the former Hiroshima University campus site. Walk through Senda-cho and you will feel the layered history of this place — a district shaped by learning that has now traded its academic skyline for one of Japan's most distinctive residential towers.
