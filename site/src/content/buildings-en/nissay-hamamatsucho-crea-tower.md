## A 29-story addition to Hamamatsucho's skyline

Hamamatsucho, Minato City, Tokyo. The departure point for the Tokyo Monorail to Haneda Airport, and a junction of the Oedo, Asakusa, and Tokaido Lines, this district has long functioned as one of Tokyo's established south-central office clusters. Anchored since the 1970s by the World Trade Center Building, it accumulated a dense concentration of office towers over the following decades.

The Nissay Hamamatsucho Crea Tower, completed in August 2018, added a 29-story, 156-meter presence to that skyline. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and built by Obayashi Corporation, it covers a total floor area of approximately 99,000 square meters. Typical floor plates measure roughly 790 tsubo — a generous, efficient layout suited to a range of tenants. A glass curtain wall of clean vertical lines gives the building a composed, contemporary face when viewed from Hamamatsucho Station.

## The broader wave of Hamamatsucho renewal

The block where Crea Tower stands belongs to a district where postwar office buildings completed in the 1960s and 1970s are gradually being replaced. The nearby World Trade Center Building — itself completed in 1970 — has entered a replacement process, and Crea Tower is one of the earlier completions in this longer generational wave of renewal.

The 156-meter height surpasses most of the surrounding vintage stock, quietly marking how the area's skyline has shifted between the original build-out era and today. Hamamatsucho saw high-rise office development earlier than most of Tokyo, which means its renewal cycle has also arrived sooner — and Crea Tower is an early data point in what will be a decades-long transformation.

## Summary

The Nissay Hamamatsucho Crea Tower was completed in 2018, with 29 floors above ground and a height of 156 meters. The combination of Nikken Sekkei's design and Obayashi Corporation's construction on this well-connected transit node captures the ongoing generational turnover of the Hamamatsucho office district. When passing through the area, look north from the station — the tower is a legible record of how Tokyo's established office zones continue to renew themselves, one building at a time.
