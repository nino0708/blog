## A New Kind of Library in Kanazawa

The Ishikawa Prefectural Library opened on 16 July 2022 in the Kodatsuno district of Kanazawa City, on the former site of Kanazawa University's Faculty of Engineering. The building rises four floors above ground with one basement level, covering a total floor area of 22,720 m². It was designed by Kankyo Design Institute, led by architect Mitsuru Senda, and constructed by a joint venture led by Shimizu Corporation.

## The Great Hall and Its Architecture

At the heart of the library is a space called the Great Hall — a sweeping, bowl-shaped reading floor where shelves of books surround visitors as they walk along gently sloping levels. The exterior wall alternates between solid panels and large glass windows, drawing natural light deep into the building while maintaining a calm environment for reading. This arrangement allows visitors to discover books by chance as they move through the space, making browsing itself part of the library experience. The spatial design encourages active exploration rather than simply retrieving a known title.

## A New Chapter for the Former University Campus

After Kanazawa University relocated to its Kakuma campus on the outskirts of the city, the Kodatsuno plateau was left awaiting redevelopment for years. In the same year the library opened, the adjacent site welcomed a new building for Kanazawa College of Art, completing a cluster of cultural and educational facilities in one place.

The library's open-access collection holds approximately 500,000 volumes, including regional records and administrative documents from Ishikawa Prefecture. Its location within walking distance of Kanazawa Castle Park and Kenrokuen makes it easy to combine a visit with the city's historic sights — a rare chance to pair centuries-old garden design with twenty-first-century library architecture.

## Summary

Completed in 2022 and spanning 22,720 m² across four floors above ground and one below, the Ishikawa Prefectural Library is a cultural landmark shaped by Mitsuru Senda's distinctive bowl-shaped Great Hall. Set in Kanazawa's education district, it offers a fresh reason to linger in a city already rich with history and architecture.
