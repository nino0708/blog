## Glass and Stone: A Tower Beside the Castle

Walk east from Osaka Castle and the skyline shifts dramatically. Beyond the park's ancient stone walls, a cluster of gleaming towers rises over the Shiromi and Jogai districts—Osaka Business Park, or OBP. The Crystal Tower, completed in 1990, stands 37 stories and 157 metres tall, its glass curtain wall catching the light in a way that makes it a fitting counterpart to the white-walled castle keep it faces. Few urban landscapes in Japan so vividly stage the conversation between centuries.

The tower includes two basement levels, bringing its total floor count to 39 storeys. Office floors are laid out for large-scale corporate tenants, with efficient floor plates suited to the demands of major firms. The building's location is served by JR Osaka-jokoen Station, which connects directly to the OBP complex, making it a commuter-friendly address despite being removed from the main Umeda business district.

## A New Business District Born from Castle Town History

OBP was conceived in the early 1980s as a bold urban renewal project. The land east of Osaka Castle had been occupied by industrial sites and factories; the city planned to convert it into a modern international business zone. Several towers rose over the following decade—Crystal Tower, Twin21, and the Matsushita IMP Building among them—each contributing a distinct architectural voice to what became a planned urban ensemble.

The juxtaposition of 17th-century castle and late-20th-century towers is no accident. Osaka's planners preserved the castle's visual authority while allowing the new district to develop its own identity. Viewed from the castle's observation deck, the towers of OBP spread out to the northeast; from the upper floors of Crystal Tower, the castle's tiled roof descends into a sea of park greenery below. This interplay of scales and eras defines the district's character.

## Summary

Crystal Tower was completed in 1990 with 37 above-ground floors and a height of 157 m. As one of the defining buildings of Osaka Business Park, it has spent more than three decades as a functional office tower while contributing to one of Japan's most memorable urban panoramas. Visitors to Osaka Castle who venture a few minutes further east will find this glass tower waiting—an invitation to compare two very different visions of what a city could be.
