## A Thousand Cranes, Built into the Walls

At 1-2-1 Otemachi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima — immediately east of the Atomic Bomb Dome — the Orizuru Tower opened in 2016. The project was not a new build but a full renovation of an existing fourteen-story office block by Hiroshima-born architect Sanbichi Hiroshi. Its name means "paper crane tower," and its defining feature — the Orizuru Wall — is immediately apparent from the street: a glass-enclosed display column rising approximately 50 meters through the interior, stacked with more than 1.27 million paper cranes received from donors around the world.

Paper cranes sent to Hiroshima as peace offerings are typically stored in the city's archive, largely out of public view. The Orizuru Wall turns the archive into architecture: each crane is sealed in a transparent capsule and the capsules are layered into a luminous vertical surface that runs from a lower floor toward the roofline. The concept — a visible storehouse of prayers — has attracted international attention both as a design gesture and as a way of giving donated cranes a second life as a permanent witness.

## An Open Roof Over Peace Memorial Park

The tower's rooftop observation space, called Hiroshima no Oka (Hiroshima Hill), is an open-air deck with no enclosing glass — visitors stand directly in the city's weather and air. From the deck, the Aioi Bridge, the Peace Memorial Park, and the Atomic Bomb Dome align in direct view below. Architect Sanbichi intended the unglazed opening as a way of connecting the viewer physically with Hiroshima's climate and landscape rather than observing it through a filtered barrier.

The renovation also made extensive use of domestic timber, for which the building received the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism Award at the Timber Use Excellent Facilities Contest in Reiwa 1 (2019). The combination of a concrete frame with timber and steel detailing gives the interior a warmth that distinguishes it from a conventional observation facility.

## Summary

Orizuru Tower (2016, 14 floors) is a renovated office building in central Hiroshima, redesigned by Sanbichi Hiroshi to house the Orizuru Wall — a 50-meter glass display of 1.27 million paper cranes — and an open-air observation deck overlooking the Peace Memorial Park and the Atomic Bomb Dome.
