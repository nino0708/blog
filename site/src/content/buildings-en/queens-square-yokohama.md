## Three Towers Stepping Down to the Bay

On the south side of the Yokohama Landmark Tower, three graduated towers rise from the Minato Mirai 21 waterfront district. Queens Square Yokohama opened on 18 July 1997 as a mixed-use complex anchored by three high-rise office towers — Queens Tower A, B and C. Tower A, the tallest, reaches 36 floors above ground (five below) and stands 171.8 metres. Tower B rises 28 floors to 138 metres; Tower C, 21 floors to 109 metres. The deliberate step in height across the three towers creates a visual cascade toward Tokyo Bay, a signature feature of the Minato Mirai skyline.

The Minato Mirai 21 district was built on land formerly occupied by the Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Yokohama Shipyard, as part of Yokohama City's large-scale waterfront redevelopment programme. [Yokohama Landmark Tower](/en/buildings/yokohama-landmark-tower/) was the centrepiece, and Queens Square Yokohama was planned as the complex immediately to its south — adding office, retail and cultural uses to the emerging urban district. The three towers share a connected low-rise podium containing shops, a convention hall and a cinema complex, creating one of the larger mixed-use assemblies on the Kanagawa waterfront.

## A Skyline Planned From the Start

The graduated silhouette of Queens Square Yokohama was not accidental: Minato Mirai's master plan assigned height bands to different zones, with the Landmark Tower at the peak and surrounding buildings stepping down incrementally toward the waterfront. Queens Square embodies this planning logic, its three towers forming a staircase profile that reinforces the district's hierarchical skyline rather than competing with it.

Direct connection to Minatomirai Station on the Minato Mirai Line — which links the district to Shibuya, Yokohama, and Motomachi-Chukagai — makes the complex an easy stop on any tour of the waterfront. For visitors arriving by rail, Queens Square is often the first major ensemble of towers they encounter as they emerge from the station, setting the scale for the regenerated harbour front.

## Summary

Queens Square Yokohama opened in July 1997 as a mixed-use complex at the heart of the Minato Mirai 21 planned district. Its three office towers — A at 171.8 m / 36F, B at 138 m / 28F, C at 109 m / 21F — step down toward the waterfront in a deliberate profile that reflects the master-planning logic of one of Japan's most coherent urban redevelopments. A direct link to Minatomirai Station makes the complex a natural starting point for exploring Yokohama's regenerated harbour front on foot.
