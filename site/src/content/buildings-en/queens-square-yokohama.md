## Three Towers That Complete Minato Mirai

Minato Mirai Station on the Minatomirai Line opens directly into Queen's Square Yokohama, a complex of three towers in the Minato Mirai 21 district of Yokohama's Nishi Ward. Construction started in February 1994, completion came in June 1997, and the complex opened to the public on 18 July 1997. It stands on Block 24 of Minato Mirai 21, alongside Yokohama Landmark Tower as one of the district's defining high-rise landmarks.

The tallest of the three is Queen's Tower A: 36 above-ground floors (plus a 2-floor crown structure) and a height of 171.80 metres. Nikken Sekkei handled the basic design, and JGC Holdings used the tower as its headquarters for many years. Tower B (28F, 137.8m) and Tower C (21F, 109.1m) complete the group. The developer was the T·R·Y90 consortium — Tokyu Corporation, Sumitomo Corporation, Mitsubishi Estate, JGC, and others — with Taisei, Kajima, and Tokyu Construction handling the build.

## A Complete City Block Under One Roof

What distinguishes Queen's Square from a straightforward office development is its range of uses. Queen's Mall, the commercial arcade running between the towers, connects directly to the station and stays dry regardless of weather. Yokohama Minato Mirai Hall, a concert hall integrated into the complex, hosts classical music and other performances and brings a cultural dimension to what would otherwise be a purely commercial precinct. Together the mall and the concert hall gave Minato Mirai 21 a depth of urban life that offices and retail alone could not provide.

Minato Mirai 21 has been developed under Yokohama City's leadership since the 1980s. Yokohama Landmark Tower opened in 1993; four years later Queen's Square added the layers of commerce, culture, and hospitality that rounded out the district's offering. Together the two developments gave Yokohama a new city centre that it had long sought on its reclaimed waterfront.

## Summary

Queen's Square Yokohama opened in 1997 with Queen's Tower A reaching 36 floors and 171.8 metres in the Minato Mirai district. Designed by Nikken Sekkei, the three-tower complex brings offices, a shopping mall, a hotel, and a concert hall under one connected precinct — all directly above a station. Arriving by train and stepping straight into the mall is as much a part of the experience as looking up at the towers from the waterfront promenade.
