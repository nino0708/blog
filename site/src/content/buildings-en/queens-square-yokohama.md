## The Waterfront Gets Its Second Anchor

Queens Square Yokohama opened in 1997 at 2-3 Minatomirai, Nishi, Yokohama, four years after the nearby Yokohama Landmark Tower set a new bar for the city's ambitions. The complex comprises three towers — Queens Tower A, B, and C — with Tower A reaching 36 floors above ground (five below) and a height of 171.8 metres, making it among the tallest structures in Kanagawa Prefecture. Total floor area across the development exceeds 496,000 square metres. Design was shared between Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei and Nikken Sekkei; development was led by the T·R·Y90 consortium anchored by Tokyu Corporation and Sumitomo Corporation.

The project was part of the Minato Mirai 21 master plan, a decades-long effort to replace the port's industrial infrastructure with a mixed-use urban district facing Yokohama Bay. Queens Square completed the district's commercial core, giving Landmark Tower a neighbour in scale and purpose.

## Concert Hall, Retail, and a Direct Rail Link

The complex is more than office tower and shopping mall. Spanning the lower floors of Towers A through C is the Queens Square Arena zone, anchored by Yokohama Minato Mirai Hall — a 2,020-seat concert hall that has hosted orchestras, pop concerts, and graduation ceremonies since opening. The hall's presence transformed Minato Mirai from a business address into a destination for cultural life.

The upper floors of Tower A originally housed a five-star hotel with harbour and Landmark Tower views. Retail levels connect directly to both Sakuragicho Station on the JR Negishi Line and Minatomirai Station on the Minato Mirai Line, making the complex a natural hub for anyone arriving by train. The underground concourse links seamlessly to Landmark Tower and the broader Minato Mirai underground network.

## Summary

Queens Square Yokohama, completed 1997, is a 36-floor, 171.8 m mixed-use complex at the heart of the Minato Mirai 21 district. Alongside Landmark Tower, it anchors the skyline that now defines Yokohama for visitors arriving from the sea or from Sakuragicho. The concert hall it houses has made Minato Mirai a cultural venue as well as a commercial one — a distinction that sets this development apart from a standard office tower.
