## The Queen Towers of Minato Mirai

Queen's Square Yokohama opened on 18 July 1997 in the Minato Mirai 21 district of Yokohama's Nishi Ward, directly adjacent to Yokohama Landmark Tower. The complex centres on three supertall office towers: Queens Tower A, the tallest at 36 floors above ground and five below, reaches a maximum height of 171.80 metres; Queens Tower B rises 28 floors to approximately 138 metres; and Queens Tower C stands 21 floors tall at approximately 109 metres. Together with the surrounding facilities, the complex covers a combined gross floor area of 496,385 square metres. Completion was in June 1997, with the design shared between Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei and Nikken Sekkei, and construction carried out by Taiseisetudan, Kajima Corporation and Tokyu Construction.

## A City within a City

The development consortium assembled for Queen's Square was exceptionally broad, led by the T.R.Y.90 joint venture — comprising Tokyu Corporation, Sumitomo Corporation and six other partners — alongside Mitsubishi Estate, the Urban Renaissance Agency and JGC Holdings. Alongside the three office towers, the programme encompasses the Minato Mirai Tokyu Square shopping mall, the 2,020-seat Yokohama Minato Mirai Concert Hall and the Yokohama Bay Hotel Tokyu (25 floors). Queens Tower A houses the headquarters of JGC Holdings across most of its floors, while Towers B and C accommodate a variety of tenants. All three towers are connected by a corridor bridge at the seventeenth floor. The "Queens" name derives from the Minato Mirai 21 Three Towers concept: Landmark (corporate headquarters), Queens (commerce) and Kings (the historic Kanagawa Prefectural Hall and Yokohama City Hall). In 2004, Minato Mirai Station on the Minatomirai Line opened with a direct underground connection to the complex's basement concourse.

## Summary

Queen's Square Yokohama was completed in 1997, with Queens Tower A reaching 171.80 metres across 36 floors — forming the commercial and cultural counterpart to the adjacent Landmark Tower at the heart of Minato Mirai 21. The complex remains one of Yokohama's most recognisable landmarks and an easy base for exploring the waterfront district. Walking the upper corridors that link all three towers, or pausing for a concert at the hall beneath, is a fine way to understand the scale of ambition that shaped this corner of Japan's second city.
