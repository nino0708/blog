## Osaka's Symbol Above the Shinsekai

In the lanes of Higashinari-cho, Naniwa Ward, Osaka, a 103-metre steel tower materialises without warning. Completed on 28 October 1956, the present Tsutenkaku is the second tower on this site — the first opened in 1912, modelled on the Eiffel Tower, and was dismantled in 1943 for wartime material supply. The structural design of the rebuilt tower was entrusted to Tachu Naito, the engineer who would later design Nagoya TV Tower and Tokyo Tower, earning him the nickname "Tower Doctor." Takenaka Corporation oversaw the design and construction; Okumura Gumi carried out the building work.

The first Tsutenkaku was born as the centrepiece of Shinsekai amusement park, developed on the site of the 1903 National Industrial Exhibition. Its hybrid form — an Eiffel-style lower structure topped by an observation deck — was a sensation in Meiji-era Osaka. Its 1943 demolition left the Shinsekai neighbourhood without its defining landmark for over a decade. Local residents and shopkeepers pooled resources to establish Tsutenkaku Kanko Co., Ltd. and fund the reconstruction, and by 1956 the steel tower had risen again.

## Seven Decades as a Downtown Cultural Property

Today's tower is a steel-framed structure, 103 metres to the crown and 108 metres to the tip of the lightning rod. The fifth-floor observation deck is home to the Billiken statue — a good-luck deity whose soles must be rubbed for wishes to come true. The current Billiken is the third, continuing a tradition that stretches back to the first tower.

In a district untouched by the supertall towers of Namba and Umeda, the 103-metre silhouette of Tsutenkaku dominates the skyline with unchallenged clarity. In 2003 the tower was registered as a national tangible cultural property, recognising its role as a post-war community achievement and a living document of Osaka's popular culture. Alongside [Abeno Harukas](/en/buildings/abeno-harukas), Tsutenkaku forms one of the defining vertical landmarks of southern Osaka.

## Summary

Tsutenkaku was completed in 1956, stands 103 metres tall (108 m with lightning rod), and is a steel-framed tower of 5 above-ground and 1 below-ground floors. Rebuilt by local initiative with structural engineering by Tachu Naito and construction by Okumura Gumi, it was designated a national tangible cultural property in 2003. The Shinsekai neighbourhood at its feet — thick with kushikatsu and doteyaki restaurants — makes for one of Osaka's most atmospheric strolls.
