## Kobe's Government Tower on the Waterfront

Completed in 1989, Kobe City Hall Building No. 1 rises 30 stories and 132 meters above Sannomiya, the commercial heart of Kobe's Chuo Ward. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and built by Takenaka Corporation, the tower serves as the city's main municipal office and stands just minutes from the main rail hub. Its white curtain-wall facade, defined by strong vertical fins, gives the building a quietly authoritative presence between the port and the Rokko mountain range.

On the 24th floor, the city maintains a free public observation lounge open to residents and visitors without reservation. On a clear day the panorama extends from Kobe Port in one direction to the Rokko Mountains in the other — a sweeping view that is unusual for a government building, and one of central Kobe's most accessible high-level vantage points.

## A Civic Anchor Through Earthquake and Reconstruction

The Great Hanshin Earthquake struck in January 1995, just years after the building opened. While much of Kobe suffered catastrophic damage, Building No. 1 sustained comparatively minor harm and immediately became the city's disaster response headquarters. Its continued operation through the crisis, and throughout the decade of reconstruction that followed, cemented its role as the institutional backbone of the city's recovery.

The adjacent Building No. 2, a lower structure, is now slated for redevelopment, with construction scheduled to begin in 2026 and complete in 2029. Through these changes, Building No. 1 remains Kobe's municipal anchor — its 30-story profile a fixed point in a skyline that continues to evolve.

## Summary

Built in 1989 to a design by Nikken Sekkei, Kobe City Hall Building No. 1 is a 30-story, 132-meter tower that has anchored civic life in Kobe for over three decades. The free observation lounge on the 24th floor offers an open invitation to take in the harbor and mountains above the city — rare for a government building, and well worth a stop on any walk through Sannomiya.
