## A New Presence on Osaka's Central Corridor

The UrbanNet Midosuji Building stands at 4-2-13 Awajimachi, Chuo Ward, Osaka — a 21-story, 2-basement office tower completed in January 2024. The building opened to tenants in June 2024. With a total floor area of approximately 42,425 square meters, it was developed by NTT Urban Development and delivered as a design-build project by Kajima Corporation.

Midosuji is Osaka's most prestigious commercial artery. The wide, ginkgo-lined boulevard runs north to south through the heart of the city, connecting the Umeda business district in the north to Namba in the south. Banks, corporate headquarters, and major retailers have occupied its flanks since the 1930s, earning the street comparisons to Paris's Champs-Élysées. The UrbanNet Midosuji Building extends this tradition of substantial commercial construction along one of Japan's most storied avenues.

## NTT Urban Development's Osaka Investment

NTT Urban Development is a Tokyo Stock Exchange–listed developer with office and mixed-use properties across Japan's major cities. Its investment in the Awajimachi address — deep in Osaka's central business district near Yodoyabashi Station — reflects continued confidence in the city's office market. Kajima Corporation managed the project as a design-build contractor, maintaining design quality and schedule control under a single contract.

At 42,425 square meters of total floor area, the building offers substantial multi-tenant office space at one of Osaka's most accessible addresses. The basement levels provide the supporting infrastructure — parking, utilities, storage — that high-specification office tenants require, while the above-ground floors maximize usable office area.

## Summary

The UrbanNet Midosuji Building is a 21-story office tower completed in January 2024 on Osaka's iconic Midosuji boulevard. Developed by NTT Urban Development and delivered by Kajima Corporation, it adds modern large-floor office space to one of Japan's most storied commercial streets. Walking Midosuji on foot — past its ginkgo trees and postwar facades — remains one of the best ways to absorb Osaka's commercial identity, and this building is a fresh addition to that familiar view.
