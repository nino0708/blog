## A Concrete High-Rise That Rewrote the Rules of Seismic Isolation

In 2011, a 29-story, 3-basement-level office tower was completed at Nishihommachi 1-4-1 in Osaka's Nishi-ku, directly connected to Hommachi Station on the Osaka Metro. The Orix Hommachi Building was designed and built as a single turnkey contract by Takenaka Corporation, with a total floor area of 41,555 m² on a compact 2,694 m² site.

What set this building apart in the construction world was its structure. While most high-rise office towers favour structural steel or steel-reinforced concrete frames, the Orix Hommachi Building uses a reinforced concrete (RC) frame combined with a seismic isolation system. The heavy RC mass is decoupled from earthquake motion by isolation bearings at the base, achieving both excellent seismic performance and cost efficiency — a combination rarely seen at this height. Takenaka Corporation's choice to use RC for a 29-story tower reflects a careful balancing of cost, structural performance, and construction schedule.

## Hommachi: The Business Core of Osaka

Hommachi sits at the crossroads of Midosuji — the north-south spine of Osaka's financial district — and Chuo-dori running east and west. The area has seen continuous office building renewal since the 1990s. Orix's decision to develop a large-scale rental office tower at a subway-connected site reflects a strategic bet on a neighbourhood with stable tenant demand. Stacking 29 floors onto a 2,694 m² footprint maximises the floor-area ratio while still delivering generous standard floor plates for corporate tenants.

## Summary

Twenty-nine stories above ground, three below, 41,555 m² in total — the Orix Hommachi Building completed in 2011 at Nishihommachi 1-4-1 is a rare example of RC high-rise construction with seismic isolation, designed and built by Takenaka Corporation. Directly linked to Hommachi Station in the heart of Osaka's business core, it stands as a disciplined and technically ambitious contribution to the city's office skyline.
