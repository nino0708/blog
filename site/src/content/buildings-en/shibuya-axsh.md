## A Tower Rises from Shibuya's Southern Slope

SHIBUYA AXSH is a 23-story, 3-basement mixed-use tower at 2-17-1 Shibuya, Shibuya City, Tokyo. Completed in May 2024 and opened on July 8, 2024, the building rises approximately 120 meters with a total floor area of roughly 44,500 square meters. Its lower floors house retail space while mid and upper floors are dedicated to offices. Design was handled by a joint venture of Tokyu Design Consultants, Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei, and Pacific Consultants; Takenaka Corporation served as the general contractor.

The Sakuragaoka district south of Shibuya Station had long resisted large-scale redevelopment. A topography divided by railway lines, combined with a dense cluster of wooden houses and small commercial buildings built on steep slopes, made coordinated redevelopment particularly challenging. The area felt disconnected from the bustling shopping and entertainment streets that define most of Shibuya. The urban redevelopment project finally unlocked that potential, and SHIBUYA AXSH stands as its centerpiece.

## The Name: Bringing People Together

"AXSH" is a phonetic spelling of "握手" (akushu), the Japanese word for a handshake. The name reflects the ambition to connect people, the neighborhood, and the wider city. Alongside the opening, pedestrian decks linking the building to Shibuya Station were completed, dramatically improving the flow of foot traffic through an area that once required navigating narrow alleys on steep inclines.

Just as SHIBUYA SCRAMBLE SQUARE and Shibuya Hikarie reshaped the eastern side of the station, SHIBUYA AXSH marks a new chapter for the southern approach. The redevelopment enhanced walkability across multiple levels, adding a modern civic gateway to a district that had quietly lagged behind the rest of Shibuya for decades.

## Summary

SHIBUYA AXSH is a 23-story, approximately 120-meter tower that opened in July 2024 at the southern exit of Shibuya Station. It completed the Sakuragaoka urban redevelopment, transforming a hillside of narrow lanes into a connected mixed-use destination. The handshake embedded in its name plays out daily as commuters, shoppers, and office workers cross its newly built public plazas. When you visit Shibuya, make sure to explore the reimagined southern slopes.
