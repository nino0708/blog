## The World's Longest Cable-Stayed Span

Tatara Bridge crosses the Tatara Strait between Ikuchijima Island (Onomichi City, Hiroshima Prefecture) and Ōmishima Island (Imabari City, Ehime Prefecture). It opened on May 1, 1999, with a main span of 890 meters and a total length of 1,480 meters — dimensions that set a world record for cable-stayed bridges at the time of completion. The bridge's clearance height of approximately 26 meters above the sea surface accommodates the vessel traffic that passes through the Seto Inland Sea beneath it. The Honshu-Shikoku Bridge Expressway Authority constructed the bridge as one of six spanning the Nishi-Seto Expressway (Shimanami Kaidō).

A cable-stayed bridge achieves its span by running diagonal cables from tall towers down to the deck on both sides. At Tatara Bridge, the cables spread from each tower in a fan-like pattern, creating a distinctive wing shape visible from the islands on either side. The towers rise to approximately 220 meters above the roadway — taller than many urban skyscrapers — and can be seen from considerable distances across the water. The design combines structural efficiency with an aesthetic quality that complements the quiet island scenery of the Seto Inland Sea.

## Islands, Cyclists, and a Record Bridge

Tatara Bridge occupies a central position on the Shimanami Kaidō, the cycling and driving route that links Onomichi with Imabari across six bridges. Cyclists cross it on a dedicated lane and can descend to Ōmishima — known for its citrus groves and the ancient Ōyamazumi Shrine — before continuing across the next bridge. On the Ōmishima side, a roadside park provides one of the best viewpoints for photographing the entire bridge structure against the sea and sky. The bridge also marks the prefectural boundary between Hiroshima and Ehime, connecting two regions that share both the Seto Inland Sea and the heritage of this monumental engineering project.

## Summary

Tatara Bridge opened in May 1999 with a cable-stayed main span of 890 meters and total length of 1,480 meters, holding the world record for cable-stayed spans at its completion. Built by the Honshu-Shikoku Bridge Expressway Authority as part of the Shimanami Kaidō, it bridges Ikuchijima and Ōmishima across the Seto Inland Sea. Drivers and cyclists crossing it experience both a feat of late-twentieth-century bridge engineering and one of the most scenic passages along any road in Japan.
