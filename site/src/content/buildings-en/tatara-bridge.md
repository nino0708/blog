## A World Record Span in the Seto Inland Sea

Tatara Bridge links Ōshima Island in Imabari City, Ehime Prefecture, with Ikuchi Island in Onomichi City, Hiroshima Prefecture. When it opened on May 1, 1999, its center span of 890 meters set a world record for cable-stayed bridges — a distinction it held until France's Millau Viaduct surpassed it in 2004. The two H-shaped pylons rise 220 meters above sea level, and the cable fans spreading from their tops give the bridge a distinctive harp-like silhouette visible across the surrounding archipelago.

The bridge's total length is 1,480 meters. Constructing its foundations required sinking massive caissons into the seabed of the Seto Inland Sea, a feat that demanded the most advanced engineering methods of the late 1990s. The project was overseen by the Honshu-Shikoku Bridge Authority, the same body responsible for the Akashi Kaikyō Bridge and the Seto Ōhashi Bridge.

## Gateway to the Cycling Paradise

Tatara Bridge sits at the midpoint of the Nishiseto Expressway — known informally as the Shimanami Kaidō — which is the only route among Japan's three Honshū–Shikoku connections to include a dedicated cycling and pedestrian path along its entire length. Cyclists stopping at the Tatara Shimanami Park beside the bridge's eastern tower sometimes hear a faint musical hum produced by wind passing through the cables, a natural resonance that locals call the "singing dragon."

The surrounding islands of Ōshima and Ikuchi are renowned for citrus cultivation and traditional shipbuilding. The bridge's opening shortened travel times dramatically and transformed economic life across the island chain. Today, Tatara Bridge is one of the most photographed landmarks on the Shimanami Kaidō cycling route, which draws visitors from across Japan and overseas.

## Summary

Tatara Bridge opened in 1999 as part of the Nishiseto Expressway, with a pylon height of 220 meters, a total length of 1,480 meters, and a main span of 890 meters that was the world's longest for a cable-stayed bridge at the time. It stands as a landmark achievement in Japanese bridge engineering and as the scenic centerpiece of one of Asia's most celebrated cycling routes. If you are planning a journey along the Shimanami Kaidō, the view from the Tatara Bridge cycling path — islands stretching to every horizon — is one of Japan's finest.
