## A Residential Tower That Reached for the Record Books

Cross the Arakawa River heading north from Tokyo and the urban fabric of Saitama Prefecture unfolds. Amid the low-rise streets of Kawaguchi's Motogo district, a white tower shoots upward with startling abruptness—Elza Tower 55. Completed in 1998 by developer Daikyo, the building stands 55 floors tall and 186 metres high, with one level below ground. At the time of its completion, no residential building in Japan stood taller. That record held until 2004.

Kawaguchi was historically a city of foundries—cast-iron manufacturers that supplied much of Tokyo's metalwork during the Meiji and Taisho eras. By the 1990s, the industrial landscape was giving way to residential development as Tokyo's commuter belt pushed northward. Elza Tower 55 was a product of that transition: a statement that suburbia could house not just houses and low-rise apartments, but a proper skyscraper capable of commanding a skyline. Construction was handled by Takenaka Corporation, whose engineers resolved the structural challenges of building at this height for residential use.

## Six Years at the Top of Japan's Residential Skyline

The tower's six-year tenure as Japan's tallest residence coincided with a rapid acceleration in high-rise apartment construction across the country. Earthquake-resistant and seismically isolated designs were maturing, making it economically viable to stack homes higher than ever before. Elza Tower 55 rode the leading edge of this wave—and in doing so, demonstrated that the demand for sky-high living existed well beyond the central wards of Tokyo.

From the upper floors, clear-day views take in a wide sweep from Tokyo Bay to the foothills of the Chichibu mountains; on exceptional days, the outline of Mount Fuji is visible to the southwest. This panorama became part of the building's identity, setting a precedent that subsequent tower mansions would be expected to match. The tower remains visible from the banks of the Arakawa River, a vertical punctuation mark in an otherwise horizontal landscape.

## Summary

Elza Tower 55 was completed in 1998 with 55 above-ground floors and a height of 186 m. As Japan's tallest residential building from 1998 to 2004, it played a formative role in establishing the tower-mansion typology now common across the country's major cities. A walk along the Arakawa embankment near Kawaguchi makes clear why this building became a symbol of the city—its slender silhouette rising above the rooftops is impossible to overlook.
