## A Cable-Stayed Bridge on the Sumida

Chuo Ohashi opened on 26 August 1993, connecting Tsukuda 1-chome and Tsukishima 1-chome across the Sumida River in Chuo Ward, Tokyo. The bridge spans 210.7 metres and is 25.0 metres wide. Its two A-shaped towers carry the deck by radiating cables, creating a symmetrical silhouette that reflects cleanly off the river surface. Notably, the bridge opened on the same day as the Rainbow Bridge over Tokyo Bay — 26 August 1993 stands as a double entry in the history of Tokyo's waterway crossings.

A bronze sculpture titled "Messenger" stands on the bridge deck. The work was a gift from the city of Paris to Tokyo, presented in recognition of the sister-river designation between the Seine and the Sumida. The sculpture brings an unexpected note of European atmosphere to this stretch of the river, and viewing it alongside the water below and the city skyline beyond forms a scene distinctive to Chuo Ohashi.

## Where Edo's Reclaimed Land Meets the Modern Skyline

The Tsukuda and Tsukishima neighbourhoods on either side of the bridge trace their origins to the Edo period, when fishermen from the Osaka region were invited to settle on newly reclaimed land in the river delta. Today high-rise residential towers stand alongside narrow lanes associated with monjayaki — the local style of savoury pancake — and street patterns that have changed little since the Edo era. This layering of periods is rare in central Tokyo.

Standing on Tsukishima and looking back across the bridge, the viewer takes in the modern office towers of Chuo Ward in one direction and the remaining low-rise streetscape in the other. That compressed contrast — high-rise and shitamachi, 21st century and Edo — makes Chuo Ohashi a favourite spot for photographers and those who walk Tokyo's rivers.

## Summary

Completed in August 1993, Chuo Ohashi crosses 210.7 metres of the Sumida River on two cable-stayed towers. Beyond its function as a city crossing, it carries a Paris-gifted sculpture and a view that holds centuries of Tokyo in a single glance. Walk the neighbouring [Kachidoki Bridge](/buildings/kachidoki-bridge/) or [Eitai Bridge](/buildings/eitaibashi/) and you have a half-day of riverside architecture along one of Japan's most storied waterways.
