## A New Administrative Core in Northern Kanazawa

The Ishikawa Prefectural Government Building stands in the Kuramatsuki district, a planned administrative and business zone located north-west of central Kanazawa. Rising 19 storeys to a height of 98.8 metres, the tower opened on 6 January 2003, marking the relocation of Ishikawa Prefecture's administrative functions from the former prefectural office near Kanazawa Castle Park.

The former building, a Renaissance Revival structure completed in 1924, has been preserved and repurposed as the Ishikawa Prefectural Government Memorial Shiinoki Guesthouse, a public cultural venue. The move to Kuramatsuki allowed the historic building to be restored while concentrating modern government functions in a single high-rise.

## The Top-Floor Observation Lounge

One of the building's defining features is a free observation lounge occupying the entire 19th floor. Open to residents and visitors without reservation, the lounge commands a wide panorama: Kanazawa's rooftops to the south, the snow-capped peaks of Hakusan to the east, and on clear days a glimmer of the Sea of Japan to the west. It has become a quiet vantage point for travellers who want a full picture of the landscape surrounding the castle town.

Since the opening of the Hokuriku Shinkansen to Kanazawa in 2015, the city has seen a significant rise in both domestic and international visitors. Kuramatsuki sits apart from the heritage districts that draw most tourists, yet the observation lounge gives the area a reason to visit: an unhurried, free-to-enter rooftop perspective on a city that balances centuries of craft culture with a quietly evolving skyline.

## Summary

Opened in 2003, the 19-storey Ishikawa Prefectural Government Building at 98.8 metres is Kanazawa's most prominent government tower, and its free top-floor lounge rewards a detour from the castle town's historic lanes.
