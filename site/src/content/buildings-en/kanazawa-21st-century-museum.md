## A Circle Without a Front

The 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art stands in central Kanazawa, a short walk from Kenroku-en garden. Opened on 9 October 2004, the building was designed by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa working as SANAA. It is essentially flat — almost a single storey — with a circular footprint roughly 113 metres in diameter. The outer wall is glass throughout, so passers-by can see into the building and visitors inside see out to the surrounding park.

SANAA described the building as "a park-like place open to the city." That description is built into the architecture literally: four entrances are placed at equal intervals around the perimeter, so the building has no front and no back. A free-to-enter public zone wraps through the circle alongside the ticketed gallery zone, separated by glass partitions rather than solid walls. Someone who comes only to sit in the café or walk through the corridors sees the galleries, and gallery-goers see the city through the outer glass.

Inside, the gallery rooms vary in size and are scattered without a fixed sequence. There is no prescribed route. Visitors choose their own path from room to room, and the experience shifts depending on the order. Among the permanent works, Leandro Erlich's *Swimming Pool* — a glass-floored installation that makes visitors on one level appear to be underwater to those on another — has become one of the most photographed contemporary artworks in Japan.

SANAA received the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the 2004 Venice Architecture Biennale the same year the museum opened.

## Summary

The 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa opened in 2004, designed by SANAA. Its circular form, absent hierarchy of entrances, and transparent walls offer a quiet but persistent argument about what a public museum can be.
