## Japan's First Double-Skin Supertall

Breeze Tower completed in July 2008 at 2-4-9 Umeda, Kita Ward, Osaka, adjacent to Herbis Plaza in the West Umeda district. The building stands 34 stories above ground with three basement levels, reaching 174.9 m and offering approximately 84,750 sqm of total floor area. Sankei Building and Shimazu Shoten developed the project; Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei handled structural and executive design; Düsseldorf-based Ingenhoven Architects served as design architect; and Kajima Corporation carried out construction.

The building's most distinctive feature is its double-skin facade — a system where the exterior envelope consists of two layers of glass with a ventilated air gap between them. The outer skin shields the inner facade from direct solar radiation, allows natural ventilation through the gap, and improves thermal insulation. At this height and scale, it was unprecedented in Japan at the time of completion.

## Breeze Breeze and the Office Floors

The lower levels (basement 1 through floor 6) house the Breeze Breeze retail complex, a curated fashion and dining destination that draws visitors independently of the office tenants above. Floors 7–8 contain a hall facility, and the remaining upper floors are occupied as offices. This layered programming reflects the West Umeda site's role as both a transit-adjacent commercial hub and an established office address.

West Umeda has historically developed more gradually than the area around Osaka Station to its east. Breeze Tower's bold facade and environmental credentials positioned it as a flagship of the district's gradual rise.

## Summary

Breeze Tower, completed in July 2008, stands 34 stories and 174.9 m tall with approximately 84,750 sqm of floor space. Developed by Sankei Building in Umeda, Osaka, its Ingenhoven-designed double-skin facade represents a landmark in Japanese green architecture. Walking through West Umeda, the tower's glass-wrapped silhouette is unmistakable and well worth a close look.
