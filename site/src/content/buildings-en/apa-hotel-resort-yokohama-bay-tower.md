## A 2,311-Room Tower on Yokohama's Waterfront

Opened in September 2019, APA Hotel & Resort Yokohama Bay Tower is a 35-story, 145-meter skyscraper on the Minatomirai 21 waterfront in Naka Ward, Yokohama. With 2,311 rooms spread across a gross floor area of approximately 65,700 square meters, it ranks among the largest hotels in Japan by room count. The tower was developed and is operated by the APA Group, one of Japan's largest hotel chains, and stands between the historic bay and the modern commercial district of Minatomirai.

The facade is a glass-and-aluminium curtain wall with strong horizontal banding, rising above the surrounding cluster of offices and hotels to become one of the tallest structures in the district. Rooms on the upper floors look directly over Yokohama Port, Yamashita Park, and the span of Yokohama Bay Bridge — a bay-view panorama that has made the hotel a popular base for tourists visiting the port city.

## Yokohama's Waterfront and Its Hotel Demand

The Minatomirai 21 district traces its origin to a large-scale waterfront redevelopment project the City of Yokohama began in 1983. Landmark Tower, Cosmo World, and a string of office and commercial buildings were developed over subsequent decades, drawing large numbers of visitors; yet the district's accommodation supply was long considered insufficient for the demand it generated. APA Hotel & Resort Yokohama Bay Tower addressed a portion of that shortfall when it opened, absorbing domestic and international guests looking for a large-capacity hotel within walking distance of Yokohama's main sights.

The nearest station is Bashamichi on the Minatomirai Line, roughly five minutes on foot. The historic Red Brick Warehouses and Nihon-odori are both close by, making the tower a convenient anchor for exploring Yokohama's port-era architecture as well as its modern skyline.

## Summary

APA Hotel & Resort Yokohama Bay Tower — 35 stories, 145 meters, 2,311 rooms — opened in Minatomirai in September 2019 and quickly became one of the largest hotels in Japan. Its waterfront position gives upper-floor rooms a sweeping view of Yokohama Bay, while Bashamichi Station puts Yokohama's main sights within easy reach on foot. If you're planning a stay in Yokohama, the tower places you at the boundary between the historic port city and its modern seafront.
