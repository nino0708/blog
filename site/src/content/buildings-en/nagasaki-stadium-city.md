## A Private Company Builds a City

Nagasaki Stadium City opened on 14 October 2024 in Koto-machi, central Nagasaki, representing one of Japan's most ambitious privately funded urban developments in recent memory. Developed and operated entirely by Japanet Holdings — the mail-order and media conglomerate that also owns Nagasaki's J.League football club, V-Varen Nagasaki — the complex was built at a cost exceeding ¥100 billion without reliance on public funding. Total floor area is approximately 194,000 m². At its heart are Peace Stadium, a 20,000-seat football-specific venue, and a 6,000-seat multi-purpose arena. Design and construction were led by Takenaka Corporation, Toda Corporation, Environmental Design Institute, and Yasui Architects & Engineers.

What sets Nagasaki Stadium City apart from a conventional sports venue is its explicit ambition to function as a neighbourhood rather than an event facility. Commercial areas, co-working offices, and an international-grade hotel are woven through the complex so that it remains active on non-match days. The stadium turf management, arena floor configurations, and wayfinding were all designed with year-round operational efficiency in mind.

## Sport-Led Urban Regeneration in a Regional City

In Japan, sports stadia and arenas are typically built with public investment, making Nagasaki's all-private model a closely watched experiment. The city's central district had long struggled with ageing building stock and declining footfall, and the scale of Japanet's investment — over ¥100 billion at a single address — has raised expectations of a wider ripple effect across adjacent commercial and business districts. The arena also serves a B.League basketball club, and its flexible floor plan targets concerts, combat sports, and exhibitions to keep occupancy high outside the sports calendar.

## Summary

Nagasaki Stadium City opened in October 2024, covering approximately 194,000 m² with a 20,000-seat football stadium, a 6,000-seat arena, a hotel, retail spaces, and offices — all financed and managed by Japanet Holdings with over ¥100 billion of private capital. The project demonstrates that sport-led, privately funded urban regeneration is achievable at scale in a regional Japanese city, and the new flows of people it has generated at the heart of Nagasaki are already reshaping the neighbourhood around it.
