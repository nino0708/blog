## Shibuya's Western Gateway

Emerging from Shibuya Station's west exit, the first thing that stops the eye is Shibuya Mark City — two towers rising from a shared podium at the foot of Dogenzaka. The East tower (25 floors, 99.67 metres) and West tower (23 floors, 95.55 metres) were completed in January 2000, designed by a joint venture of Nihon Sekkei and Tokyu Design Consultants.

What sets the building apart from a conventional office-hotel complex is its intimate entanglement with public transit. The Keio Inokashira Line's Shibuya terminus is enclosed within the structure, and one of Tokyo's busiest intercity bus terminals occupies its lower levels. The building is not merely adjacent to Shibuya's transit network — it is part of it.

## A Head Start on Shibuya's Skyline

When Mark City opened in 2000, the massive redevelopment projects that would later reshape Shibuya had not yet begun. [Shibuya Hikarie](/buildings/shibuya-hikarie/) would not arrive until 2012, and [Shibuya Scramble Square](/buildings/shibuya-scramble-square/) would follow only in 2019. By that timeline, Mark City served as Shibuya's first modern mixed-use skyscraper at the station's edge, occupying a position and a scale that newer towers would vastly exceed — but never erase.

The complex remains one of the first things visitors encounter when leaving the station on the Dogenzaka side. The bus terminal hums with long-distance coaches; commuters funnel through the Keio Line gates within; shoppers navigate the lower floors. Over two decades after its opening, Mark City operates as quietly essential infrastructure, the kind of building that a city only notices when it imagines doing without it.

## Summary

Shibuya Mark City, completed in 2000, comprises two towers — the 25-floor East and 23-floor West — reaching just under 100 metres. Designed by Nihon Sekkei and Tokyu Design Consultants, it incorporates the Keio Inokashira Line's Shibuya station and a major bus terminal, fusing transit infrastructure with offices, a hotel, and retail. As the first large-scale mixed-use tower to be built at Shibuya Station, it set an early template for the district's ongoing vertical transformation. Cross Shibuya's scramble intersection and look west along Dogenzaka — Mark City's twin towers frame the start of the climb.
