## Twin Towers on the Shibaura Waterfront

A ten-minute walk from Hamamatsucho Station or a short distance from Hinode Station on the Yurikamome line, Seavans S Tower and its neighbour N Tower rise together from a block in Shibaura, Minato. Both were completed in 1991, each standing 24 floors above ground with two basement levels. The name Seavans — evoking a seafront residence — reflects the ambitions of a development designed to transform a former industrial strip along Tokyo Bay into a viable office address.

The two towers are linked by a landscaped atrium courtyard between them. This shared outdoor space, partly sheltered by the surrounding buildings, provides a midday gathering point for the complex's tenants and has contributed to the complex's reputation for thoughtful site planning.

## A Pioneer of Bay-Area Office Development

Shibaura's transformation from factory and warehouse district to office and residential waterfront was one of Tokyo's signature urban shifts of the late 1980s and 1990s. Seavans stood among the first large-scale office offerings in the area, bringing substantial floorplates — over 600 tsubo per standard floor — to a location with straightforward access to arterial transit. The complex attracted business tenants seeking large, contiguous floors in a setting that felt distinct from the crowded central business district.

A major renovation carried out in 2012 updated the building's facilities and refreshed its office specifications. The building is currently managed by a Daiwa House-affiliated property management company and continues to operate as an active multi-tenant office complex. In recent years the Shibaura canal-side has seen a wave of residential and commercial development, and the waterfront promenade now draws a lunchtime crowd that would have been inconceivable in the site's industrial past.

## Summary

Seavans S Tower was completed in 1991 — 24 floors above ground, two below — as the southern half of a twin-tower office complex in Shibaura, Minato. The building's courtyard atrium linking it to N Tower earned recognition for its urban design, and a 2012 renovation kept its specifications current. Walk from Hamamatsucho or Hinode stations to reach the complex and to trace the broader transformation of Tokyo's inner waterfront from heavy industry to contemporary office and residential neighbourhood.
