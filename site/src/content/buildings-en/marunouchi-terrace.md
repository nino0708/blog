## A Low Building That Opens to the Street

Among the glass towers that line the avenues of Marunouchi, one building stands out for doing the opposite of reaching upward. Marunouchi Terrace, completed on September 30, 2020, is ten floors tall and 56.65 meters high — a measured, almost modest presence alongside the supertalls that surround it. Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei handled the design. Every floor of the building opens onto a glass-enclosed terrace facing Marunouchi Nakadori, giving the facade a stacked horizontal rhythm rather than the smooth curtain walls of its neighbors. The effect is to make the building feel approachable from the street — each floor visible, each space connected to the outside air.

## The Former Nippon Kangyo Bank Site

The land beneath Marunouchi Terrace has history. The original Nippon Kangyo Bank headquarters — a building designed by Tōgō Murano, a major figure in modern Japanese architecture — once occupied this address. The current development replaces it with three structures: Mizuho Marunouchi Tower, a 29-story office and hotel building that carries the financial institution's primary operations; the Bank Hall, a meeting and training facility for the Japanese banking industry; and Marunouchi Terrace, the commercial wing. Together the three were developed by Mizuho Financial Group, the Japanese Bankers Association, and Mitsubishi Estate. In the pattern that Marunouchi redevelopment often follows, the new buildings increase density while the ground-level commercial program reconnects the block to the pedestrian street.

## Summary

Marunouchi Terrace was completed in 2020, with 10 floors and a height of 56.65 meters. Its defining feature — open terraces on every level — makes it one of the more distinctive commercial buildings in a district dominated by much taller glass towers. On a walk up Marunouchi Nakadori, look for its stacked horizontal lines; they offer a moment of lightness in a street of considerable architectural ambition.
