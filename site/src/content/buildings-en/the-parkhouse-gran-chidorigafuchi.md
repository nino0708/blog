## Fourteen Storeys at the Edge of the Moat

Where the Chidorigafuchi promenade quiets down north of the boathouse, the neighbourhood of Sanbancho begins. On a plot facing the outer moat of the Imperial Palace, a fourteen-storey residential building stands at a scale that neither dominates nor disappears among the surrounding low-rise streets. The Parkhouse Gran Chidorigafuchi was completed in March 2015 at 2-1 Sanbancho, Chiyoda City, developed by Mitsubishi Estate Residence as a luxury condominium.

The building contains 73 homes across fourteen above-ground and two below-ground floors, all oriented to face the moat and the Imperial Palace greenery beyond. Hanzomon Station on the Tokyo Metro Hanzomon Line lies seven minutes on foot. The building was designed jointly by Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei and Takenaka Corporation, with Takenaka also serving as the general contractor. The structure is reinforced concrete and steel, built on a seismic isolation system.

## A Quiet Address in Chidorigafuchi's Landscape

The Sanbancho and Yonbancho districts of Chiyoda retain a character shaped by embassies and older residential estates. Chidorigafuchi moat runs along the western edge, connecting this neighbourhood to the Imperial Palace grounds through a band of water and tree canopy that functions as a continuous landscape corridor. Building on a site this close to the moat means the scale and massing of the structure enter into a direct relationship with that corridor.

Fourteen storeys is a restrained height for a central Tokyo residential building, and it sits easily within the established grain of Sanbancho. The view from the units changes with each season: cherry blossoms drifting onto the water in spring, deep summer foliage, autumn colour along the bank. The seismic isolation system supports long-term habitation — a considered choice for a building positioned in one of the city's most historically regarded addresses.

## Summary

The Parkhouse Gran Chidorigafuchi was completed in March 2015 at Sanbancho 2-1, Chiyoda City. The fourteen-storey, two-basement building contains 73 homes and sits on a seismic isolation system. Design was led by Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei in collaboration with Takenaka Corporation; Takenaka also served as contractor. The building's defining quality is its position directly on the Chidorigafuchi moat, with every home oriented toward the Imperial Palace forests. Walking north from Hanzomon along the promenade and turning toward Sanbancho, the building comes into view against a backdrop of water and green canopy.
