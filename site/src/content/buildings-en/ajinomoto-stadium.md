## A Covered Stadium in the Tama Hills

About a five-minute walk from Fuda Station on the Keio Line, in the city of Chofu in western Tokyo, Tokyo Stadium opened its gates in March 2001. The structure — completed in October 2000 at a construction cost of approximately 30.7 billion yen — was designed by Nihon Sekkei and built by joint ventures led by Taisei Construction and Kajima Construction. The playing field measures 105 by 68 metres and the stadium accommodates approximately 49,970 spectators, all under a continuous roof that wraps every section of seating.

That roof is the stadium's most practical achievement. Unlike open-bowl designs that leave parts of the crowd exposed, every seat at Tokyo Stadium is sheltered from rain, a significant advantage in a city where late-summer matches can coincide with typhoon season. The result is a venue that functions reliably across seasons and weather conditions, which helps explain its enduring appeal to both clubs and event organisers.

## A Multi-Sport Legacy

FC Tokyo and Tokyo Verdy have made the stadium their shared J.League home, and rugby unions have used it regularly for international matches. The stadium's portfolio of major events is extensive: it was selected as a 2002 FIFA World Cup venue, hosted matches at Rugby World Cup 2019, and served as a football competition venue at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games. This combination — club football week in and week out, plus periodic headline international events — has given the stadium a layered identity that few suburban venues achieve.

The naming rights deal that branded it Ajinomoto Stadium brought it one of the most widely recognised commercial stadium names in Japan, though it retains its official designation as Tokyo Stadium in municipal documents.

## Summary

Tokyo Stadium (Ajinomoto Stadium) was completed in October 2000 and opened in March 2001 in Chofu, western Tokyo, with a capacity of approximately 49,970 seats under a full roof. Designed by Nihon Sekkei and operated as a home ground for two J.League clubs, it has also hosted World Cup, Rugby World Cup, and Olympic matches. Walk from Fuda Station on match day — or simply on a quiet afternoon — to see one of the Tokyo metropolitan area's landmark sports venues.
