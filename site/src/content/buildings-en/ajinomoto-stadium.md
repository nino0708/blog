## A Stadium in the Musashino Landscape

Tokyo Stadium stands in Nishimachi, Chofu City, a short distance from the Koshu Kaido highway in the residential and commercial zone of western Tokyo. Completed in March 2001 and designed by Nippon Sekkei, the five-storey stadium holds approximately 50,000 spectators — a scale that brought major-league sport firmly into the western suburbs. A continuous roof canopy wraps the oval bowl, covering almost the entire seating area and providing shelter from rain across the full capacity. The proximity of the Chofu interchange makes the site accessible by road as well as rail. The building opened under the name Tokyo Stadium; naming rights were later sold and it became widely known as Ajinomoto Stadium, though the formal name has since been updated through further naming arrangements.

## Home Ground and National Stage

Nippon Sekkei's design prioritises the relationship between the pitch and the stands. Seating angles and row spacing were calibrated to bring spectators as close as structurally possible to the playing surface, so that even the furthest sections of the stand feel engaged with the action below. FC Tokyo and Tokyo Verdy, two of the capital's J.League clubs, have used the stadium as their home ground since it opened, giving the venue a continuous and deeply local identity. The Japan national football team has also played numerous home matches here. In 2019 the stadium hosted several matches in the Rugby World Cup, extending its record from domestic football to international rugby. For a stadium in the outer suburbs, the scale — 50,000 seats, the full professional infrastructure — delivers an atmosphere on match day that rivals any venue in central Tokyo.

## Summary

Tokyo Stadium (Ajinomoto Stadium) opened in March 2001, five floors above ground, designed by Nippon Sekkei for the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. Its canopied bowl holds approximately 50,000 spectators and has served J.League clubs, national teams and Rugby World Cup matches over more than two decades. Even outside match days the stadium's sheer size is worth experiencing up close — a walk around its perimeter in Chofu gives a sense of how thoroughly a single building can anchor a neighbourhood's sporting identity.
