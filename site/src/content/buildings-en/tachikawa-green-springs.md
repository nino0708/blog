## A Campus of Buildings at the Edge of a Park

Green Springs opened in 2020 at 3-1 Midori-cho, Tachikawa, on land adjacent to Showa Memorial Park — the vast national park that stretches north of Tachikawa Station. Rather than a single high-rise, the development takes the form of a campus: multiple buildings of varying scale are arranged around a central outdoor concourse that invites visitors to move between the indoor and outdoor environments throughout the day. The project was designed by Yamashita Sekkei, which received a design award for the complex.

Key programs include PLAY! PARK, an indoor-outdoor event and performance venue with capacity for approximately 2,500 people; PLAY! MUSEUM, a contemporary art gallery; office buildings with large terraces; and hotel accommodation. Commercial and dining tenants are distributed across the campus at ground level.

## Connecting City and Nature in Tachikawa

Tachikawa is one of the designated regional centers of the Tama area west of Tokyo, historically defined by its proximity to a former US air base — Showa Memorial Park itself sits on reclaimed base land. The Green Springs project is part of a continuing effort to activate the northern side of Tachikawa Station and to anchor a new urban district at the park's edge.

The multi-building campus format, rather than a single large-footprint block, was a deliberate design choice by Yamashita Sekkei. The fragmented arrangement improves ventilation, provides visual continuity with the park beyond, and allows each sub-building to be expanded or updated independently — a quality the design team described as "sustainability, expandability, and renewability."

The result is a place where a visitor might cycle from the park into a museum, work in a WeWork office, attend an evening concert, and stay overnight in the hotel — all without leaving the complex.

## Summary

Green Springs (2020) is a campus-style mixed-use development in Tachikawa designed by Yamashita Sekkei, blending performing arts, offices, a hotel, and museum space with Showa Memorial Park's greenery just beyond its boundary.
