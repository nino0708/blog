## A Linear Park Above the Shibuya Streetscape

Miyashita Park opened in August 2020 on the site of the former Miyashita Park municipal facility in Jingumae, Shibuya, Tokyo. The project was the first in Shibuya to use the Japanese government's tachi-toshi kouen seido (three-dimensional urban park system), a framework that allows private commercial development to be combined with public park space in the same structure. The North Block rises 18 floors above two basement levels to a height of 75.1 m; the South Block is a four-story, 21.4 m structure. Combined total floor area reaches approximately 46,000 m². Nikken Sekkei led the concept and basic design; Takenaka Corporation handled execution design and construction. The result is a steel-frame structure light enough to carry a fully landscaped 330 m rooftop park above the South Block, complete with grass lawns, playground equipment, a skateboard park, a climbing wall, and a basketball court—all free to enter.

## From Contested Space to Urban Landmark

The site's history is layered. The original Miyashita Park, opened in 1953, served for decades as a free public green space while also becoming a de facto home for homeless residents—a tension that generated debate throughout the 2010s when redevelopment plans advanced. Mitsui Fudosan secured a 30-year fixed-term land lease from the ward, and the design process navigated competing community interests before construction began. The North Block's upper floors house the hotel "sequence MIYASHITA PARK," and a continuous route connects street level, the commercial floors, and the rooftop park through escalators and sky decks. The building now links Shibuya Stream to the Omotesando area, drawing pedestrians who might not previously have passed through this corner of Shibuya.

## Summary

Completed in 2020 and reaching 75.1 m across 18 floors, Miyashita Park demonstrates how public green space and private commercial development can share a single structure. Walking the 330 m rooftop while looking out over the Jingumae rooftops gives an unusually calm perspective on one of Tokyo's most energetic neighbourhoods.
