## A Public Park Lifted into the Sky

MIYASHITA PARK stands at 6-20-10 Jingumae, Shibuya, Tokyo — an 18-story, 2-basement complex completed in 2020. Developed by Mitsui Fudosan and designed by Nikken Sekkei, the building has a total floor area of approximately 46,000 square meters, stacking retail floors, a hotel, and a rooftop public park into a single elongated structure running along Meiji Avenue.

The project's core idea was to apply Japan's "three-dimensional urban park" legislation, which allows a city-designated park to be rebuilt on top of a private building in land-scarce urban areas. The original Miyashita Park, opened in 1966, had been central to Shibuya's youth culture for decades. Rather than simply replace it, the redevelopment lifted the park onto the rooftop — roughly four stories above street level — where a lawn plaza, skate park, and climbing wall now offer an open public space above the city rooftops. The retail floors occupy basement level 2 through the third floor, with elevators and exterior staircases connecting shoppers to the park above.

## The Eastern Gateway of Shibuya's Renewal

Throughout the 2010s, Shibuya Station's surroundings underwent intensive redevelopment, adding towers like Shibuya Scramble Square and Shibuya Stream. MIYASHITA PARK fits into this transformation as the development along the Meiji Avenue side, completing the redevelopment as an urban fabric rather than a cluster of isolated towers. At the basement level, a pedestrian passage connects toward the Shibuya Stream side along the Shibuya River, extending the walkable network from the station toward Harajuku and Jingumae.

The building opened at the height of Japan's first COVID-19 state-of-emergency declaration in 2020, initially seeing subdued footfall due to movement restrictions. As conditions normalized, however, it found its footing as one of Shibuya's new landmarks, attracting both domestic and international visitors. The design — inserting a narrow tower into a constrained site between Meiji Avenue and the Shibuya River — demonstrates how contemporary urban architecture can balance private commercial development with public obligation.

## Summary

MIYASHITA PARK opened in 2020 as an 18-story, 2-basement mixed-use complex with approximately 46,000 square meters of floor area. Developed by Mitsui Fudosan and designed by Nikken Sekkei, it revived the former Miyashita Park as a rooftop urban park while stacking retail and hotel functions below it. As part of the broader Shibuya redevelopment, it connects the station area to Meiji Avenue and beyond. The rooftop park offers a rare elevated view of the Shibuya cityscape — well worth visiting between stops on a Shibuya walk.
