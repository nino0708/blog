## A Supertall Complex at Umeda's Western Edge

Standing at 2-5-25 Umeda, Kita Ward, Osaka, Herbis OSAKA rises 40 floors and approximately 190 meters above the western end of Umeda. Completed in 1997 and developed by Hanshin Electric Railway, with design and construction by Takenaka Corporation, the tower brought a dense mix of office, hotel, and retail uses to the edge of Osaka's most concentrated commercial district.

The building's upper floors from the 29th to the 37th house The Ritz-Carlton Osaka, while the lower floors connect directly to the Herbis PLAZA shopping complex and the city's underground mall network. This vertical layering of luxury hotel, offices, and retail space made Herbis OSAKA one of the first large-scale mixed-use towers in the Osaka-Kita area, setting a template that later developments in Umeda would expand upon.

## Umeda's Western Redevelopment

The land west of Umeda Station was converted during the 1990s as former freight yards and industrial sites made way for urban development. Herbis OSAKA's 1997 opening was a pivotal moment in that transformation. A companion building, Herbis PLAZA ENT, was added to the west in 2005, completing the commercial footprint along the walkway connecting Hanshin Osaka Umeda Station and JR Osaka Station.

When completed, Herbis OSAKA's approximate height of 190 meters placed it among the tallest buildings in Osaka Prefecture, standing alongside the Umeda Sky Building (completed 1993, 173 m) to the northwest. The tower held that prominence until [Grand Front Osaka](/buildings/grand-front-osaka/) opened in 2013, ushering in the next phase of the district's growth.

## Summary

Herbis OSAKA was completed in 1997, rising 40 floors to approximately 190 meters. Developed by Hanshin Electric Railway and built by Takenaka Corporation, it remains a defining presence at Umeda's western edge. The best way to experience it is to enter from the underground mall, pass through Herbis PLAZA, step out onto street level, and walk northwest toward the Umeda Sky Building — a short route that traces the westward spread of one of Japan's most layered commercial districts.
