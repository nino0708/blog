## Osaka's First Roofed Stadium

On 1 March 1997, Kyocera Dome Osaka — formally known as Osaka Dome — opened in Nishi Ward, a few minutes on foot from the Osaka Municipal Subway's Dome-Mae Chiyozaki station. It was designed by Nikken Sekkei and developed by Osaka City Dome Co., Ltd. At 72 metres tall and roughly 166 metres in diameter, it became Osaka's first fully enclosed baseball stadium, sheltering about 44,000 spectators under a fixed steel-and-PTFE-membrane roof that cannot retract.

When the dome opened, the Orix BlueWave (now Orix Buffaloes) moved in as the primary tenant. The building quickly proved its value as a concert venue as well: the fixed roof, combined with the large floor area, made it one of the most sought-after spaces in Japan for large-scale touring shows. Kyocera Corporation acquired naming rights in 2006, giving the stadium its present name; the original formal name, Osaka Dome, remains the legal designation.

## Dome Architecture on the Western Fringe of Central Osaka

Nishi Ward lies just west of Osaka's commercial core. The dome's arrival in 1997 spurred redevelopment of the surrounding blocks, adding hotels, restaurants, and retail facilities to what had previously been a quieter residential and industrial pocket. The area now draws visitors on both event and non-event days.

Osaka's architectural ambition runs in many directions simultaneously. For a sense of how the city has rebuilt itself in recent decades, the dome's horizontal mass contrasts interestingly with the vertical thrust of [Grand Front Osaka](/en/buildings/grand-front-osaka/) in Umeda (opened 2013) or [Abeno Harukas](/en/buildings/abeno-harukas/) in the south (300 metres, completed 2014). Each represents a different scale and typology of large-scale building in the same city.
