## Jean Nouvel in Shiodome

In Higashi-Shimbashi, Minato City — in the Shiodome district redeveloped from the former Shiodome Freight Station — the Dentsu Headquarters Building opened in December 2002. The building stands 213 metres tall across 48 storeys, its design led by the Atelier Jean Nouvel of French architect Jean Nouvel, with Jerde Partnership International as a co-design partner. Construction was carried out by a joint venture of five of Japan's largest general contractors: Obayashi, Kajima, Shimizu, Taisei and Takenaka. The programme combines the Dentsu Group's consolidated head office with the Caretta Shiodome retail, restaurant and theatre complex in the lower floors.

The facade is characteristic of Nouvel's interest in light and reflection: glass planes shift in tone and luminosity across the day, and the tower becomes a lit object at night, visible from the waterfront near Hamarikyu Gardens and from the elevated expressway that skirts the district. The upper floors are given to Dentsu's office operations; the lower Caretta Shiodome section hosts restaurants, shops and the Dentsu Hall performance venue, with an observation restaurant near the summit.

## The Centrepiece of a Redeveloped District

Shiodome was redeveloped through the 2000s on a large footprint cleared from freight rail infrastructure, and the Dentsu Building was planned as the centrepiece of that effort. Neighbouring structures — including Acty Shiodome and Shiodome City Center — make up a cluster of high-rises that together have established Shiodome as a southern Tokyo business and entertainment hub.

The involvement of a European architect of Nouvel's standing in a major Japanese corporate headquarters was unusual at the time, and it brought international attention to the building. Among Tokyo's high-rise canon, relatively few large towers can point to a prominent foreign designer, and the Dentsu Building remains a notable exception.

## Summary

The Dentsu Headquarters Building opened in December 2002: 48 storeys, 213 metres tall, designed by Jean Nouvel in Shiodome's major urban redevelopment zone. It is simultaneously a corporate office building, a public retail and cultural destination, and a piece of international architecture visible from Tokyo Bay.
