## Tange's Museum at the Heart of Minato Mirai

Yokohama Museum of Art stands in Minato Mirai 21, Nishi Ward, Yokohama, completed on 31 March 1988 and opened on 3 November 1989. The design came from Kenzo Tange Associates, the firm responsible for some of modern Japan's most significant civic architecture. The building is a reinforced steel-concrete structure of eight stories above ground (with parts rising to three stories), covering a total floor area of 27,014 square meters on a site of 19,803 square meters. Its most prominent feature is a large arcade-style atrium at the entrance, which draws visitors naturally from the open forecourt into the museum interior. The museum opened several years before the Yokohama Landmark Tower (1993), making it one of the first major landmarks to give architectural form to Minato Mirai 21's cultural ambitions.

## From Opening Day to Renewal

Since its inauguration, the museum has built a collection centered on modern and contemporary art, actively incorporating photography and video work alongside painting and sculpture. It established itself as the principal art museum for Kanagawa Prefecture and drew visitors from across the metropolitan region. A first large-scale renovation began in March 2021 and ran for approximately three years; the museum reopened in March 2025. The renovation's design and supervision was handled by Tange Urban Architecture, Kenzo Tange's successor firm, while architect Kumiko Inui's office oversaw the interior spatial arrangement and signage design. The updated spaces allow more artworks to be shown more clearly, while preserving the original character of Tange's building.

A walk combining the museum with the [Yokohama Landmark Tower](/buildings/yokohama-landmark-tower/) and [Osanbashi Cruise Terminal](/buildings/osanbashi-cruise-terminal/) maps out three decades of ambition in Minato Mirai.

## Summary

Yokohama Museum of Art was completed in 1988 and opened in 1989. Designed by Kenzo Tange Associates, this eight-story, 27,014-square-meter building has served as the cultural anchor of Minato Mirai 21 for more than three decades. Its 2025 reopening gave it a refreshed interior while keeping Tange's spacious forecourt and atrium intact — a good reason to visit Minato Mirai again.
