## Chiba's Convention Centrepiece

Makuhari Messe stands in the Makuhari New Urban Centre in Mihama Ward, Chiba, roughly 30 kilometres east of central Tokyo. It opened in October 1989, designed by Fumihiko Maki — one of the most celebrated Japanese architects of the twentieth century and a Pritzker Prize laureate. The complex comprises three main components: a large exhibition hall block, an international conference centre and an event hall. Together they cover a total floor area of 164,919 square metres, making the facility one of the largest convention venues in Japan.

Makuhari New Urban Centre was a major urban development project initiated by Chiba City and Chiba Prefecture in the 1980s. Former coastal industrial land along Tokyo Bay was reimagined as a sub-centre combining office, commercial and convention functions. Makuhari Messe was the keystone facility planned from the outset to anchor the district. Maki's design delivers clean geometric facades and column-free exhibition spaces that maximise layout flexibility — a hallmark of his disciplined modernist approach.

## A Venue that Shaped Japan's Convention Culture

Since opening, Makuhari Messe has hosted international trade fairs, motor shows, academic conferences and large-scale concerts. The column-free main exhibition halls allow organisers to configure space freely for events of vastly different character. The international conference centre provides multiple auditoriums and breakout rooms, enabling concurrent sessions for large international academic gatherings. JR Keiyo Line's Kaihin-Makuhari Station is within walking distance, and both Haneda and Narita airports are accessible within an hour, giving the complex an enviable transport profile.

Makuhari Messe opened in 1989 with a total floor area of 164,919 m², designed by Fumihiko Maki. Visit during a major trade fair and experience how this planned district on Chiba's reclaimed coast transforms into a temporary city within a city.
