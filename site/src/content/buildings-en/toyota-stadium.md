## A World Cup Venue on the Yahagi River

About fifteen minutes on foot from Meitetsu Toyota-shi or Shin-Toyota stations, on the bank of the Yahagi River in Toyota, Aichi, stands Toyota Stadium. Completed in October 2001, the facility was developed by Toyota City and designed by Kisho Kurokawa of Kisho Kurokawa Architect & Associates. It rises six floors with a seating capacity of 44,380. The following year it hosted several matches — including group-stage and round-of-sixteen games — in the 2002 FIFA World Cup, co-hosted by Japan and South Korea.

The plan is roughly elliptical, and all stands are covered by a continuous roof. A retractable section over the pitch opens or closes according to weather, giving the stadium full-weather capability for official football matches. The natural-grass pitch sits low in the bowl, with the lower tier of seating angled steeply to bring spectators close to the action — a configuration that generates a strong atmosphere during large matches.

## Kisho Kurokawa and the City of Cars

Kisho Kurokawa is one of Japan's most internationally recognized architects, known as a founding voice of the Metabolism movement in the 1960s. His later work — including the Kuala Lumpur International Airport Terminal (1998) and the National Art Center, Tokyo (2007) — showed a continuing engagement with large-scale public infrastructure. Toyota Stadium, completed three years before his death in 2007, belongs to this late period.

Toyota City's urban identity is shaped by its role as the global headquarters city for Toyota Motor Corporation. The stadium operates as a civic anchor in a city whose public life is otherwise closely associated with the automobile industry. Since opening, it has served as the home ground for Nagoya Grampus, one of the original founding clubs of the J.League, drawing supporters from across the Chukyo metropolitan area on match days.

## Summary

Toyota Stadium was completed in October 2001, designed by Kisho Kurokawa for Toyota City. Its retractable-roof design seats 44,380 on a natural-grass pitch, and it served as an official 2002 FIFA World Cup venue. Today it continues as the home of Nagoya Grampus in the J.League.
