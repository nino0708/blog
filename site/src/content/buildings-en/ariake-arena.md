## 15,000 Seats on the Bay

In the reclaimed waterfront district of Ariake in Koto City, a large-scale indoor arena completed in 2019 brought a new sporting heartbeat to Tokyo's bay area. Ariake Arena stands five storeys tall and reaches a height of about 37 metres — compact in profile compared with office towers, but immense in footprint. Its approximately 15,000 seats made it the designated venue for volleyball and wheelchair basketball at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Tokyo Metropolitan Government developed the facility at a reported cost of around ¥36.1 billion; the basic design was led by Kume Sekkei, with Takenaka Corporation leading the construction joint venture.

The arena's wide, low-slung roof gives it a distinctive horizontality that sits in dialogue with the flat, open landscape of Ariake. Inside, steeply raked seating wraps tightly around the arena floor, pulling spectators close to the action regardless of where they sit. That intimacy was praised during the Olympic tournaments and remains a draw for concerts and sporting events held there since the Games.

## Ariake's Evolution as an Events Hub

Ariake has been evolving for decades into Tokyo's convention and entertainment district. Tokyo Big Sight, Ariake Colosseum, and several large sports facilities already lined the waterfront before the arena was added. The 2019 completion of Ariake Arena — alongside other Olympic venues nearby — cemented the area's role as a destination for large-scale gatherings.

Post-Games, the arena has hosted music concerts, e-sports events, and professional sports, demonstrating the versatility its design intended. It is accessible from both Rinkai Line's International Exhibition Center Station and Yurikamome's Ariake Tennis no Mori Station, making it easy to combine a visit with a walk along the waterfront promenade or a stop at Ariake Garden's shopping complex.

## Summary

Ariake Arena was completed in 2019 — five floors, approximately 37 metres tall, seating around 15,000. Developed by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and designed by Kume Sekkei, it served as a centrepiece of the Tokyo 2020 Games and has continued as a major events venue since. Head to the Ariake waterfront and look for the broad, horizontal roofline that marks one of Japan's most versatile indoor arenas.
