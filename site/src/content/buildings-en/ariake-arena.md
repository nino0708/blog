## A 15,000-Seat Arena on Tokyo's Waterfront

Ariake Arena opened in December 2019 on the reclaimed land of Tokyo's Ariake district. At five stories tall and about 37 meters in height, with a total floor area of 47,252 square meters, the facility was developed by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government as a dedicated Olympic venue. During the 2020 Tokyo Olympics, it hosted the volleyball competitions; at the Paralympics, it was the stage for wheelchair rugby and wheelchair basketball.

Ariake sits on land that, as recently as the early 2000s, was largely industrial. The arena's completion accelerated the district's shift into one of Tokyo's principal large-scale event and sports destinations.

## Anchor of a Waterfront Sports District

The arena's floor system is designed for flexibility—concerts, ice shows, and sporting events all fit within its reconfigurable space. With a capacity of approximately 15,000, it stands among the largest arenas in Japan and operates alongside Ariake Colosseum and Tokyo Big Sight to form a dense cluster of major event venues accessible by both Rinkai Line and the Yurikamome monorail.

The arena's exterior projects a solid, unadorned mass suited to its scale, a form that fits the wide industrial scale of the Ariake waterfront it transformed.

## Summary

Completed in 2019, Ariake Arena is a five-story, roughly 37-meter-tall multipurpose facility with a total floor area of 47,252 square meters. Built by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government as an Olympic venue, it has remained one of the most active large arenas in Tokyo since the Games concluded. Walk the Ariake waterfront on a clear day and the arena's bulk reads clearly against Tokyo Bay's open horizon.
