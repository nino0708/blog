## A 47-Storey Tower Linked to the Station

At Shin-Maruko-Higashi 3-chome, Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki — connected directly to Musashikosugi Station by a pedestrian deck — stands Park City Musashikosugi Station Forest Tower. Completed in August 2008, the building rises 47 storeys above ground and three below, to a height of 162.8 metres. It was developed by Mitsui Fudosan and Shin-Nisseki Real Estate, with façade design by architect Jun Mitsui, and forms part of the Kosugi Station Southern Area Redevelopment project that transformed former industrial land into a mixed residential and commercial district.

The name "Station Forest" reflects the intention behind the design: direct station connectivity combined with greenery woven into the public space at the base of the tower. At the time of completion, this kind of station-integrated residential tower — tall, convenient, and green-tinged — represented one vision of how Japanese suburban rail hubs might reinvent themselves for a new generation of urban residents.

## The 2019 Typhoon and Urban Flood Risk

Musashikosugi became the subject of intense national attention in October 2019, when Typhoon Hagibis brought flooding to the Tama River basin. Mechanical parking facilities and electrical systems concentrated in basements — a design convention of many tower apartment buildings — were shown to carry specific vulnerabilities in flood events. The episode prompted a broader rethinking of how river geography, hazard maps, and urban topography relate to high-rise residential living.

Station Forest Tower sits at some distance from the Tama River's main channel, but the experience placed all of Musashikosugi's residential towers in a new frame. What had been discussed almost entirely in terms of convenience and lifestyle acquired a counterweight: the question of how a city built on former factory land, beside a river, should plan for water.

## Summary

Park City Musashikosugi Station Forest Tower was completed in August 2008, rising 47 storeys above grade to 162.8 metres. Developed by Mitsui Fudosan and Shin-Nisseki Real Estate, with façade design by Jun Mitsui, it anchored the redevelopment of the Kosugi Station Southern Area and brought Musashikosugi into the era of high-rise residential living.

Walk out onto the station deck and look up at the 47 floors above you, then think about the flat industrial land this all replaced — and the river that runs nearby. The tower is a record of ambition, convenience, and the questions that followed.
