## Tokyo's International Gateway

Haneda Airport Terminal 3, originally opened on 21 October 2010 as the International Passenger Terminal, brought full-scale international air travel back to central Tokyo. The five-storey building — with a total floor area of 153,581 square metres — was developed by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism alongside Tokyo International Air Terminal Corporation. Design was carried out by a joint venture of Azusa Sekkei, Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects Japan, and Yasui Architects.

The building's horizontal plan — five storeys distributed across a vast floor plate — prioritises the clarity of passenger flow. Check-in halls, departure gates, customs, and immigration are arranged across this spread, designed to accommodate the high volumes of international passenger traffic that the terminal was built to handle. The scale of the terminal reflects the ambition of Japan's national aviation policy: to position Haneda, just fourteen kilometres from central Tokyo, as a full-service international hub.

## Reopening the City Airport to the World

Haneda had operated primarily as a domestic airport for most of its post-war history, with international routes concentrated at Narita from 1978 onward. The opening of a fourth runway in 2010, coinciding with Terminal 3, marked the formal transition back to large-scale international operations at the city airport. The shorter surface journey from central Tokyo compared to Narita was a central appeal, and the terminal has since attracted major international carriers across a wide range of routes.

A satellite building expanded capacity in 2014. On 14 March 2020, the facility was renamed from "International Passenger Terminal" to "Terminal 3," bringing its designation in line with the existing Terminal 1 and Terminal 2.

## Summary

Opened in 2010, five floors above ground, with a total floor area of 153,581 square metres, Haneda Airport Terminal 3 was designed by a joint venture led by Azusa Sekkei and Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects Japan. The terminal represents Japan's commitment to making its nearest major city airport a full international gateway. Quick rail connections from central Tokyo — via Tokyo Monorail or Keikyu line — make it an easy starting point for any international journey from the capital.
