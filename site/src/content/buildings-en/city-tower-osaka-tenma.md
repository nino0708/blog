## A Tall Tower in a Neighbourhood of Narrow Streets

City Tower Osaka Tenma The River & Parks was completed in January–February 2010 in Kita Ward, Osaka. At 45 storeys above ground and one below, it contains 650 residential units developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development. The site lies on the north bank of the Okawa — the old course of the Yodo River — where views extend south towards Osaka Castle Park and east along the river corridor. The building arrived in a district whose character had long been defined by low-rise commerce and dense residential streets rather than tall towers.

Tenma takes its name from Osaka Temmangu, the Shinto shrine to the scholar-deity Tenjin, and the neighbourhood wraps around one of Japan's longest covered shopping arcades. This mix of shrine, market street and working-class housing gave Tenma a distinctly local flavour for most of the twentieth century. In the 2000s, as central Osaka saw a wave of tower condominium development, Tenma became one of the areas where that wave reached — bringing with it residents who wanted city-centre living at a lower cost than Umeda or Nakanoshima.

## River, Park and Skyline

The building's name references two of its setting's defining features: the Okawa River to the south and the green belt of Tsurunomi Park nearby. A 45-storey tower in this location has a pronounced effect on the local skyline; its silhouette reads clearly from the south bank of the Okawa and from the elevated walkways around Osaka Castle. Construction was handled by Obayashi Corporation. The project's 650 units made it one of the largest single residential towers in the North Osaka area at the time of completion.

## Summary

City Tower Osaka Tenma The River & Parks, completed in 2010, is a 45-storey, one-basement residential tower with 650 units developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development and built by Obayashi Corporation. It brought the scale and verticality of central Osaka's redevelopment era to a neighbourhood previously untouched by tall buildings, and its river views and proximity to Osaka Temmangu give it a setting unlike most downtown towers. For more of Osaka's vertical cityscape, [Grand Front Osaka](/buildings/grand-front-osaka) is a short walk or subway ride north, and offers a very different scale of mixed-use urban redevelopment to compare.
