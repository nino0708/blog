## A Nearly 200-Metre Tower on an Old Shitamachi Island

In April 2026, a 58-story tower was completed in Tsukishima 3-chome, Chuo Ward, Tokyo. Grand City Tower Tsukishima, developed jointly by Sumitomo Realty & Development, Tokyo Tatemono, Daiwa House Industry, and the Metropolitan Fireproof Building Corporation, stands 197.65 metres (maximum height 199.45 m) above the island. It is the product of the Tsukishima 3-chome North District Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project and represents the most dramatic single change to the neighbourhood's skyline in its modern history.

Tsukishima is best known outside Japan for its *monjayaki* pancake restaurants along the Nishinaka-dori shopping street. For much of its postwar history, the island retained a dense, low-rise shitamachi character while the adjacent waterfront of Kachidoki and Harumi filled with high-rise towers like [Deux Tours Kachidoki](/en/buildings/deux-tours-kachidoki/) and [Harumi Flag](/en/buildings/harumi-flag/). The completion of Grand City Tower Tsukishima marks the arrival of a supertall residential tower at the centre of that traditional streetscape.

## Four Parties, One Public Framework

Type 1 Urban Redevelopment is a Japanese planning mechanism that consolidates fragmented land rights in densely built, fire-prone blocks, enabling large-scale rebuilding with fire-resistant structures. In this case, three major private developers and a public housing agency formed the development entity. The inclusion of the Metropolitan Fireproof Building Corporation signals the public interest dimension of the project: replacing wooden-construction districts with fire-resistant urban fabric is an explicit policy goal in Tokyo.

Grand City Tower Tsukishima arrived in the same wave as [Mid Tower Grand](/en/buildings/mid-tower-grand-tsukishima/), completed in 2020 in Tsukishima 1-chome, showing how redevelopment pressure has moved through the island district by district.

## Summary

Grand City Tower Tsukishima was completed in April 2026, with 58 stories above ground and two basement levels, reaching 197.65 metres. It stands as one of the tallest residential towers in Tokyo and as the centrepiece of a public-private redevelopment effort that transformed a dense wooden-construction block in Tsukishima into a landmark supertall.

Walking from Tsukishima Station toward the 3-chome end of the island, the contrast between the surviving low-rise restaurant lanes and the 58-story tower behind them is immediate and instructive — the tensions and possibilities of Tokyo's ongoing urban renewal made visible in a single view.
