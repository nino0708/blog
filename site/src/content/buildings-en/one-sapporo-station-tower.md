## Hokkaido's High-Rise at the North Exit

Completed in October 2023, ONE Sapporo Station Tower rises 48 stories and 175 meters from a site about 200 meters north of Sapporo Station's north exit, in the Kita 8 Nishi 1 district. The 624-unit tower was developed by the Sapporo Station North Exit 8-1 District Urban Redevelopment Association and designed and built by Taisei Corporation in collaboration with Docon. Its reinforced concrete structure — with partial steel elements — is engineered to perform in Hokkaido's harsh winters, delivering both seismic resistance and the insulation standards that sub-zero temperatures demand.

As one of the tallest residential buildings in Hokkaido, the tower adds a prominent new element to the Sapporo skyline when viewed from the city's wide, grid-planned streets.

## The North Exit and Sapporo's Urban Balance

Sapporo's urban core developed historically around Odori Park and the southern side of the station, leaving the north exit area comparatively underbuilt. The Kita 8 Nishi 1 redevelopment project was part of a broader effort to extend the gravity of central Sapporo northward, integrating public realm improvements with the high-density residential program. The tower's 48 floors bring density and vibrancy to a quarter that is evolving from low-rise mixed-use into a transit-oriented urban neighborhood.

On clear Hokkaido days, upper floors of ONE Sapporo Station Tower command views of Mount Teine to the west and the Ishikari Plain stretching outward — a reminder that Sapporo, dense city though it is, sits in one of Japan's most expansive natural landscapes.

## Summary

ONE Sapporo Station Tower completed in October 2023 with 48 floors above ground and 2 below, standing 175 m tall and housing 624 residences near Sapporo Station's north exit. Designed and constructed by Taisei Corporation and Docon, it represents the northward expansion of central Sapporo and stands as one of the most significant additions to the Hokkaido skyline in recent years. If you find yourself at Sapporo Station, walk to the north exit plaza and look up at the tower — it is a good introduction to how far Japan's northernmost major city has grown.
