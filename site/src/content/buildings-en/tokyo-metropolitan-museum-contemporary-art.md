## A Stone Corridor Beside Kiba Park

In March 1995, the Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo opened on a site adjacent to Kiba Park in Koto City, establishing itself as Japan's largest dedicated venue for contemporary art. The building was designed by Takahiko Yanagisawa and TAK Architects & Urban Planning, and its stone-clad facades give it a weight and seriousness that stands in deliberate contrast to the surrounding park greenery. The approach from the street involves descending to a semi-basement level before being drawn into the building's interior, so that the visitor's first experience is one of immersion rather than arrival. At 33,515 square metres of total floor area, the building accommodates permanent collection galleries, temporary exhibition halls, an art library and a screening room, allowing multiple large-scale exhibitions to run simultaneously. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government funded and operates the museum, which forms part of a broader cultural infrastructure that stretches from the Kiba district towards the galleries of Kiyosumi-Shirakawa to the north-east.

## Scale for the Largest International Exhibitions

The museum was established to specialise in works from the 1950s onward, covering both Japanese and international contemporary art. Its scale is a practical asset: the ability to host touring retrospectives, large installations and international group shows without having to compromise on the works selected. After operating for more than two decades, the building underwent a major renovation from 2016 to 2019, reopening with refreshed galleries, improved facilities and updated public amenities. The surrounding Kiba Park — formerly the site of a timber storage district that served Edo-period construction — provides a generous open space that makes the museum easy to reach on foot from several directions, and the two destinations combine naturally as an afternoon itinerary.

## Summary

The Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo opened in 1995, designed by Takahiko Yanagisawa + TAK Architects & Urban Planning and developed by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, with a total floor area of 33,515 square metres. The stone-clad building beside Kiba Park is Japan's largest contemporary art museum and has hosted major international exhibitions since its opening. A renovation completed in 2019 brought the facilities up to date, and the park setting makes the museum a welcoming destination even for visitors who arrive with only a loose intention to look around.
