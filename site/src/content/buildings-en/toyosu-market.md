## From Tsukiji to Toyosu: A New Era for Tokyo's Central Market

On October 11, 2018, Tokyo's central wholesale market opened its doors in the Toyosu district of Koto City, formally relocating from the historic Tsukiji site where fish and produce had been traded for over eight decades. The new facility covers approximately 40.7 hectares of reclaimed Tokyo Bay land and offers a total floor area of roughly 510,000 square meters — about 1.7 times the footprint of its predecessor.

The complex is divided into three main zones. Block 5 houses the produce building (three stories, approximately 26 meters tall), Block 6 contains the intermediate wholesale seafood building (five stories, approximately 33 meters), and Block 7 accommodates the wholesale seafood building. All three structures are built in SRC (steel-reinforced concrete) construction, with Nikken Sekkei involved in structural engineering and multiple joint-venture contractors handling each block.

The defining engineering feature is the elevated-floor design. The site was formerly occupied by Tokyo Gas industrial facilities, and years of debate surrounded the contamination of the underlying soil. After extensive excavation and soil replacement, the buildings were raised above ground level to allow outside air to circulate beneath the floors — a continuous monitoring system controls the concentration of volatile organic compounds. The project stands as a landmark example of combining civil engineering with modern market architecture under exceptional environmental constraints.

## A Waterfront District Transformed

The Toyosu neighborhood has undergone dramatic urbanization since the 1990s. Tower-residence developments such as [Skyz Tower & Garden](/buildings/skyz-tower-and-garden/) and [Bayz Tower & Garden](/buildings/bayz-tower-and-garden/) now rise nearby, while the Sensaku-Banrai entertainment and wellness complex adjacent to the market adds tourism to the site's industrial identity. Tokyo Metro Yurakucho Line "Toyosu Station" is a short walk away.

Beyond its function as a food-supply hub, Toyosu Market has an architectural presence all its own: the exposed steel cantilevers of its elevated corridors, the vast loading bays, and the constant motion of turret trucks tracing optimized logistics routes all express a disciplined industrial beauty. For visitors curious about how a modern city feeds itself — and what that machinery looks like from the inside — the market's observation walkways offer a rare and compelling perspective.

## Summary

Opened October 2018, with a site area of approximately 40.7 hectares on reclaimed Tokyo Bay land, Toyosu Market represents Tokyo's most ambitious food-infrastructure project of the twenty-first century. Its elevated SRC structures reconcile soil remediation requirements with cutting-edge market operations, reshaping the city's waterfront in the process.
