## A New Market on a Former Gas Factory Site

On 11 October 2018, the Toyosu Market opened on a waterfront site in Koto City that had previously housed the Tokyo Gas Toyosu Works. The market is operated by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and functions as the city's central wholesale market for seafood and produce, taking over those roles from the old Tsukiji Market. The complex comprises several interconnected buildings — including the seafood wholesalers' building, the intermediate wholesale seafood building, the produce building and the administration building — arranged around shared infrastructure on a site enclosed against the outside environment. The choice of Toyosu as the relocation site was contentious and took years to finalise, but the facility has been operational since 2018 and now handles the daily supply chain that feeds much of the Tokyo metropolitan area. The location, in the bayside section of Koto City between the Odaiba waterfront and the dense inner wards, places the market within easy reach of distribution routes heading in every direction.

## Enclosed and Temperature-Controlled

The design philosophy of Toyosu Market departs sharply from that of Tsukiji, which was essentially an open-air facility protected by a roof. Toyosu is fully enclosed, with controlled temperature and hygiene zones that keep fish and produce within a consistent cold chain from arrival through to dispatch. The seafood buildings rise to five storeys and the produce building to three, giving the complex a vertical dimension unusual for a wholesale market. Elevated viewing galleries allow members of the public to observe the morning auctions from above without entering the working floor, making the market accessible as a destination in its own right. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government established the facility on reclaimed land that had been used for industrial purposes for decades, addressing contamination issues before construction.

## Summary

Toyosu Market was completed and opened in 2018 by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. Its enclosed, temperature-controlled design represents a fundamental shift in how Tokyo manages its central wholesale fish and produce trade. The waterfront setting, combined with the public viewing galleries, makes an early-morning visit a worthwhile experience — the scale of the facility and the speed of the auction floor offer a vivid picture of the logistics behind Tokyo's food supply.
