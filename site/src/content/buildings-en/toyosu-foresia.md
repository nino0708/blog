## A Shipyard Transformed

Toyosu Foresia stands one minute's walk from Toyosu Station on the Tokyo Metro Yurakucho Line, on land that was once the manufacturing heart of IHI Corporation (formerly Ishikawajima-Harima Heavy Industries). Completed on 15 July 2014, the building rises 16 floors above ground — with two basement levels and two rooftop penthouse floors — reaching a maximum height of 83.20 metres and encompassing a total floor area of 101,502 square metres. Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei designed the building and Obayashi Corporation served as general contractor. The base-isolated seismic foundation, relatively uncommon at this building scale, reflects both the site's proximity to Tokyo Bay reclaimed land and the ambition to deliver premium office space on the Toyosu waterfront.

## IHI's Legacy and the New Toyosu

From the Edo period onwards, the Ishikawajima area of Toyosu was a centre of Japanese shipbuilding. When IHI wound down its heavy industrial operations here during the 1990s, a large swathe of industrial waterfront became available for redevelopment. Mitsubishi Estate, working through a special-purpose company alongside IHI as landowner, delivered the first phase as Toyosu Front in 2010, followed by Toyosu Foresia in 2014. The name "Foresia" blends the English word "forest" with a phonetic echo of Toyosu, signalling the building's 44 percent site-greening ratio — among the highest in the area. The standard floor plate of approximately 4,579 square metres is one of the largest in Toyosu, designed to accommodate major tenants seeking wide, column-free open-plan layouts.

## Summary

Toyosu Foresia was completed in 2014 with 16 floors and a height of 83.20 metres on the former IHI industrial site in Koto ward. The building represents the second phase of a redevelopment series that has been steadily converting Toyosu's decommissioned shipyards into a modern waterfront business district. Arriving at Toyosu Station and walking north toward the canal, it is easy to trace the quiet transformation from heavy industry to green office campus — a journey that takes only a few minutes on foot but spans decades of urban change.
