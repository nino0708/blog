## Twin Pylons at the Mouth of Nagasaki Harbour

Nagasaki Megami Bridge — nicknamed "Venus Wing" — crosses the mouth of Nagasaki Harbour in Nagasaki City, Nagasaki Prefecture. The bridge was completed on 11 December 2005 as a three-span continuous steel cable-stayed bridge. Its main bridge section is 880 meters long, with a central span of 480 meters — the sixth-longest among Japanese cable-stayed bridges at the time of completion. Two steel pylons, each 170 meters tall, rise on the eastern and western shores of the harbour entrance, and cables fan out symmetrically from both pylons to support the deck. Total road length including approach structures is 1,289 meters. The deck clears the sea surface by 65 meters, a height chosen to allow large cruise ships to pass beneath the bridge on their way in and out of Nagasaki Harbour.

## Connecting a Port City's Shores

Before the bridge opened, the journey by road from one side of the harbour mouth to the other required a significant detour inland. Megami Bridge resolved that gap in the network, linking the western districts of Nagasaki City more directly to the city center and dramatically reducing travel time for residents on both shores. As a port city that has played a central role in Japan's international trade since the Edo period, Nagasaki had a particular practical need for a crossing that could accommodate large ocean-going vessels without restricting harbour access.

The engineering quality of the design was recognized with the Japan Society of Civil Engineers' Tanaka Award for 2005, one of the most prestigious honors in Japanese civil engineering. At night, the lit pylons and cable arrays add a sculptural dimension to Nagasaki's famous harbor skyline. For those tracing the arc of modern bridge engineering across Kyushu, [Wakato Bridge](/buildings/wakato-bridge/) and [Kanmon Bridge](/buildings/kanmon-bridge/) provide useful comparisons from earlier decades.

## Summary

Nagasaki Megami Bridge was completed in December 2005: a 880-meter cable-stayed bridge with 170-meter pylons and a 65-meter navigational clearance that keeps Nagasaki Harbour open to large ships. Its nickname "Venus Wing" captures the elegant spread of its cable arrays, which light up the harbour entrance after dark. A visit to Nagasaki is a natural occasion to see this bridge alongside the city's historic port landscape.
