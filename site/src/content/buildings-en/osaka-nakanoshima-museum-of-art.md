## The Black Box on Nakanoshima

On February 2, 2022, the Osaka Nakanoshima Museum of Art opened on the eastern tip of Nakanoshima island, near where the Okawa and Tosabori rivers converge. Designed by Katsuhiko Endo Architects, the building is a five-story, 39-meter-tall matte-black rectangular volume with a total floor area of 17,305 square meters. Its uncompromising geometry stands in deliberate contrast to Nakanoshima's neoclassical neighbors—the Osaka Central Public Hall and the city's waterside civic buildings built during the Meiji and Taisho eras.

The collection spans modern and contemporary art and design from the Meiji period to the present, with notable holdings by Saeki Yuzo and other Western-influenced Japanese painters.

## Completing a Century-Long Cultural Axis

Nakanoshima has been the civic and cultural heart of Osaka since the Meiji era, but lacked a comprehensive museum dedicated to modern Japanese art. The museum's opening filled that gap, providing for the first time a systematic public home for Osaka's accumulated collections.

The building's ground and third floors are designed as publicly accessible "pass-through" spaces, lowering the threshold for visitors who may not have come specifically for the galleries. An escalator spine running vertically through the full height of the building gives a kinetic sense of the structure's 39-meter section.

## Summary

The Osaka Nakanoshima Museum of Art, completed in 2022 at five stories and 39.05 meters tall with a floor area of 17,305 square meters, is Osaka's first comprehensive modern-art museum. Designed by Katsuhiko Endo, its black mass anchors the east end of Nakanoshima with unmistakable presence. Walk the river promenade to see the full volume reflected in the Okawa on a calm morning.
