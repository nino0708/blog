## The Black Box on the Water

Nakanoshima is a narrow strip of land between the Dojima and Tosabori rivers in central Osaka. Near the island's western end, a dark rectangular building sits on low supports above a wide plaza, its surfaces finished in black precast concrete panels. This is the Osaka Museum of Art, Nakanoshima, designed by Katsuhiko Endo Architects and opened on 2 February 2022.

The building stands five floors above ground and reaches a height of approximately 39 metres. Total floor area is about 20,012 square metres. The structure is steel-framed with base isolation. What strikes most visitors first is the colour and the apparent weight: a black box, lifted off the ground, solid against the sky and the river light. A passage runs around the perimeter at ground level, free to walk through, so the building touches the city without enclosing it.

The museum holds more than 6,000 works, including European modernist paintings alongside twentieth-century Japanese art. The collection took shape over roughly four decades during which the city of Osaka accumulated works while planning for a permanent home. The building finally gives that collection a fixed address. Underground, an art plaza is open without charge, providing space for the public regardless of whether a ticketed exhibition is running above.

Directly adjacent stands the National Museum of Art, Osaka — a building that descends underground from a sculptural steel frame at street level. The two institutions share the western end of Nakanoshima, turning the area into a walkable cluster for contemporary and modern art in a city better known abroad for its food.

## Summary

The Osaka Museum of Art, Nakanoshima opened in 2022, five floors, designed by Katsuhiko Endo Architects, with a collection forty years in the making now in a permanent home. The black form elevated above Nakanoshima's waterside plaza is striking in any light. When visiting Osaka, combine it with the National Museum of Art next door, or pair with [Grand Front Osaka](/buildings/grand-front-osaka) for a fuller picture of how the city has built into its centre over the past two decades.
