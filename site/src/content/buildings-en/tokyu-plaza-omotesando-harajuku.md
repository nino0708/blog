## A Kaleidoscope at the Corner of Omotesando and Harajuku

Tokyu Plaza Omotesando Harajuku opened on 18 April 2012 on the corner lot at 4-30-3 Jingumae, Shibuya Ward — where Omotesando and Harajuku meet. The building is seven above-ground floors and two basement floors with a gross floor area of 11,855 square metres, developed by Tokyu Fudosan and designed by Hiroshi Nakamura of NAP Architects. Both JR Harajuku Station and Tokyo Metro Omotesando Station are within easy walking distance.

The building's design language starts at street level. The entrance atrium is lined entirely with mirrors arranged in a kaleidoscope pattern, so that every surface reflects every other. Visitors entering the building pass through a corridor of multiplied light and geometry before arriving at the retail floors above. The effect drew architecture writers when the building opened and has remained one of the most photographed interiors in the Omotesando area. The facade above the entrance takes a more restrained approach: horizontal bands of timber louvres and greenery that respond to the zelkova tree-lined boulevard nearby.

## From Opening Day to the Omokado Relaunch

In April 2024, the building relaunched under the name Omokado — a compressed form of "Omotesando no kado" (the corner of Omotesando). The renaming reflected a redesigned tenant mix and zone layout, while retaining the kaleidoscope entrance and the rooftop terrace known as Omohara no Mori (Forest of Omohara). The architectural experience that made the original building a point of interest in the area was carried over into the new version.

The Omotesando–Harajuku corridor has been one of Tokyo's densest concentrations of flagship stores and architecturally distinctive commercial buildings since the 1990s. Omotesando Hills (2006) and several brand flagship buildings along the boulevard are among its better-known landmarks. Tokyu Plaza Omotesando Harajuku claimed its own place in that setting through the entrance more than through scale: a seven-floor building that makes itself known through its front door.

## Summary

Tokyu Plaza Omotesando Harajuku opened in 2012 as a seven-floor commercial building designed by Hiroshi Nakamura of NAP Architects. Its mirrored kaleidoscope entrance, 11,855 square metres of retail space, and rooftop garden gave it a distinct architectural identity on one of Tokyo's best-known shopping streets. In April 2024 the building was relaunched as Omokado, refreshing its tenancy while keeping the architecture that made it recognisable.
