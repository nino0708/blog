## Six Bridges Across the Inland Sea

The Nishi-Seto Expressway — widely known as the Shimanami Kaidō — runs 46.6 kilometers from Onomichi City in Hiroshima Prefecture to Imabari City in Ehime Prefecture, crossing the Seto Inland Sea via six bridge structures. It was developed by the Honshu-Shikoku Bridge Expressway Authority and reached full operation on April 29, 2006. Construction began in 1973, and the first bridge to open was the Ōshima Bridge in 1979; the route was completed over a span of twenty-seven years, with individual bridges opening in stages.

The six bridges — Innoshima Bridge, Ikuchijima Bridge, Tatara Bridge, Ōshima Bridge, Hakata-Ōshima Bridge, and Kurushima-Kaikyō Bridge — each span a different island-to-island gap, threading through Innoshima, Ikuchijima, Ōshima, Hakatajima, and Ōmishima. Each bridge uses a different structural form: cable-stayed, suspension, or truss. Tatara Bridge, with its cable-stayed main span of 890 meters, held the world record for cable-stayed spans at the time of its opening in 1999. Kurushima-Kaikyō Bridge, a triple-suspension-bridge chain stretching over 4,100 meters, is among the longest series of bridges in Japan.

## A World-Class Cycling Route

What sets the Nishi-Seto Expressway apart from every other expressway in Japan is that cyclists and pedestrians can traverse virtually its entire length. A dedicated cycling lane — in places sharing a road shoulder, in others using a separate path — runs for roughly 70 kilometers through the route, allowing riders to descend from each bridge onto the islands and continue to the next. The scenery changes dramatically from bridge to bridge: lemon groves on Ikuchijima, art installations on Ōshima, the tidal rapids visible from Kurushima. This combination of engineering spectacle and natural landscape has made the Shimanami Kaidō one of the most internationally recognized cycling destinations in Asia.

## Summary

The Nishi-Seto Expressway completed its full 46.6-kilometer alignment in April 2006, connecting Hiroshima and Ehime prefectures across the Seto Inland Sea through six bridges. Developed by the Honshu-Shikoku Bridge Expressway Authority, it carries freight and passengers while also offering a rare cycling and walking route through one of Japan's most beautiful maritime landscapes. Whether crossing by car or by bicycle, the Shimanami Kaidō offers a view of the sea, the bridges, and the islands that no other road in Japan can match.
