## A Tower on the Hill

Higashiyama Sky Tower rises 134 metres from a ridge inside Higashiyama Park, Chikusa Ward, Nagoya — built not on flat city ground but atop a wooded hill. Completed in 1989 to mark the centennial of Nagoya's municipal government, the tower reaches an effective elevation of about 214 metres above sea level, giving its observation deck a panorama that rivals far taller structures in the city centre. The city of Nagoya served as developer; design was handled by the Nagoya City Architecture Bureau and Nihon Sogō Kenchiku Jimusho Nagoya Branch.

The observation deck sits at 100 metres above ground. On a clear day, visitors can see the entire Nagoya plain and, beyond it, Ise Bay. The structure also serves as a broadcast relay station and an emergency radio relay node, blending visitor amenity with essential urban infrastructure.

## Nightscape Heritage

In 2004, Higashiyama Sky Tower received designation as a Japan Night View Heritage site. The recognition reflects the particular quality of a view from a wooded hilltop: the grid-street pattern of Nagoya's city centre turns into a carpet of light at night, while the trees of Higashiyama Zoological and Botanical Garden — opened in 1937 — disappear into darkness around the tower's base. The contrast between the illuminated city and the surrounding natural darkness is precisely what distinguishes the experience here from that of a tower in a dense downtown.

Combining the tower visit with a walk through Higashiyama Zoological and Botanical Garden makes for an unusually layered half-day outing in Nagoya, mixing urban panorama with green landscape.

## Summary

Higashiyama Sky Tower was completed in 1989, stands 134 metres tall, and rises from the ridge in Higashiyama Park. It combines observation deck, broadcast relay station, and disaster-prevention radio functions in a single structure.
