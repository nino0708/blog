## An Arched Roof Rises from Tokyo Bay's Edge

In the Tatsumi Seaside Park along the shores of Tokyo Bay, the Tokyo Aquatics Centre completed in 2020 stands as one of the city's most striking post-Olympic landmarks. The building rises four floors above ground with one below, its roof reaching approximately 37 metres at its highest point. That roof is the structure's defining feature: rather than assembling it in place at height, engineers built the entire arched frame on the ground and lifted it into position using hydraulic jacks — a construction technique that prioritised both safety and precision. The total construction cost came to approximately 56.7 billion yen. The basic design was handled by Yamashita Sekkei, with Obayashi Corporation leading the construction joint venture.

Three competition pools — a 50-metre main pool for swimming and water polo, plus a diving pool — are housed beneath a single roof, allowing multiple disciplines to share the same facility simultaneously. The 50-metre pool meets FINA international standards with 10 lanes, and deep blue tiles lend it the visual depth familiar from broadcast television.

## Built to Shrink: Planning for the Post-Games Era

During the Tokyo 2020 Games, the centre hosted swimming, water polo, artistic swimming, and diving events, accommodating approximately 15,000 spectators in its temporary-and-permanent hybrid seating layout. What set the facility apart in design terms was that the reduction had been planned from the outset: temporary stands were designed to be dismantled after the Games, allowing the facility to convert to a 5,000-seat venue suited to regular competitions and public use.

This philosophy — building for two scales at once — was a deliberate response to criticism that Olympic venues often become expensive underused shells. The centre now operates as a base for domestic and international aquatics competitions alongside everyday swimming programmes for local residents, set within the green landscape of Tatsumi Seaside Park.

## Summary

The Tokyo Aquatics Centre was completed in 2020, with four floors above ground and a roof height of approximately 37 metres. Built as the principal aquatics venue for the Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games, it has since been converted to a 5,000-seat facility that serves both elite competition and public swimming. A walk through Tatsumi Seaside Park offers a close-up view of the arched roof that sheltered some of the most memorable moments of those Games.
