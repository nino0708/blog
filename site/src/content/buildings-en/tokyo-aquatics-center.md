## An Olympic Pool at the Waterfront

The Tokyo Aquatics Centre stands in Tatsumi, Koto City, on the reclaimed land close to Tokyo Bay that has long been the city's sporting shoreline. Completed in 2020, the building rises four floors above ground and one below, reaching a height of 37 metres. Its most recognisable feature is the gently undulating roof — a broad, wave-like canopy that evokes flowing water from across the park. Designed by Yamashita Sekkei and constructed for the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, the building encloses three pools: a 50-metre competition pool, a sub pool, and a diving pool. The site adjoins Tatsumi-no-Mori Seaside Park, giving the facility a setting of greenery and sea breeze that feels unusually expansive for central Tokyo.

## From the Games to Every Swimmer

The centre was built as the main aquatics venue for the Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games, hosting swimming, water polo, and artistic swimming. After the Games closed, the building was retained as an Olympic legacy facility and opened to the general public in April 2023. It now operates on two parallel tracks: community swimming lessons and school programmes on one side, national-level competitions with international timing systems on the other. The world-class equipment installed for the Olympics — the precision lane timing, the spectator seating — transfers seamlessly to hosting domestic championships and training camps. As an example of post-Games venue reuse, the centre has found a sustainable second life that serves both elite sport and everyday recreation.

## Summary

The Tokyo Aquatics Centre was completed in 2020 — four floors above ground, one below, 37 metres tall, designed by Yamashita Sekkei. Its undulating roof marks the Tatsumi waterfront, and its three pools carry the Olympic legacy forward through daily public use. A walk through the adjacent seaside park, followed by a visit to the pool where Olympians competed, is one of the quieter pleasures east Tokyo offers.
