## A New Face for Dogenzaka

Walk out of Shibuya Station's west exit and climb a few steps up Dogenzaka, and a glass-clad tower rises on your left. Shibuya Fukuras was completed in October 2019 — 18 floors above ground, four below, standing 103.96 meters tall. Shimizu Corporation handled design and construction; Tokyu Land Corporation led development. It was built on the site of the former Tokyu Plaza Shibuya, incorporating the adjacent block into a unified redevelopment zone. The lower floors operate as a commercial facility under the brand "Tokyu Plaza Shibuya," with a succession of escalators and open terraces drawing visitors upward toward a rooftop level.

## A Transit Plaza Woven into the Building

What sets Shibuya Fukuras apart is not only the tower itself but the rethinking of pedestrian flow it brought to the station's west side. A vertically continuous circulation spine — called an "urban core" — connects underground concourse levels, the ground plane, and elevated deck routes in a single path. A multilevel transit plaza, including a bus terminal, is folded into the building's lower floors. The west exit of Shibuya Station had long been a tangle of competing routes and modes; this redevelopment organized those layers, making movement legible. While the east side of the station anchors the district's signature supertalls — Shibuya Scramble Square, Shibuya Hikarie, Shibuya Stream — Fukuras marked the parallel renewal of the Dogenzaka side.

## Summary

Shibuya Fukuras was completed in 2019, 18 floors tall, at a height of 103.96 meters. It stands as a key chapter in the reshaping of Shibuya's western approach — combining a commercial anchor with the kind of transit infrastructure that actually makes a busy station easier to use. Coming up Dogenzaka, look to your right: the building signals a west side that has quietly begun to catch up with the east.
