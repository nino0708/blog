## A Skyscraper Rising at Hiroshima Station's South Exit

Step out of Hiroshima Station's south exit and City Tower Hiroshima immediately commands attention — a 52-story, 189-meter residential tower that completed in August 2016. Developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development and built by Maeda Corporation, the 513-unit tower emerged from the Hiroshima Station South Exit Block B urban redevelopment project, which transformed a former freight and industrial zone into a high-density urban node. The tower connects directly to the underground concourse at Hiroshima Station's south exit, offering residents seamless access to the city's rail and tram network.

Its seismic-isolation structure reflects the region's awareness of earthquake risk, while south-facing upper floors offer unobstructed views across Hiroshima Bay and, on clear days, the Seto Inland Sea.

## Rewriting the Hiroshima Skyline

Before the redevelopment, the Matsubara-cho area south of Hiroshima Station was underused despite its prime location. The Station South Exit Block B project brought together Sumitomo Realty, Bic Camera, and the Hiroshima Station South Exit Redevelopment Association, delivering not just the residential tower but also improved public spaces and pedestrian connections.

When City Tower Hiroshima topped out in 2016, it was among the tallest structures in the Chugoku region, signaling a new chapter for Hiroshima's urban ambitions. The tower's sheer scale — visible from neighborhoods across the city — made it an unmistakable new landmark beside the castle and the Peace Memorial Park in defining the city's visual identity.

## Summary

Completed in August 2016 with 52 floors above ground and 2 below, City Tower Hiroshima stands 189 meters tall as one of the defining elements of modern Hiroshima's skyline. Its station-direct access and commanding views have made it a benchmark residential address in Western Japan. If you are walking Hiroshima, look up from the station plaza — the tower overhead is the clearest measure of how far this city has risen since its postwar reconstruction.
