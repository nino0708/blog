## A Marunouchi Address on the Yaesu Side

Its address reads Marunouchi 1-chome, yet the main entrance faces the Nihombashi Exit of Tokyo Station — the Yaesu side. This geographic nuance gives Marunouchi Trust City its distinctive character among the towers clustered around the station. The complex developed by Mori Trust consists of two buildings: Trust Tower N, completed in September 2003 as a 19-story, 3-basement structure reaching approximately 99.6 meters, and the Main Tower, completed in December 2008 as a 37-story, 4-basement skyscraper standing 178 meters tall. The Main Tower was designed by Yasui Architects & Engineers, Morimura Sekkei, and Toda Corporation.

The Nihombashi Exit side of Tokyo Station has historically carried a more commercial, mercantile character than the ceremonial western facade — adjacent to the trading district of Nihombashi, where goods and capital have circulated since the Edo period. Placing a luxury tower on this side of the station reflects the way the redevelopment of the broader Otemachi-Marunouchi-Yurakucho area had, by the 2000s, extended its ambition to every face of the station.

## A Luxury Hotel at the Top of the City

Floors 27 through 37 of the Main Tower are occupied by Shangri-La Hotel Tokyo, the flagship Japanese property of the Hong Kong-based luxury hospitality group, which opened in 2009. Its placement in the uppermost portion of a prime office tower follows a model that became a recurring feature of Tokyo's major redevelopments: stacking a high-end hotel atop a commercial and office base to maximize return from a prominent address. The same template appears at Toranomon Hills and elsewhere across the city.

The name Marunouchi Trust City deliberately claims the Marunouchi identity for a location that sits at the boundary between two very different faces of the station. It is a reminder that in Tokyo's densest business districts, addresses and neighborhoods overlap, bleed into one another, and resist neat categorization.

## Summary

Marunouchi Trust City — Trust Tower N (2003) and the Main Tower (2008, 37 stories, 178 meters) — is Mori Trust's contribution to the remaking of the area immediately adjacent to Tokyo Station. With Shangri-La Hotel Tokyo anchoring its upper floors, the complex sits at the intersection of corporate real estate and luxury hospitality. Exit Tokyo Station from the Nihombashi side and look for the two towers: one facing Marunouchi, one facing Yaesu, together mapping the layered geography of central Tokyo.
