## An early landmark in a changing district

Musashi-Kosugi, Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki. Before this name became synonymous with a cluster of high-rise condominiums, one tower helped set that transformation in motion. The Kosugi Tower was completed in May 2008: 49 floors above ground, 2 below, 160 metres tall, with 689 residential units. Built by Taisei Corporation with design supervision by architect Jun Mitsui, the reinforced-concrete structure was among the first high-rises to signal what Musashi-Kosugi was about to become.

For much of the twentieth century, the area around Musashi-Kosugi Station was an industrial district. As factories relocated or contracted from the 1990s onwards, their sites became development opportunities. The Kosugi Tower arrived early in that shift, giving a vertical form to what had until recently been a flat, factory-floor landscape.

## A chapter in Musashi-Kosugi's high-rise story

When The Kosugi Tower was completed in 2008, the surrounding blocks were still mid-transformation. In the years that followed, taller towers rose nearby — Park City Musashi-Kosugi Mid Sky Tower (59 floors) among them — and the district's skyline kept climbing. The Kosugi Tower belongs to the first chapter of that story, representing the moment when height arrived in Musashi-Kosugi as a new normal. Jun Mitsui's design involvement points to an ambition for architecture that went beyond mere density.

## Summary

The Kosugi Tower was completed in May 2008, with 49 floors above ground, 2 below, a height of 160 metres, and 689 residential units. Built by Taisei Corporation with design supervision by Jun Mitsui, it stands in Musashi-Kosugi as an early witness to the area's sweeping change from industrial district to high-rise residential hub. A walk around the station today, with towers now rising in every direction, gives a vivid sense of how much that change has accelerated since this building first appeared on the skyline.
