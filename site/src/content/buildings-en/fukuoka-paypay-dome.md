## Japan's First Retractable-Roof Stadium

Fukuoka PayPay Dome was completed in 1993 on reclaimed land in Jigyohama, Chuo Ward, Fukuoka City. Standing 83.96 metres tall with a diameter of 212 metres, it was the first stadium in Japan to feature a retractable roof, capable of opening to the sky on fine days or closing to provide a fully weather-proof venue. The scale of the dome — its roof panels dividing into three sections that slide open along tracks — was an engineering landmark in Japanese stadium construction.

The dome was built on a site facing Hakata Bay, on land reclaimed as part of Fukuoka's coastal development during the late 1980s. The surrounding Jigyohama area was largely undeveloped at the time of the dome's opening; the stadium's arrival helped establish the precinct as one of Fukuoka City's major commercial and entertainment destinations. The adjacent Fukuoka Tower, completed a few years earlier in 1989, had already begun attracting visitors to the bayside zone.

## The Bay Zone and Its Anchor

Over the three decades since opening, the dome has become the centrepiece of a broader mixed-use precinct that includes hotels, a shopping complex and event spaces. Its circular silhouette is unmistakable from the waterfront and from elevated expressways ringing the area. The retractable roof, once a novelty, has since been adopted by other Japanese domes, but Fukuoka's remains the first. The stadium hosts not only professional baseball but also large concerts and special events throughout the year, keeping the venue active even in the off-season.

## Summary

Fukuoka PayPay Dome was completed in 1993 at 83.96 m high and 212 m across — Japan's first stadium with a retractable roof. Its pioneering engineering and bayside location have made it a landmark of Fukuoka City for over 30 years. Walk the Jigyohama waterfront, look up at the dome's curved profile against the bay, and appreciate how a single building can define an entire city district.
