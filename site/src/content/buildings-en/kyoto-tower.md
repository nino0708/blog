## A White Candle Above the Ancient Capital

Step out of Kyoto Station's north exit and a slender white silhouette immediately pulls the eye skyward. Kyoto Tower, completed in December 1964, rises 131 metres above sea level — the tallest structure in the city. Architect Mamoru Yamada modelled the steel-tube tower on a traditional Japanese candle (wa-rōsoku), setting it atop a nine-storey reinforced concrete podium building. The tapering white curves give the tower an unmistakeable presence against the low-rise cityscape below.

The year 1964 was also the year of the Tokyo Olympics, when Japan's high-growth era was in full swing. For Kyoto — a city whose identity rests on a thousand years of history — planting a modern observation tower at its railway gateway was a deliberate statement about tourism and the future. Early critics called the design out of step with the ancient skyline, but sixty years on the tower has become an inseparable part of the station forecourt. Yamada, who also designed Japan's National Yoyogi Gymnasium and other landmark civic structures, paired the innovative steel mast with a structural concrete base that was ahead of its time.

## Three Mountain Ranges and a Grid Below

The observation deck at 100 metres frames one of Japan's most distinctive urban panoramas. Kyoto's streets still follow the checkerboard grid laid out when the capital was founded in 794, and the mountains of Higashiyama, Kitayama, and Nishiyama form a gentle arc around the city on three sides. On clear days the skyscrapers of Osaka are visible to the south; at dusk the ridgelines glow amber before the city lights of Fushimi Inari and To-ji pagoda emerge below.

Practical access is hard to beat. City buses for Kiyomizudera, Kinkakuji, and Arashiyama depart from the terminal directly beneath the tower, and Shinkansen connections to Osaka and Tokyo are a short walk away. Staying in the Kyoto Station area means you can check the day's weather from the deck before heading out, and return to the same view after dark. Compared with its contemporaries — [Fukuoka Tower](/en/buildings/fukuoka-tower/) to the west or [Kobe Port Tower](/en/buildings/kobe-port-tower/) on the coast — Kyoto's panorama is defined by a basin enclosed by hills, an effect no other Japanese city can replicate.

In April 2024, electronics manufacturer Nidec Corporation acquired naming rights, adding "Nidec Kyoto Tower" to the official name, though the white cylinder itself is unchanged.

## Summary

Completed in December 1964, Kyoto Tower is a 131-metre steel-mast observation tower designed by Mamoru Yamada, rising from a nine-storey podium in front of Kyoto Station. Its 100-metre observation deck delivers an unmatched view of the city's ancient grid framed by three mountain ranges — a perspective that captures the geographical character of the old capital at a glance. Whether you arrive by Shinkansen or local train, the tower is the perfect starting point for exploring Kyoto on foot.
