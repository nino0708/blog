## Tokyo's New Maritime Gateway

Tokyo International Cruise Terminal opened on 10 September 2020 on reclaimed land in the Aomi district of Kōtō City, part of the Rinkai Fukutoshin (Tokyo Waterfront Secondary Urban Centre). The building is four storeys tall with a total floor area of approximately 19,000 square metres. The architectural design was handled by Yasui Architects & Engineers and construction was carried out by Penta-Ocean Construction. The key specification is the berthing infrastructure: a single quay wall 430 metres in length with a water depth of 11.5 metres — dimensions that allow the world's largest ocean liners to moor directly alongside. Public transport access is provided by the Yurikamome automated guideway transit line, whose "Tokyo International Cruise Terminal" station sits directly connected to the terminal building.

The building's internal organisation prioritises the flow of arriving and departing passengers: embarkation, disembarkation, immigration processing, and baggage handling are distributed across the floors. The design engages its waterfront setting with an open, horizontally extended form. Tokyo Port had previously managed cruise ship calls at quays near Shinagawa and Hamamatsucho, but the increasing size of modern ocean liners had made those berths inadequate. The new terminal was planned as the centrepiece of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's Bureau of Port and Harbour strategy to attract more international cruise ship calls.

## A Cruise Hub in the Waterfront Secondary City

The Aomi district where the terminal stands is part of the larger Rinkai Fukutoshin development area, a belt of reclaimed land built up from the late 1980s onward. Immediately to the north stands [Tokyo Big Sight](/buildings/tokyo-big-site), one of Japan's largest exhibition halls. The area as a whole — served by the [Yurikamome](/en/railways/yurikamome/) line — mixes convention, retail, sports, and transport infrastructure. In front of the terminal the bay opens wide, and on clear days the twin-truss form of [Tokyo Gate Bridge](/buildings/tokyo-gate-bridge) is visible across the water to the southeast.

The terminal's opening coincided with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, which severely restricted cruise ship arrivals in the years that followed. As international cruising has recovered, the terminal has begun to fulfil its intended role. Passengers stepping off a large ship here encounter a 180-degree panorama of Tokyo Port — a first impression of the city quite unlike anything available from the land side. Visitors arriving by Yurikamome will find that the quayside, with its broad horizon and occasional presence of very large ships, makes a worthwhile destination in its own right.

## Summary

Tokyo International Cruise Terminal opened 10 September 2020 and covers approximately 19,000 square metres across four floors. Its 430-metre quay wall at a depth of 11.5 metres accommodates the world's largest cruise vessels, and a dedicated Yurikamome station provides direct rail access. Designed by Yasui Architects & Engineers and built by Penta-Ocean Construction, the terminal brings Tokyo Port a maritime gateway scaled to the demands of contemporary international cruising — and adds one more reason to ride the Yurikamome out to the waterfront.
