## The Inverted Pyramid of Tokyo Bay

Tokyo Big Sight—formally the Tokyo International Exhibition Center—was completed in October 1995 and opened to the public in April 1996 on reclaimed land in the Ariake area of Koto City. Its instantly recognizable conference tower, an inverted pyramid rising eight floors above one basement level to a height of 58 m, was assembled on the ground and then jacked into position, one of the more striking construction feats of its era. Designed by AXS Satow, the complex encompasses the east, west, and south exhibition halls as well as the conference tower, with a combined total floor area of 316,990 m²—consistently among the largest exhibition venues in Japan.

## Hub of Tokyo's Trade-Show Culture

The Ariake site was developed as part of Tokyo's broader waterfront reclamation project launched in the 1980s. The venue is served directly by the Rinkai Line (Kokusai-Tenjijo Station) and closely by the Yurikamome monorail (Tokyo Big Sight Station), giving it strong transit access from central Tokyo. It has hosted everything from automobile shows and technology expos to Comiket, the world's largest self-published comic fair, which turns the entire complex into a bustling community space twice each year. During the 2020 Tokyo Olympics, the halls served as the main press and broadcasting center. The combination of sheer scale, flexible hall configurations, and waterfront setting has made Tokyo Big Sight a cornerstone of Tokyo's MICE infrastructure.

## Summary

Completed in 1995 and covering 316,990 m² across eight exhibition and conference floors, Tokyo Big Sight remains Japan's premier international exhibition venue. The inverted-pyramid conference tower is a landmark visible from across the bay—and reason enough to make the trip to Ariake even when no show is on.
