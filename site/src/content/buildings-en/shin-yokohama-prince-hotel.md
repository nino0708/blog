## A Cylindrical Tower by the Shinkansen: Shin-Yokohama, 1992

Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel rises 149 metres over Shin-Yokohama 3-chome, Kohoku Ward, Yokohama, a few minutes' walk from JR Tokaido Shinkansen "Shin-Yokohama Station." The hotel opened in March 1992, with the design carried out by the in-house design division of Shimizu Corporation for the developer Seibu Railway's Prince Hotels brand.

The building is 42 floors above ground and 3 below, with 904 guest rooms. Its near-cylindrical form presents an identical profile from every approach direction — a formal choice that suits the hotel's role as a 360-degree urban landmark at the core of Shin-Yokohama's station precinct. Guest rooms run along the building's full circumference at each floor, and the upper storeys offer panoramic views of Yokohama's Kohoku hills, the Tsurumi River basin, and on clear days Mount Fuji to the west.

The project belongs to a wave of high-rise urban hotels developed by Seibu Railway across the Tokyo metropolitan area in the late 1980s and early 1990s — a strategy that paired the Prince Hotels brand with major transit hubs to capture business and event travellers arriving by rail. The Shin-Yokohama site, anchored by the Shinkansen, was a natural fit: the station had been Yokohama's northern gateway since the Tokaido Shinkansen began running in 1964, and the hotel gave visitors a prominent destination directly at the platform exit.

## New Yokohama: Station District to Sports Hub

When the hotel opened, the Shin-Yokohama district was already a planned urban node, but its development was still accelerating. [Nissan Stadium](/buildings/nissan-stadium/) (opened 1998, capacity 72,327) would rise roughly one kilometre to the east in the following years, drawing millions of event visitors and reinforcing the area's status as a major destination. The hotel's proximity to the stadium made it the natural accommodation anchor for World Cup and J.League crowds alike.

[Yokohama Landmark Tower](/buildings/yokohama-landmark-tower/), the city's tallest building at 296 metres, stands approximately eight kilometres to the south in Minato Mirai. Where Landmark Tower defines Yokohama's harbour skyline, Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel anchors the city's northern interior. Seen from the upper floors at dusk, the two towers mark the two ends of Yokohama's urban geography: port and railway, waterfront and inland.

## Summary

Completed March 1992, 42 floors, 149 metres tall, 904 rooms. Designed by Shimizu Corporation's design division for Prince Hotels, Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel's cylindrical tower remains the defining landmark of Yokohama's Shinkansen gateway district, three decades after its opening.
