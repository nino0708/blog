## A Tower by the Shinkansen

Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel stands approximately 150 metres tall in Kohoku Ward, Yokohama — 42 storeys above ground and 3 below. Completed in March 1992, it was developed by Seibu Properties and opened with 904 guest rooms, a scale that positions it as a major convention and event hotel rather than simply a city accommodation. The site is a short walk from Shin-Yokohama Station, served by the Tokaido Shinkansen, giving it immediate access to travellers arriving from Tokyo, Nagoya, and Osaka.

The 904 rooms have made it a natural host for large-scale events. Nissan Stadium (Yokohama International Stadium) is also nearby in the Shin-Yokohama district, and international sporting competitions held there — rugby world cup matches, football tournaments — regularly bring competitors, officials, and spectators to this area, with the hotel absorbing a significant share.

## The Making of Shin-Yokohama

Shin-Yokohama as an urban place is essentially a creation of the Shinkansen era. The station opened in 1964 when the Tokaido Shinkansen began operating, but the district around it remained mostly underdeveloped until the 1980s and 1990s, when office buildings, hotels, and commercial facilities filled in rapidly. Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel's 1992 completion coincided with this period of maturation, adding a prominent high-rise to what had become a recognisable sub-centre.

Today the area benefits from a further connection: the Tokyu Shin-Yokohama Line, opened in 2023, links Shin-Yokohama to the Tokyu Toyoko Line and directly to Shibuya, reinforcing the district's role as a transit hub.

Shin-Yokohama Prince Hotel was completed in 1992 with 42 floors, a height of approximately 150 metres, and 904 guest rooms — large enough to function as a convention centre hotel. Its location next to the Shinkansen station has made it a consistent anchor for events in the Shin-Yokohama district.
