## Born Beside Kansai Airport

On the reclaimed shores of Osaka Bay in Izumisano City, Osaka Prefecture, the Rinku Gate Tower Building was completed in August 1996 — the same year Kansai International Airport opened on the artificial island just across the water. The tower reaches 256 metres across 56 storeys above ground and two below, designed jointly by Nikken Sekkei and Yasui Architects & Engineers. Construction began in August 1992 and took four years to complete. It stands directly connected to Rinku Town Station, where both JR and Nankai Railway lines stop, linking the airport and the urban mainland through a single gateway.

The building is vertically zoned by use. The lower floors contain an international conference centre; the middle section is given to office space; the upper storeys house a hotel. The glass curtain wall reflects the blue of Osaka Bay and the airport on clear days, and glows white against the water at night. Two observation areas on the 26th and 54th floors offer panoramic views across the bay.

## A Landmark of Rinku Town

Rinku Town, where the tower stands, was developed as part of the broader planning effort that accompanied Kansai Airport from the 1980s onwards — a new urban district built on reclaimed land with the airport island visible to the east and the open waters of Osaka Bay to the west. The tower occupies a position near the foot of Rinku Large Bridge, the span that connects the mainland to the airport island. Its 256-metre silhouette between sea and sky has become the defining image of the district.

Rinku Premium Outlets and logistics facilities line the surrounding streets, and the area has established itself as a convenient hub for international visitors: Kansai Airport is less than ten minutes away by Nankai Railway, with direct trains continuing to Namba and beyond.

## Summary

The Rinku Gate Tower Building was completed in August 1996: 56 storeys, 256 metres tall, integrating a conference centre, offices and a hotel in one structure on the edge of Osaka Bay. It was conceived as the centrepiece of Rinku Town, a planned development that grew alongside Kansai International Airport, and remains the area's most recognisable landmark nearly three decades on.
