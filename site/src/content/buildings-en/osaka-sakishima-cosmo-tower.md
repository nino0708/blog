## A Tower at the Edge of Osaka Bay

On the reclaimed island of Sakishima, in the Nanko Cosmosquare district of Suminoe Ward, Osaka, a single slender tower rises 256 meters from the waterfront. The Osaka Prefectural Government Sakishima Building — popularly known as Sakishima Cosmo Tower — was completed in 1995 to designs by Nikken Sekkei and Mancini Duffy Associates. Construction began in March 1991 and the 55-story, 3-basement-floor building was completed four years later, with a total floor area of approximately 149,296 square meters. At the time of completion, it was the tallest building in the Kinki region. Its 55th-floor observation deck offers a panoramic view of Osaka Bay, Awaji Island, and Kansai International Airport. The surrounding island is largely open and low-rise, which makes the tower's presence all the more striking — there is little here to compete with it for the eye's attention.

## From WTC to Prefectural Office

The building's history carries the weight of an era. When it opened, it bore the name Osaka World Trade Center Building (Osaka WTC), conceived during the bubble economy as a hub for international trade and business on the Nanko waterfront. The project was carried out by a public-private entity led by Osaka City, and reflected an ambitious vision for Sakishima as a new global business district facing Kansai International Airport across the bay. When the bubble collapsed, the operating company accumulated unsustainable debt and fell into bankruptcy. In June 2010, the building was transferred to Osaka Prefecture and repurposed as a prefectural government office — a practical resolution that preserved one of the city's most distinctive towers while retiring its original commercial ambitions. This trajectory, from speculative international hub to government building, makes it one of the most tangible remnants of Japan's bubble-era urban vision.

## Summary

Osaka Prefectural Government Sakishima Building was completed in 1995 — 55 floors above ground, 3 below, 256 meters tall. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and Mancini Duffy Associates, it opened as the Osaka WTC before being transferred to Osaka Prefecture in 2010. The 55th-floor observatory remains open to the public, offering views stretching to Awaji Island and Kansai International Airport on a clear day. The building is a short walk from Cosmosquare Station in Nanko and makes for a rewarding visit — both as a vantage point over Osaka Bay and as a reminder of the extraordinary scale of Japan's bubble-era ambitions.
