## The Opening Move of a City's Reinvention

Tenjin Business Center opened in September 2021 on Tenjin 1-chome, Chuo Ward, Fukuoka — the absolute center of Kyushu's largest commercial district. The building rises 19 stories and two basement levels to a height of 89 m, with a total floor area of 61,100 m². Developed by Fukuoka Jisho and designed by Nihon Sekkei, its façade pairs glass and metal panels in an ordered, contemporary grid that holds its own against the layered retail and office streetscape of central Tenjin. The building's height comfortably clears the older stock it replaces, introducing a scale to this corner of the district that signals a broader transformation to come. It is a building that works as architecture and as a symbol: the deliberate first move in a larger urban chess game.

## Tenjin Big Bang and the Bet on Fukuoka's Future

In 2015, Fukuoka City launched the Tenjin Big Bang initiative — a framework using floor-area-ratio incentives and regulatory flexibility to encourage the comprehensive renewal of the central Tenjin district by 2024. The city's reasoning was explicit: its older office stock was lagging behind the demands of global business, and Fukuoka's ambition to serve as a gateway between Japan and the rest of Asia required a physically competitive downtown. Tenjin Business Center was designated the initiative's lead project, the proof of concept that could unlock momentum for the rest. Its 61,100 m² of leasable office space was designed to attract IT companies, startups, and international firms seeking a foothold in a city with direct flight connections across East and Southeast Asia. The integration of Tenjin's underground shopping arcade and surface transit network around the building ensures pedestrian flow between the new tower and the district's established retail streets.

## Summary

Completed in September 2021, 19 floors, 89 m — Tenjin Business Center is the anchor project of Fukuoka's Tenjin Big Bang renewal program, delivering 61,100 m² of modern office space in the heart of Kyushu's leading commercial center. Walk through Tenjin today and the building reads as a marker: this is where the city decided to bet on its own future.
