## A Grid of Light at the Heart of Tenjin

In Chuo Ward, Fukuoka, within walking distance of Nishitetsu Fukuoka (Tenjin) Station and the Tenjin underground shopping arcade, a 19-story office and retail complex completed in 2021 brought an unmistakable new presence to Kyushu's largest commercial district. Tenjin Business Center was developed by Fukuoka Jisho and designed jointly by Nihon Sekkei and OMA partner Shohei Shigematsu. Its façade — a deep geometric grid that filters and reflects daylight across the building's skin — stands out in a streetscape otherwise defined by postwar commercial architecture and department stores.

The building rises 19 floors above ground with two basement levels and a gross floor area of approximately 61,100 square metres. The lower floors include commercial and dining tenants that activate the street; upper floors provide next-generation office space equipped with high-speed communications infrastructure.

## The Tenjin Big Bang and a City Rewriting Its Skyline

Tenjin Business Center is the lead project of the Tenjin Big Bang, a city-led initiative that Fukuoka launched in 2015 to redevelop more than thirty buildings in the Tenjin district. The scheme exploits a revision to height restrictions related to the flight paths of Fukuoka Airport, which sits unusually close to the city centre, historically keeping much of Tenjin lower than comparable districts in other major Japanese cities.

By completing ahead of the main wave of Tenjin Big Bang projects, the building set a design and functional benchmark for what followed. It brought international architectural profile to a local developer's project and signalled that Fukuoka — long characterised as Japan's most livable mid-size city — was competing on a different level for global business and talent.

## Summary

Completed in 2021 at 19 floors above ground and roughly 61,100 square metres of floor area, Tenjin Business Center is both a striking piece of architecture and an urban catalyst. The geometric façade by OMA and Nihon Sekkei gives Fukuoka a building that reads as confidently international while staying rooted in its street context. Walk through Tenjin on a sunny afternoon and watch the light play across the grid — it is the most visible sign of the city's ongoing transformation.
