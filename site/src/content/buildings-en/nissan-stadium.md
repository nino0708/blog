## Japan's Largest Stadium: 72,327 Seats on the Tsurumi Riverbank

Nissan Stadium — officially the Yokohama International Stadium — stands in Kozukue-cho, Kohoku Ward, Yokohama, on a broad floodplain bordered by the Tsurumi River and the hills of northwestern Yokohama. The City of Yokohama developed the project, and the stadium opened in March 1998.

The building covers a total floor area of approximately 118,000 square meters. Its oval plan is ringed by a four-tier stand that encloses the field on all sides, with a sweeping canopy roof sheltering the majority of the 72,327 seats from rain. From the playing surface to the top of the uppermost tier, the structure rises roughly 60 meters — a silhouette visible from considerable distance across the flat riverside terrain.

The venue entered the global spotlight in June 2002, when it hosted the FIFA World Cup Final between Brazil and Germany. As Japan's largest stadium, it was the natural choice for the tournament's climax, and the match drew the full house of over 69,000 spectators.

## A Stadium Between City and Nature

Kozukue-cho occupies a transitional position in Yokohama's geography: immediately east of the stadium lies Kohoku New Town, one of Japan's largest planned residential developments; to the west, the Tsurumi River corridor preserves a strip of greenery and agricultural land within the metropolitan fabric. JR Yokohama Line "Shin-Yokohama Station" and the adjacent "Kozukue Station" handle the tens of thousands of visitors arriving on match and event days.

[Yokohama Landmark Tower](/buildings/yokohama-landmark-tower/) and [Yokohama Bay Bridge](/buildings/yokohama-bay-bridge/), two of the city's most recognisable structures, stand in the Minato Mirai district some eight kilometres to the south. Where those landmarks define Yokohama's harbour face, Nissan Stadium defines its northern inland character — a civic-scale venue embedded in a river landscape rather than a bay.

## Summary

Opened March 1998, 72,327 seats, total floor area approximately 118,000 square meters. Nissan Stadium is the product of Yokohama's investment in world-class sporting infrastructure, and its role as the 2002 FIFA World Cup Final venue cemented its place in both Japanese and international architectural memory. The upper concourse offers views across the Tsurumi River basin — a reminder that even the largest arenas can carry a sense of place.
