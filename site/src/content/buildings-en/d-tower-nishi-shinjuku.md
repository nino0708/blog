## A Mixed-Use Tower at the Corner of Ome-Kaido

D Tower Nishi-Shinjuku rose at the corner of Ome-Kaido Avenue and Koen-dori in Nishi-Shinjuku 6-chome, completing in April 2020 as a 29-story, approximately 132-meter tower with a total floor area of roughly 39,461 m². Developed by Daiwa House Industry, the building packs office space, a 126-unit serviced apartment wing, retail floors, and a daycare facility into one structure — an approach that makes work-life proximity tangible in the heart of Tokyo's busiest business district.

The serviced apartment component distinguishes the tower from a standard office high-rise. Designed to accommodate both domestic and international long-stay residents, each unit comes fully furnished with kitchen appliances, furniture, and home electronics. The office floors, with a standard-floor area of about 307 tsubo (roughly 1,015 m²), are designed for large-scale tenants; seismic isolation and emergency generators address business continuity. A daycare facility on-site signals the developer's intent to serve working parents. The building underwent a renovation in 2023, reflecting the tower's ongoing adaptability to changing tenant needs.

## Part of the West Shinjuku Skyline

West Shinjuku has hosted Japan's earliest cluster of skyscrapers since the 1970s subcenter development plan, and D Tower has taken a measured place in that lineage. Its 29-story height is modest compared to the 50-plus-floor towers nearby, yet its programmatic density compensates: a rare quality among landmark-seeking high-rises. The plain, functional facade focuses attention on what happens inside rather than on external spectacle.

## Summary

Completed in April 2020, D Tower Nishi-Shinjuku is a 29-story, approximately 132-meter mixed-use tower by Daiwa House Industry. It combines office, serviced apartment, retail, and childcare uses in one building — a compact model of urban life well suited to contemporary Tokyo. Walking along Ome-Kaido in west Shinjuku, you will find this tower quietly anchoring a corner where everyday life and urban infrastructure converge.
