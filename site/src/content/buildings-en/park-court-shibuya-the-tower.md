## A High-Rise in the Backstreets of Shibuya

At 1-12 Udagawacho, Shibuya Ward, a 39-story residential tower was completed in July 2020. Park Court Shibuya The Tower stands 142.69 metres tall and occupies a site just removed from the main axis of Shibuya's landmark redevelopments — not the station forecourt, but the quieter network of lanes that runs behind it.

Shibuya has been one of Tokyo's most intensely remade districts over the past decade. [Shibuya Scramble Square](/en/buildings/shibuya-scramble-square/), [Shibuya Hikarie](/en/buildings/shibuya-hikarie/), and [Shibuya Stream](/en/buildings/shibuya-stream/) have dominated headlines as office-commercial hybrids tied directly to the station. Udagawacho, by contrast, retains some of the older restaurant alleys and small bars that once characterized the neighbourhood. Park Court Shibuya The Tower's 39 stories rising here signal how far Shibuya's transformation has spread.

## Fixed-Term Leasehold as a Supply Mechanism

The project uses a fixed-term leasehold structure, under which residents acquire a right of use for a defined period rather than purchasing the underlying land. This approach allows residential supply in areas where land values are exceptionally high — Shibuya being a prime example — and has become a recognised tool in Japanese urban housing development.

Construction was carried out by Tokyu Construction, a natural pairing given Tokyu's deep historical ties to Shibuya. The building was designed by Nihon Sekkei and architect Hiroaki Hoshino (Hosino Architects), whose collaboration shaped the tower's distinctive facade. The 505-unit building runs from four basement floors to the 39th floor above grade, combining commercial and office space in the lower levels with residential floors above.

## Summary

Park Court Shibuya The Tower, completed in July 2020, stands 39 stories (four basement levels) and 142.69 metres in Udagawacho, Shibuya Ward. Developed by Mitsui Fudosan Residential using a fixed-term leasehold model, it represents a different mode of urban residential supply from the station-area mega-developments nearby.

Walking from the station toward Udagawacho, the tower appears unexpectedly at the end of a lane — a quiet reminder that Shibuya's renewal extends well beyond the grand redevelopment projects at its centre. See also: [Shibuya Fukuras](/en/buildings/shibuya-fukuras/).
