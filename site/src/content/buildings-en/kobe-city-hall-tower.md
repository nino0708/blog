## A 132-Metre Administration Tower Above Sannomiya

Kobe City Hall Tower (No. 1 Building) was completed in 1989 at Kanotecho 6-5-1, Chuo Ward, Kobe. The building rises 30 floors above ground and 3 floors below, reaching a height of 132 metres. Designed by Nikken Sekkei, the slender tower stands in the core of Sannomiya — Kobe's central business district — where it commands views northward to the Rokko mountain range and southward across Kobe Port and Osaka Bay.

The 24th floor houses a public observation lobby open free of charge to anyone who visits. On clear days the view extends to the ridge of Awaji Island. The lobby has become a well-regarded free viewpoint in the city, drawing both residents on routine visits to city offices and visitors who come specifically for the panorama of Kobe's compressed geography between mountain and sea.

## Continuity Through the 1995 Earthquake

The Great Hanshin Earthquake of January 1995 caused severe destruction across Kobe. Kobe City Hall Tower remained operational through the disaster and served as the operational centre for the city's recovery: the mayor's office, urban planning departments, finance, and welfare — the administrative functions at the core of reconstruction — continued operating from the tower throughout the years that followed.

More than 35 years after completion the building remains in active service as the city's main administration tower. At 132 metres it stands as one of the taller civic buildings in the Kinki region, and its vertical silhouette anchors the horizontal city — stretched between mountain and sea — with a clear point of civic reference.

## Summary

Completed in 1989 and reaching 132 metres across 30 floors, Kobe City Hall Tower was designed by Nikken Sekkei and has served Kobe's municipal government through decades of change, including the reconstruction from the 1995 earthquake. The free observation lobby on the 24th floor is among the most accessible elevated views in western Japan — take the lift, look south toward the port, and the reason Kobe's geography has drawn visitors for 150 years becomes immediately clear.
