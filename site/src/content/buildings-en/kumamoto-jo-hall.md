## A Convention Hall at the Foot of Kumamoto Castle

Kumamoto-Jo Hall opened in December 2019 in Sakuracho, Chuo Ward — directly east of Kumamoto Castle, the city's defining landmark. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and completed in September of that year, the building is a four-level convention complex with a gross floor area of approximately 30,000 square meters. Its main hall seats 2,300 people, and the full facility can host conventions of up to 3,000 delegates under one roof.

The hall forms the public-facility core of Sakuramachi Kumamoto, a mixed-use redevelopment that layered hotel, retail, and civic space over the site of the former Kumamoto Kōtsū Center — a large bus terminal that had occupied the city-centre block for decades. The bus terminal was rebuilt as part of the development and remains directly connected, so visitors arriving from across the prefecture by coach can reach the hall without stepping outside.

## Reconstruction After the 2016 Kumamoto Earthquakes

The April 2016 Kumamoto Earthquakes damaged Kumamoto Castle and much of the city centre, delivering a major setback to tourism and economic activity. The Sakuracho redevelopment had been in planning for some time before the disaster, but it proceeded within the broader context of civic recovery, and the opening of Kumamoto-Jo Hall in 2019 was widely marked as a milestone in the city's reconstruction.

By establishing a large conference and cultural venue close to the castle, the hall draws international conferences, performing-arts productions, and regional trade shows to the city centre, supporting the tourism and commercial recovery that the earthquakes interrupted. The phased repair of Kumamoto Castle itself — a long-running project — continues nearby, and visitors often combine the two in a single day, moving between the working restoration site and the new civic complex.

## Summary

Kumamoto-Jo Hall — four levels, 30,000 square meters, 2,300-seat main hall — was completed in 2019 to a design by Nikken Sekkei. It anchors the Sakuramachi Kumamoto redevelopment next to Kumamoto Castle and stands as one of the more visible expressions of the city's post-earthquake recovery. If you are visiting Kumamoto, the castle grounds and the hall's public spaces together make for a half-day walk that covers both the city's history and its recent reconstruction.
