## A City Hall That Reached for the Sky in 1994

A few minutes' walk from Kasuga and Korakuen stations, a 28-story tower rises to 142 meters above the spring-green hills of Bunkyo. The Bunkyo Civic Center was completed in 1994, designed by Nikken Sekkei, combining the functions of Bunkyo City Hall with a district citizen hall, convention center, and multipurpose halls within a single high-rise shell. The idea of concentrating a ward office into a supertall building was still unusual at the time; the Civic Center became one of its clearest demonstrations that public administration could occupy a tower without sacrificing accessibility.

The building stands at the intersection of Kasugadori and Hakusan-dori, its facade visible from the street as a direct public landmark. Bunkyo City — not a major commercial ward, but densely residential and deeply rooted in the capital's educational and literary history — gave itself an architecture that announced it clearly from far away.

## The Free Observation Lounge on the 25th Floor

The reason the Bunkyo Civic Center is known well beyond its ward boundaries is a single floor: the 25th, which holds a public observation lounge open at no charge. On a clear day, the lounge offers a 360-degree panorama that takes in the Shinjuku skyline to the west, Tokyo Dome below, and — under the right conditions — Mount Fuji on the distant horizon. The view is unhurried and unmonetized, making it one of the best-kept observation secrets in Tokyo for those willing to seek it out.

That a city hall would include an open, free viewing floor reflects a particular design philosophy: public buildings should be used, not merely occupied. Nikken Sekkei's decision to give the tower a public top — accessible by elevator without a ticket or reservation — has sustained the building's civic identity across three decades, long after the novelty of a high-rise ward office had faded.

## Summary

The Bunkyo Civic Center was completed in 1994 with 28 floors above ground and a height of 142 meters. Designed by Nikken Sekkei as a concentrated public-works tower for Bunkyo City, it remains one of the most visitor-friendly high-rises in Tokyo: the 25th-floor observation lounge is free, open to anyone, and delivers a panorama of the city that many paid observatories cannot match. Walk from Kasuga or Korakuen station along the wide avenue, look up at the clean grid of the facade, then ride the elevator to 25 and take in the view that thousands of Tokyoites use every week.
