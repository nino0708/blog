## A Mountain of Green in the Heart of Tenjin

Across the street from Tenjin Central Park, ACROS Fukuoka rises 14 floors above ground and three below, completed in 1995 as a publicly led mixed-use complex. Its north face reads as a conventional office building. Its south face is something altogether different: a series of cascading planted terraces — the Step Garden — that begin at the park edge and climb unbroken to the rooftop, covering the entire façade in vegetation. From the street, the effect is of a green mountain materialising in the centre of Fukuoka's commercial district.

The design is credited to Argentine architect Emilio Ambasz in joint venture with Nihon Sekkei. Ambasz is known for repeatedly exploring the idea of "green architecture," and ACROS Fukuoka is one of his best-known built works. His concept was to treat the Step Garden as a continuation of Tenjin Central Park — a public green space that climbs the building rather than stopping at its boundary. The terraces are open to the public, and a walking route leads from street level to the rooftop, where views extend across Fukuoka City toward Hakata Bay.

## Culture and Civic Life in a Single Building

ACROS Fukuoka was developed as a third-sector project involving Fukuoka Prefecture and Fukuoka City, combining public convention infrastructure with private office and retail use. Inside, the programme includes an international conference centre, the Symphony Hall (approximately 1,800 seats, home of the Kyushu Symphony Orchestra), and commercial and exhibition spaces. In the mid-1990s, building a major international conference facility in a regional city centre was uncommon; the project reflected Fukuoka's strategic interest in positioning itself as a gateway to Asia.

The Step Garden changes with the seasons, and the accessible rooftop terrace has become a quiet landmark within Tenjin's urban walks. The contrast between the building's quiet, planted south slope and the active street life below captures something essential about the project's ambition: to give the city a new kind of public space in the form of a building.

## Summary

ACROS Fukuoka, completed in 1995, is a 14-floor mixed-use complex in Tenjin, Fukuoka City. Its Step Garden — a continuous staircase of planted terraces designed by Emilio Ambasz in joint venture with Nihon Sekkei — climbs the entire south façade and invites the public to walk from street to rooftop. Within, a symphony hall, international conference centre and offices make this one of Fukuoka's central venues for culture and business alike.
