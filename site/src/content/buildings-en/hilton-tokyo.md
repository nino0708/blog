## West Shinjuku's First International Hotel

Hilton Tokyo stands at 6-6-2 Nishi-Shinjuku, Shinjuku City, Tokyo, close to Shinjuku Central Park. The hotel opened on September 1, 1984, in a building formally named Shinjuku International Building. Mitsubishi Estate designed the structure, which rises 38 stories above ground with two penthouse floors and four basement levels, to a height of 130.80 m.

West Shinjuku's transformation into a business subcenter began in earnest after the Kyoto Prize Hotel opened in 1971, followed through the 1970s and early 1980s by a series of high-rise office towers. When Hilton Tokyo opened, it brought to the district its first internationally branded luxury hotel — filling a gap that the dense cluster of office towers around it had left. The hotel's full name in Japanese is Shinjuku Kokusai Building (新宿国際ビルディング), a label that captures its ambition to serve the district's international business character.

## An Office Tower That Hosts the World

Mitsubishi Estate's design fits the visual language of the surrounding West Shinjuku towers: a curtain-wall façade of glass and metal that lines up in silhouette with the neighboring Shinjuku Center Building and Shinjuku Nomura Building. From the vantage point of Shinjuku Central Park or the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building's observation deck, the Hilton tower reads as an integral part of the subcenter's skyline rather than a standalone landmark.

Inside, the hotel has continuously served both business travelers and tourists. It offers a large inventory of guest rooms, extensive banqueting facilities, and multiple restaurants, positioning it as a self-contained destination within West Shinjuku. The building has been renovated in stages over its four decades, keeping its facilities competitive while the surrounding district absorbed newer towers including the government complex (1991) and a steady stream of redevelopment projects in subsequent years.

At 130.80 m the building sits in a mid-tier height bracket compared with the tallest structures in the subcenter, but its hotel function — with 24-hour activity, lit façade, and street-level presence — makes it one of the most alive buildings in the area at any hour.

## Summary

Opened in 1984 at 38 stories and 130.80 m, Hilton Tokyo has served West Shinjuku as its flagship international hotel for over forty years. Mitsubishi Estate's tower blends into the subcenter's orderly skyline while offering a distinctly different program — hospitality rather than offices — in the middle of one of Tokyo's densest high-rise districts. Walk the stretch between Shinjuku Central Park and the government buildings and you will see exactly how the hotel fits into the grid that shaped the western part of the city.
