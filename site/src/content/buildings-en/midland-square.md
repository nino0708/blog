## The Tower That Defined Meieki

Step out of Nagoya Station's Sakuratsūji exit and look southeast. A quiet grid of vertical lines rises calmly into the sky — Midland Square. Standing 247 meters tall with 47 floors above ground and six below, this tower has been the tallest structure in the Chubu region since it was completed in September 2006.

The design comes from Nikken Sekkei. The form is restrained: straight vertical lines repeat up the façade without flourish, letting the sheer scale of the building express itself. The commercial podium — six floors above ground and five below — connects directly to Nagoya Station, placing it at the center of one of Japan's busiest rail hubs. Office floors fill the upper stories of the main tower. The project was constructed by a joint team of four of Japan's largest general contractors: Takenaka, Obayashi, Kajima, and Shimizu — a lineup that reflects the scope of the undertaking.

## A Corporate Castle Town Makes a Statement

The ownership structure tells its own story about Nagoya. Towa Real Estate led the project, joined by Toyota Motor Corporation and The Mainichi Newspapers — an automotive giant and a national newspaper, both with deep roots in this city. Toyota's dominance has long made the Chubu region Japan's most recognizable corporate castle town; Mainichi has maintained its Nagoya editorial and printing operations for generations. Their collaboration on a supertall building at the city's main station captures something essential about Nagoya: its industries and institutions are more tightly interlocked than in almost any other Japanese city.

The high-rise story of Nagoya Station began with the completion of JR Central Towers in the late 1990s. Midland Square's arrival advanced that story by setting a new height record for the Chubu region and reinforcing Meieki's standing as a business hub of national — and international — significance.

## Summary

Midland Square was completed in 2006 with 47 floors above ground, 6 below, and a height of 247 meters. Designed by Nikken Sekkei and developed by Towa Real Estate together with Toyota Motor Corporation and The Mainichi Newspapers, it remains the tallest building in the Chubu region nearly two decades on — a composed, vertical expression of the city's corporate strength.

Walk out of Nagoya Station to the southeast and look up. The tower of straight lines rising above the station approach carries the full weight of a city that built its identity on industry, precision, and scale.
