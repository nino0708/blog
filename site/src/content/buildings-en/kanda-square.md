## A Landmark at the Edge of Jimbocho

Between the bookshop district of Jimbocho and the moat-side greenery of Takebashi, Kanda Nishikicho has long been a quieter corner of central Tokyo. That changed in February 2020 when Kanda Square was completed: a 21-story, one-basement-level office tower rising approximately 121 meters above Chiyoda City. Sumitomo Corporation, which uses the building as its headquarters, developed the project; Nikken Sekkei provided the architectural design.

The lower floors accommodate retail and food-and-beverage tenants, adding foot traffic and street-level activity to a neighborhood that had little of either. From the upper office floors, views extend toward the Imperial Palace's forests and the Takebashi junction—an unusually open panorama for a mid-city tower.

## Renewing the Kanda Nishikicho Block

Before Kanda Square, Kanda Nishikicho was characterized by postwar low-rise and mid-rise office buildings, most several decades old. The redevelopment consolidated a fragmented block into a single high-rise footprint, freeing up public open space at street level. The result is a building that feels larger than its 21 stories suggest, partly because the surrounding street grid remains relatively unchanged.

Kanda itself carries a long commercial and intellectual history—its proximity to Jimbocho's dense cluster of publishers and second-hand booksellers gives the neighborhood a distinct character that high-rise redevelopment has not entirely erased. Walking the streets around Kanda Square, the contrast between old and new Tokyo is immediately visible.

## Summary

Kanda Square was completed in February 2020 as a 21-story, 121-meter office tower in Kanda Nishikicho, Chiyoda City. Developed by Sumitomo Corporation and designed by Nikken Sekkei, it anchors the company's Tokyo headquarters and brings a renewed streetscape to the Kanda–Jimbocho corridor. The area's mix of old bookshops and new towers makes it a rewarding walk for anyone curious about how Tokyo renews itself.
