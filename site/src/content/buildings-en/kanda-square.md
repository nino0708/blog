## Knowledge Quarter, Renewed

Halfway between Kanda and Takebashi stations in Chiyoda's Kandanishikicho district, a 21-storey office tower completed in 2020 rewrote the skyline. KANDA SQUARE stands approximately 105 metres tall on a site that for decades was home to the Kanda campus of Tokyo Denki University and, next door, the former Kanda Police Station. Developer Sumitomo Corporation acquired the land after the university relocated and commissioned Nikken Sekkei to design a tower suited to the neighbourhood's intellectual character. Obayashi Corporation handled the construction.

The building's lower floors open to a public plaza and mix of cafés and retail, inviting the pedestrian flow that animates Kanda's streets. The mid and upper floors provide large-floor-plate office space that appeals to companies looking for a step away from the density of Marunouchi yet close to major transport connections.

## Kanda's Layered History

Kanda has long been Japan's bookselling capital — Jinbocho, with its second-hand bookshops, is a ten-minute walk away — and the broader area has drawn printing, publishing, and technology firms for over a century. KANDA SQUARE fits into that tradition. The site carries a kind of institutional memory: a university campus and a police station stood here, each representing civic life in its own way. The tower doesn't erase that history so much as overlay it with a contemporary workspace.

Since opening, the ground-floor plaza has become a lunchtime gathering spot for the neighbourhood's office workers, and the building has drawn tenants from both established industries and newer digital businesses. The result is a tower that reads as modern yet entirely at home in Kanda's textured streetscape.

## Summary

KANDA SQUARE was completed in 2020 — 21 floors, roughly 105 metres. Sumitomo Corporation developed the project and Nikken Sekkei designed it on the former site of Tokyo Denki University's Kanda campus. Walk over from Kanda Station, then continue to Jinbocho to combine a look at this new landmark with a wander through one of Tokyo's most beloved old-book districts.
