## A Truss Shaped by Airspace and Sea

On 12 February 2012, a distinctive steel bridge opened over Tokyo Bay between Wakasu in Kōtō City and the Chuo Breakwater outer reclaimed island. Tokyo Gate Bridge measures 2,618 metres in total length, with the main sea-spanning section reaching 1,618 metres. The structure rises 87.8 metres above the water surface at its highest point, and its 440-metre main span makes it Japan's second-longest truss bridge after Osaka's Port Bridge. The two massive truss girders facing each other across the channel prompted the affectionate nickname "Dinosaur Bridge" — the paired ribcage-like steelwork does suggest a skeleton rearing from the sea.

The form was not chosen for visual drama alone. Haneda Airport's flight paths pass directly overhead, imposing a strict height ceiling on the entire structure. At the same time, major Tokyo Port shipping lanes require adequate clearance beneath the deck for large vessels. A suspension bridge would have raised towers too high. The solution was a steel three-span continuous truss-box composite structure: strong enough to span the channel without tall towers, and compact enough to stay within the airspace limit. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's Kanto Regional Development Bureau was the project authority; basic and detailed design was carried out by Central Consultants and Pacific Consultants.

## Road Access to the Reclaimed Island

The bridge is not a stand-alone structure. It forms the centrepiece of the Tokyo Port Coastal Road (Phase II), an approximately 8-kilometre corridor connecting Jōnanjima via the Chuo Breakwater outer reclaimed area to Wakasu. The outer reclaimed island has long received Tokyo's municipal waste and hosts a range of facilities and logistics facilities; until the bridge opened, land access required a lengthy detour or sea transport. The new road connection significantly improved trucking efficiency and reorganised the port's freight network.

At the Wakasu end of the bridge, Wakasu Kaihin Park provides a riverside approach. The four-lane motorway itself is vehicle-only, but the park's shoreline offers close views of the two truss spans rising against the sky — an industrial silhouette that reads as one of Tokyo Bay's most striking civil structures.

## Summary

Tokyo Gate Bridge, opened 12 February 2012, spans 2,618 metres with a 440-metre main truss span and stands 87.8 metres above the water. Its paired truss-box girders — a direct product of the airspace and navigation constraints that ruled out a suspension bridge — have given the crossing an identity as vivid as any designed landmark on the bay.

From Wakasu Kaihin Park the full bridge profile is visible against the horizon, with [Rainbow Bridge](/buildings/rainbow-bridge) and the city skyline framed in the distance. The Kōtō waterfront extends naturally toward the new developments at [Tokyo Port City Takeshiba](/buildings/tokyo-portcity-takeshiba).
