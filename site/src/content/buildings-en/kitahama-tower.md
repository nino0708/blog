## A Riverside Landmark in the Heart of Osaka

Along the Tosabori River in Chuo Ward, Osaka, a 54-story residential tower was completed in March 2009. Known officially as The Kitahama, it rises to 209.4 metres — Japan's tallest residential building at the time of its completion. The tower stands where the historic streets of Kitahama meet the water, bringing a contemporary vertical statement to one of Osaka's most storied neighbourhoods.

Kitahama has long been Osaka's financial heart. Home to the Osaka Securities Exchange for generations, and often called "the Wall Street of the Orient," the neighbourhood carries a dense legacy of Western-style Meiji- and Taisho-era buildings. The Tosabori River traces its southern edge; across the water lies [Nakanoshima Festival Tower](/en/buildings/nakanoshima-festival-tower/) and the civic island of Nakanoshima, with its tree-lined promenade and historic public hall. The Kitahama thus rises at the intersection of commerce, history, and the water that defines Osaka as a city of rivers.

## A Ten-Company Partnership at Scale

Ten companies formed the development consortium, including Sanyo Homes, NTT Urban Development, and Heiwa Real Estate. Design was handled jointly by Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei and Nihon Sekkei, with construction by Kajima Corporation and Haseko Corporation. The sheer number of parties reflects both the scale of the undertaking and the complexity of land rights in a dense urban block — a pattern familiar to students of Japanese urban redevelopment.

The 54-story program stands comparison with the commercial towers of the Osaka Business Park and the mixed-use blocks of [Grand Front Osaka](/en/buildings/grand-front-osaka/) to the north. A riverside garden terrace at the base and a refined lobby set the tone for residents; from the upper floors, the layered panorama of river, bridges, brick buildings and [Abeno Harukas](/en/buildings/abeno-harukas/) on the southern horizon makes The Kitahama's position clear.

## Summary

The Kitahama, completed in March 2009, stands 54 storeys above ground (one basement level) and reaches 209.4 metres. It was, at completion, the tallest residential tower in Japan, and it remains a landmark in Osaka's Kitahama district. A ten-company joint venture brought it to life, bringing an ambitious residential tower into a neighbourhood shaped by centuries of commerce.

Walking the riverside promenade from the Kitahama side, you can look up at 209 metres of residential tower framed by the historic streetscape — a vivid reminder of how Osaka's past and present share the same skyline.
