## An office tower at the edge of Meieki

Northwest of Nagoya Station, in the Nishi-ward address of Meieki 2-chome, the Nagoya Prime Central Tower rises to 23 floors — 106.23 metres. Completed in 2009, the building was developed by a special-purpose company formed by Tokyo Tatemono and Marubeni, with Shimizu Corporation handling both the design and the construction. Floors 3 through 23 are given over entirely to offices; the ground floor holds retail. The result is a practical office tower without architectural flourish, built to serve the concentrated demand for workspace that has defined the Meieki district since Japan National Railways handed the land to private developers.

Nagoya Station — referred to locally as Meieki, a compression of the station's Japanese name — is one of Japan's most intensely developed transit hubs outside central Tokyo. The opening of JR Central Towers in 1999 set the template: a terminal building as a vertical city in its own right. Midland Square (247 metres), JR Gate Tower (220 metres), and a succession of mid-rise buildings filled in around them. Nagoya Prime Central Tower occupies this middle tier. It is not among the tallest or the most celebrated buildings in the cluster, but it adds usable office floor space to a district in consistent demand for it.

## Tokyo capital in Nagoya

The pairing of Tokyo Tatemono and Marubeni as developers reflects a trend that accelerated through the 2000s: major metropolitan real-estate companies moving into Nagoya's redevelopment independently of the local corporate hierarchy. Historically, the largest Nagoya projects were tied to JR Tokai, Meitetsu, or other regional anchors. The arrival of Tokyo-based developers on their own account signalled that central Nagoya had become attractive enough to justify the capital commitment without a local partner's backing.

Shimizu's design-build arrangement kept responsibility unified under a single contractor — a pragmatic choice for a mid-rise office project where schedule efficiency and accountability matter more than formal distinction between architect and builder.

## Summary

Nagoya Prime Central Tower was completed in 2009: 23 floors, 106.23 metres. Developed by Tokyo Tatemono and Marubeni, built by Shimizu Corporation, it represents the steady accumulation of commercial floor space around Nagoya Station that has made Meieki one of Japan's most concentrated office addresses outside Tokyo. Explore the area and you'll find that Nagoya's tower landscape rewards the patient walker — there is more to the story than the landmark skyscrapers at its centre.
