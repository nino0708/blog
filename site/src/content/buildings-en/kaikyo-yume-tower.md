## A 153-Metre Tower Over the Kanmon Strait

Kaikyo Yume Tower stands in Buzenndacho, Shimonoseki, Yamaguchi Prefecture — a few minutes' walk from JR Shimonoseki Station. Completed in 1996, the 30-floor, 153-metre tower was developed by Yamaguchi Prefecture and designed by NTT Facilities. Its observatory sits at 143 metres, and on clear days visitors can look straight across the Kanmon Strait to Kitakyushu on the opposite shore.

The tower was developed as part of a complex that also includes a convention facility, blending tourism and conference functions. It is listed with the Japan Tower Association and is known for its nighttime illumination, which reflects off the strait below and has made the structure a recognisable symbol of the city. Its current official name, Ovision Kaikyo Yume Tower, reflects a naming-rights sponsorship arrangement.

## Shimonoseki and the Weight of Its Location

Shimonoseki occupies the westernmost tip of Honshu, facing Kitakyushu across the narrow Kanmon Strait. The city has been a strategic maritime gateway for centuries — the meeting point of the Seto Inland Sea and the Sea of Japan. In the final years of the Edo period, Western gunboats clashed with Choshu domain forces here in what is known as the Shimonoseki Campaign. In 1895, the Treaty of Shimonoseki (Sino-Japanese War peace treaty) was signed in the city, anchoring Shimonoseki to a turning point in modern Japanese history.

The 1996 tower gave residents and visitors a new vantage point: looking down on a strait that has carried ships for many centuries. The view from the observatory frames that history in a way few other perspectives can.

## Summary

Completed 1996, 30 floors, 153 m. Kaikyo Yume Tower stands at Shimonoseki's gateway, offering sweeping views of the Kanmon Strait from an observatory at 143 m. The experience of looking from the tip of Honshu across to Kyushu is well worth the journey.
