## A New Civic Centre for the Kannai District

Yokohama's new city hall was completed in 2020 on the site of the former city hall in Honcho, Naka Ward. The building is 32 above-ground floors, 2 basement floors, a 2-floor crown structure, and a height of 155.4 metres. It connects directly to Bashamichi Station on the Minatomirai Line, making the main public service counters accessible without street-level navigation from the nearest underground station.

The building was designed by a joint venture of Takenaka Corporation and Maki and Associates. Fumihiko Maki, whose practice has designed major public buildings across Japan including Makuhari Messe and structures at Tokyo's Hillside Terrace, led the architectural side of the partnership. Construction was carried out by a Takenaka and Nishimatsu Construction joint venture. The former city hall, built in 1959, was demolished to make way for the new building; separate plans are under development for the cleared site.

## Consolidating City Services Under One Roof

Before the new building opened, Yokohama City's administrative functions were spread across multiple facilities, requiring residents to travel between buildings for different services. The new city hall consolidates the city's main departments into a single address, allowing most municipal procedures to be completed in one visit.

The Kannai and Kangai district, where the new city hall stands, is Yokohama's historic city centre. In recent years the area has seen new activity: startup support facilities have opened, and the former city hall site is being planned for mixed-use redevelopment. The new city hall sits at the heart of this change, providing a stable civic anchor while the surrounding blocks are reconfigured for new uses.

## Summary

Yokohama's new city hall was completed in 2020 as a 32-floor, 155-metre tower in the Kannai district. Designed by Takenaka Corporation and Maki and Associates, the building consolidates the city's administrative functions in one directly station-connected building. Its position in the historically significant Kannai area places it at the centre of ongoing urban regeneration efforts in central Yokohama.
