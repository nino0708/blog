## A Factory Becomes a City

LaZona Kawasaki Plaza opened on 28 September 2006 at 72-1 Horikawa-cho, Saiwai, Kawasaki, on land that had for decades housed a Toshiba heavy-industry plant. Developed jointly by Mitsui Fudosan and NREG Toshiba Real Estate (now Nomura Real Estate), the complex covers a total floor area of approximately 172,303 square metres. Exterior design was contributed by Ricardo Bofill, the Barcelona-born architect known for his neo-classical vocabulary and monumental housing projects in France and Spain. Structural and interior design was handled by Yamashita Sekkei.

Before 2006 the west side of Kawasaki Station had little reason to detain a commuter. Rail passengers transferred and kept moving; the commercial energy of the city was concentrated on the east side. LaZona reversed that pattern with a single opening: the large arched entrance facing the station plaza announces a retail destination rather than a factory gate, and the flow of foot traffic around Kawasaki Station has been different ever since.

## Bofill's Arches and the Open Plaza

Ricardo Bofill's contribution is most visible in the curved, limestone-toned façade that arcs across the station-side frontage. Rather than presenting a solid retail wall to the street, the design opens toward the station plaza through large colonnaded arches, treating the public square in front as a continuation of the building's own space — an approach more common to civic architecture than commercial development. The effect on arrival is of entering a place rather than a store.

Inside, a central open-air terrace (the rooftop garden level) and a retail layout that encourages circulation across multiple floors reinforce the sense of the building as urban space. Kawasaki had long been defined by its industrial heritage; LaZona made the argument, through a single building, that the same land could serve a different kind of city life after the factories closed.

## Summary

LaZona Kawasaki Plaza, opened 2006, covers approximately 172,303 square metres on the former Toshiba Horikawa-cho factory site in Saiwai, Kawasaki. The complex brought Ricardo Bofill's distinctive arched façade to a former industrial district, and with it a new pedestrian logic to Kawasaki's west-exit plaza. For anyone interested in urban redevelopment or simply in how a city reinvents itself after heavy industry, the contrast between the building and the industrial history beneath it is worth the detour from Kawasaki Station.
