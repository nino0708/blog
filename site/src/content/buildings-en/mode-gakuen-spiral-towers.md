## A Spiral Tower Built by a Vocational School

Meieki 4-chome, Nakamura Ward, Nagoya. Northwest of JR Nagoya Station, three curved fins twist around a central core and rise 170 metres into the sky. Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers was completed on 25 March 2008. Developed by the Japan Education Foundation — the parent organisation of vocational colleges operating under the Mode Gakuen brand — and designed by Nikken Sekkei with construction by Obayashi Corporation, the building is 36 stories above ground and three below. Its name describes both the spiral exterior and the schools within: Mode Gakuen (fashion and design), HAL Nagoya (IT and game design), and an allied medical-welfare college, collectively enrolling thousands of students in a single high-rise.

The decision to house an educational institution in a landmark-calibre skyscraper was deliberate. The Japan Education Foundation operates on the idea that architecture is a recruiting tool — a building with a memorable silhouette functions as a permanent, citywide advertisement for the schools inside. The same logic drives the organisation's Cocoon Tower in Shinjuku, Tokyo, which opened a year later and packages the same concept in a different organic form.

## Nagoya's Skyline Receives an Outlier

The towers that define Nagoya Station's skyline are overwhelmingly commercial: office floors, hotel rooms, retail podiums. Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers introduces a third institutional type alongside them, and the exterior articulates that difference. The three spiralling fins are not a purely cosmetic gesture: they also perform a structural role, stiffening the tower against lateral loads, though the primary driver of the form was the client's desire for visual impact.

Commercial space managed by Mitsui Fudosan occupies parts of the lower floors, opening the building to visitors who have no connection to the schools above. The ground floor and basement levels are therefore more porous than a pure educational building would be, making the tower a usable part of the city rather than a closed campus elevated above street level.

## Summary

Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers, completed in 2008, is a 36-floor, 170-metre vocational school skyscraper whose spiral silhouette stands apart from the commercial towers of Nagoya Station's skyline. The Japan Education Foundation's strategy of using architecture as identity was applied here with consistent conviction.
