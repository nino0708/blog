## A Steel Arc Over the Strait

Kanmon Bridge stretches 1,068 metres across the Kanmon Strait, linking Shimonoseki in Yamaguchi Prefecture with Moji Ward in Kitakyushu, Fukuoka Prefecture. It opened on 14 November 1973 as part of the Kanmon Expressway (a branch of the Chugoku Expressway), and upon completion it was Japan's first expressway bridge to span a sea strait. The main span measures 712 metres, the twin pylons rise 133.8 metres above sea level, and the deck clears the water by approximately 61 metres — enough headroom for large ocean-going vessels.

The Kanmon Strait, at its narrowest barely 700 metres wide, has long divided and connected Honshu and Kyushu. Ferry crossings once served travellers for centuries; the Kanmon Rail Tunnel, completed in 1942, was the first fixed link to burrow beneath the strait. The bridge chose the opposite approach: soaring above the water in a single elegant sweep. Its 712-metre main span made it the longest suspension bridge in Japan at the time of opening.

## Landmark of the Strait

From the Shimonoseki shore, the bridge frames the ancient battlefield of Dan-no-ura — site of the 1185 sea battle that ended the Genpei War — against the industrial skyline of Moji beyond. Cargo ships, ferries and tankers pass beneath it in a constant procession, a reminder that the strait remains one of Japan's busiest shipping lanes. Together with the Kanmon Road Tunnel (National Route 2), the bridge gives the region two fixed road crossings, cementing its role as a pivotal junction between the two main islands.

## Summary

Opened in 1973, Kanmon Bridge spans 1,068 m with a 712-metre main span and 133.8-metre pylons. Japan's first sea-strait expressway crossing, it still carries heavy traffic daily and stands as the defining landmark of the Kanmon region. Walk the Shimonoseki waterfront, watch the ships pass beneath the bridge, and let the strait's long history of crossings settle in.
