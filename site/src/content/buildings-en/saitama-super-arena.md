## A Movable Core That Makes a Multipurpose Arena

Saitama Super Arena stands in Saitama Shintoshin, the planned urban centre developed in Chuo Ward of Saitama City. Developed by Saitama Prefecture and co-designed by Nikken Sekkei and American firm Ellerbe Becket (now HOK), the arena held a soft opening in May 2000 and its full opening in September 2000.

The defining feature is the MOVE system: a large section of the arena floor sits on rails and can be repositioned to convert between configurations. In Arena Mode — with capacity reaching approximately 37,000 — the layout suits major sports events. Stadium Mode, used for concerts and other performances, creates a different spatial relationship between the stage and the audience. This flexibility has made the venue home to professional basketball (B.League), large-scale music events, and combat sports competitions alike.

## Saitama Shintoshin and the Arena's Role

Saitama Shintoshin was redeveloped from the former site of a Japanese Army munitions factory and former Japanese National Railways railyard. Through the 1990s, government office complexes, the arena and retail facilities were built up together around what became Saitama Shintoshin Station (opened 2000), served by JR Utsunomiya, Takasaki, Keihin-Tohoku and Saikyo lines. The Super Arena is the symbolic centrepiece of the new district, forming part of a cluster with the adjacent Cocoon City shopping complex and government buildings.

## Summary

Completed 2000, Saitama Super Arena is Saitama Prefecture's flagship large-scale venue, with capacity up to 36,500 and a unique movable-core system that adapts the space for different events. Directly accessible from JR Saitama Shintoshin Station, it is a cornerstone of the Shintoshin development.
