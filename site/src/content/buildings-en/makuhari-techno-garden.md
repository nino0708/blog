## An Intelligent Building for a New Town

Makuhari Techno Garden opened in April 1990 in Mihama Ward, Chiba City, as a 25-storey, 106-metre complex on the reclaimed waterfront of Makuhari New Town. The design was carried out by the Makuhari Techno Garden Design Joint Venture, a consortium of Shimizu Corporation, Nippon Steel Corporation, and NTT. The complex centres on twin towers — B Building and D Building — connected by lower podium structures.

At the time of its opening, the complex drew wide attention as a pioneering example of an "intelligent building" developed under Japan's Act for Promotion of Private-Sector Development (民活法). Its advanced information and communications infrastructure, combined with energy-saving systems, set a benchmark for office design in the early 1990s. The architectural and technical quality was recognised with the 32nd BCS Prize (Building Contractors Society Award).

## Makuhari New Town: A Planned City

When Makuhari Techno Garden opened, Makuhari New Town was still taking shape. Reclaimed from Tokyo Bay over some 522 hectares, the district was designed to become an international business and research hub for Chiba Prefecture. [Makuhari Messe](/en/buildings/makuhari-messe), which opened in 1989, led the way, followed by office, residential, and retail facilities filling in through the early 1990s.

The twin towers of Techno Garden became part of the emerging skyline seen from the east, and their white façades remain a recognisable element of the district more than three decades on. The complex is a few minutes' walk from JR Kaihin-Makuhari Station, making it easy to visit alongside Makuhari Messe and the surrounding waterfront area.

## Summary

Makuhari Techno Garden opened in 1990 with 25 floors above ground and a height of 106 metres, standing as one of the first major intelligent office buildings in Japan. Its BCS Prize win and its role in Makuhari New Town's formation give it a notable place in the history of Japanese commercial architecture.
