## A Tower Straddling the Tracks

Diamond Gate Ikebukuro, completed in February 2019, is a 20-storey office tower rising 99.98 metres above Toshima ward. The building's most striking feature — a pair of diagonal X-braces that crisscross its façade — is not merely decorative. Because the tower stands directly over the operating tracks of the Seibu Ikebukuro Line, conventional pile foundations were impossible in that zone, and the X-braces carry the building's lateral loads instead. This makes Diamond Gate the first high-rise in Japan erected over a live urban railway.

## The Name in the Steel

The building's name layers several meanings. "Daiya" (ダイヤ) refers simultaneously to the Japanese word for a railway timetable (daiyagram), the diamond silhouette traced by the X-braces, and the Seibu Group's identity colour. Developed by Seibu Railway and Seibu Properties, with Nikken Sekkei as design architect and a joint venture of Obayashi Corporation and Seibu Construction as contractor, the 49,661 m² tower now houses the Seibu Holdings headquarters alongside a range of office tenants. Its completion in early 2019 marked a new phase for the south exit of Ikebukuro Station — an area where further large-scale redevelopment projects are still under way, continuing to reshape the neighbourhood's skyline.

## Summary

Diamond Gate Ikebukuro opened in 2019 as a 20-storey, 99.98-metre tower — Japan's first skyscraper to span operating railway tracks. Its X-brace structure resolves an engineering challenge imposed by the Seibu Ikebukuro Line running directly beneath the building while giving it a distinctive silhouette on the Ikebukuro skyline. Step out of the south exit of Ikebukuro Station and look up: the tower's angular steel geometry makes an unmistakable first impression on the street.
