## 185 metres: a high-water mark in Musashi-Kosugi

Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki — Musashi-Kosugi. By 2016, the race to build tall in this district had been running for the better part of a decade. City Tower Musashi-Kosugi, completed in January of that year, raised the bar again: 53 floors above ground, 3 below, 185 metres, 800 units. Developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development, it ranks among the tallest residential buildings in Kanagawa Prefecture, and stands as a marker of the district's high-rise ambitions reaching one of their most tangible expressions.

The tower's height places its upper floors well above the surrounding rooflines — views toward the Tama River, the Tokyo lowlands, and the distant ranges are among its defining characteristics as a residence. The building joins a district where high-rise towers have changed the skyline in every direction: The Kosugi Tower (49 floors, 2008) and Park City Musashi-Kosugi Mid Sky Tower (59 floors) are among the neighbours that together make this one of the most concentrated tower clusters outside central Tokyo.

## Transport, growth, and the question of infrastructure

Musashi-Kosugi has drawn residential investment largely because of its transport connections: the Tokyu Toyoko Line, the Nambu Line, and the JR Yokosuka and Shonan-Shinjuku Lines all converge here, making central Tokyo and Yokohama easily reachable. That accessibility has driven rapid population growth. The question of how the station, roads, schools and utilities keep pace with the new residents that each successive tower brings has become part of Musashi-Kosugi's public story, and City Tower Musashi-Kosugi, as one of the largest additions, is central to that conversation.

## Summary

City Tower Musashi-Kosugi was completed in January 2016, with 53 floors above ground, 3 below, a height of 185 metres, and 800 residential units. Developed by Sumitomo Realty & Development, it stands as one of the tallest residential towers in Kanagawa, embodying the full ambition of Musashi-Kosugi's transformation from an industrial suburb into a high-rise district. Walking the area today — looking up at the towers that now define the skyline — is to experience one of the most dramatic urban changes in the greater Tokyo region over the past two decades.
