## Three Wings Spiralling Above Nagoya Station

A short walk from the Sakuradori exit of Nagoya Station in Nakamura Ward, the Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers were completed in 2008. The building stands 170 metres tall across 36 storeys above ground and three below, designed by Nikken Sekkei and built by Obayashi Corporation, with construction beginning in 2005. The three educational wings — each reaching a different height, at 26, 31 and 36 storeys respectively — twist around each other as they rise, lending the building an appearance unlike any other in the Nagoya skyline.

The structure houses three specialist schools: Nagoya Mode Gakuen (fashion), HAL Nagoya (computers and design) and Nagoya Isen (medical and welfare). Each wing corresponds to one of the schools. The engineering approach — connecting three towers of different heights into a single unified structure — was a demonstration of Japanese construction technology at the time of its completion.

## A Campus That Redefines the Streetscape

Nagoya Station's immediate surroundings are home to some of the densest high-rise development in Aichi Prefecture — Midland Square, JR Gate Tower and other towers form a compact cluster around the terminal. Within this environment, the Spiral Towers stand apart by being shaped first and foremost by the idea of a building rather than by its function. The irregular window rhythm and the spiralling facade give it an expressive presence that the surrounding commercial towers do not share.

The building functions as a fully working campus, with classrooms, studios and libraries spread across the floors. Upper-level views across Nagoya's urban fabric are part of the daily experience for students who pass through the building.

## Summary

The Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers were completed in 2008: 36 storeys, 170 metres tall, housing three specialist schools in a triple-helix tower designed by Nikken Sekkei near Nagoya Station. The building demonstrates that a large educational facility can generate architectural boldness, adding a genuinely distinctive silhouette to one of Japan's busiest station districts.
