## Three Fan-Shaped Wings in a Spiral

Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers stands in Tsubaki-cho, Nakamura Ward, Nagoya, a short walk from the west exit of JR Nagoya Station. Completed in March 2008, the building rises 36 stories above ground (3 below) to a height of 170 meters. It was designed by Nikken Sekkei and built by Obayashi Corporation.

The building's defining characteristic is its three fan-shaped classroom wings that rotate around a shared core, creating a continuous spiral silhouette. Three schools of the Mode Gakuen educational group share the structure: Nagoya Mode Gakuen (fashion and design), HAL Nagoya (computer science), and Nagoya Isen (healthcare and welfare). Each wing was allocated to one of the three schools, stacked vertically within the same tower. The building is, at its core, a school that thousands of students commute to every morning — yet its sculptural form has earned coverage in international architecture media.

## A School Among Office Towers

The area around Nagoya Station has become one of Japan's most concentrated clusters of high-rise construction, with [JR Central Towers](/buildings/jr-central-towers/) (1999), [Midland Square](/buildings/midland-square/) (2007), and [JR Gate Tower](/buildings/jr-gate-tower/) (2017) nearby. Spiral Towers joined this skyline in 2008 while standing apart from all of them — not an office building, not a hotel, but an educational facility.

The wing geometry means the tower never shows the same profile twice as you walk around it. During the day the metal cladding catches light from different angles; at night, interior lighting filters through the glass to create a glowing lantern effect. The structural engineering required to realize this angular form earned recognition alongside Nikken Sekkei's architectural design.

## Summary

Mode Gakuen Spiral Towers (2008, 36 floors, 170 m) is a one-of-a-kind vocational school tower designed by Nikken Sekkei. Its spiraling facade makes it one of the most recognizable silhouettes in the Nagoya skyline.
