## A Mitsubishi Tower on the Okawa Riverfront

Walking along the Okawa River near Temmabashi in Osaka, a slender tower comes into view above the tree-lined embankment. This is OAP Tower, the main building of the Osaka Amenity Park (OAP) complex. Completed in January 1996, the 39-story building stands 176.3 metres above ground level in Kita Ward, Osaka, and was jointly developed by Mitsubishi Estate and Mitsubishi Materials.

OAP was planned as a mixed-use district incorporating the tower alongside a hotel building, retail facilities, and rental housing. The concept of clustering multiple urban functions on a riverside site facing the Okawa represented a kind of ambitious city-making that characterised major Japanese developers in the bubble era — and OAP was one of the projects that survived the economic downturn of the early 1990s to actually reach completion.

## A 1990s Complex That Shaped the Riverfront

OAP Tower was designed not as a single-function office building but as a mixed tower with a hotel occupying its upper floors. At 176 metres and 39 floors, it remained one of the taller buildings in Osaka's Temmabashi area for years after its opening. The complex as a whole offered a model of riverside urban living and working that would later become more common — but in 1996, the combination of offices, hotel, housing, and retail along the Okawa was relatively novel.

Temmabashi station, served by the Tanimachi subway line and the Keihan railway, connects directly to the complex, making OAP convenient for both business and leisure. Osaka Castle Park lies a short walk to the east, and Nakanoshima Island is close to the west, placing OAP at a natural crossroads of Osaka's cultural geography. The riverside promenade below the tower offers a pleasant approach, with the building's glass face reflecting sky and water in different ways depending on the time of day.

## Summary

OAP Tower was completed in 1996 as a 39-story, 176-metre mixed office and hotel building on the Okawa riverfront in Osaka. Developed by Mitsubishi Estate and Mitsubishi Materials, it stands as a landmark of 1990s riverside urban development in the city. Walking along the Okawa promenade and looking up at the tower from the water's edge remains one of the more rewarding ways to appreciate how Osaka's Temmabashi district has accumulated its layers of history and modern form.
