## A Compact Tower Reshapes the North Exit

In March 2010, a new chapter opened for the north side of JR Shizuoka Station. Aoi Tower — 25 floors above ground, two below, with a total floor area of around 53,300 square metres — was completed as the culmination of an urban redevelopment association project in Konya-cho, Aoi Ward. Total project costs reached approximately 23 billion yen, and the association took about four years from establishment to construction completion.

The tower's programme layers uses vertically in a way that reads almost like a cross-section of civic life: basement and ground levels for retail, the third floor dedicated to the Shizuoka City Museum of Art, and floors seven through twenty-three set aside for offices. The project is a textbook example of public–private urban regeneration, bringing a city art museum into a developer-led tower without separating it from the daily flow of people.

## A Museum Embedded in a Business Tower

The Shizuoka City Museum of Art is perhaps the most distinctive element of Aoi Tower. Locating a municipally operated art institution within a mixed-use commercial building is uncommon in Japan, yet here it works seamlessly: elevators from the street-level plaza carry visitors directly to the gallery level, keeping the museum accessible as a destination in its own right rather than buried in a podium.

Shizuoka City sits almost exactly between Tokyo and Nagoya on the Tokaido corridor, served by the Tokaido Shinkansen and the Tokaido Main Line. The city has long served as a manufacturing and logistics hub, and the Aoi Tower's completion reflected ambitions to strengthen the urban core to match that regional significance. A comparable exercise in provincial-city landmark-making is visible along the same corridor in nearby [Act City Hamamatsu](/en/buildings/act-city-hamamatsu/), which brought an international convention facility and 45-floor hotel tower to Hamamatsu Station.

## Summary

Aoi Tower was completed in March 2010 — 25 floors, with a total floor area of approximately 53,300 m². It stands as a model of urban redevelopment in a regional city, weaving a public art museum and commercial space into a single high-rise frame. If you pass through Shizuoka on the Shinkansen or stop for the city's famous green tea, walk north from the station and look for the tower — and consider going up to the third floor to see what a city museum embedded in a skyscraper feels like.
