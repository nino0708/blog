## Japan's Largest Football Stadium

Saitama Stadium 2002 stands in the green belt of Midori Ward, Saitama, with a capacity of 63,700 seats — the largest of any dedicated football stadium in Japan. Completed in 2001 to a design by Azusa Sekkei, the stadium was built in anticipation of the 2002 FIFA World Cup co-hosted by Japan and South Korea, with its name embedding the year of the tournament itself.

The building's most distinctive feature is its sweeping roof structure, rising to approximately 58 meters and covering the stands in a form said to recall a pair of egrets in flight. The white steel arches curve dramatically over the seating bowl, shielding spectators from the elements while giving the stadium a striking silhouette above the flat, low-rise surroundings on the eastern outskirts of Saitama City.

## From the World Cup to a Football Stronghold

The stadium hosted four matches during the 2002 FIFA World Cup, including knockout-round fixtures that brought the world's best players to Saitama for the first time. After the tournament it became the permanent home of Urawa Red Diamonds, one of the most supported clubs in Japanese football, and their home matches at the stadium regularly sell out all 63,700 seats in a spectacle of red and noise that rivals atmospheres anywhere in Asia.

Beyond club football, the stadium has served repeatedly as a venue for Japan national team matches and for finals of the Emperor's Cup. The nearest station is Urawa-Misono on the Saitama Railway, roughly fifteen minutes on foot from the stadium gates — a walk that, on match day, turns into a river of red shirts.

## Summary

Completed in 2001 and designed by Azusa Sekkei, Saitama Stadium 2002 is a 63,700-seat football-only venue and the largest of its kind in Japan. Its soaring roof, echoing the wings of egrets in flight, is a landmark visible for miles across the flat Kanto plain. It made its mark on football history during the 2002 FIFA World Cup and has since become the heartbeat of Urawa Red Diamonds supporters — a stadium worth visiting whether a match is on or not.
