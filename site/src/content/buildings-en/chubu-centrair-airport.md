## An Airport Built at Sea

Chubu Centrair International Airport sits on a man-made island about 1.5 kilometers off the coast of Tokoname City in Ise Bay. The name "Centrair" blends "Central" and "Air," capturing both the airport's geographic position at the heart of Japan and its role as an aviation gateway. It opened on February 17, 2005 — the same year Aichi hosted Expo 2005 — and quickly established itself as a significant international hub for the Chūbu region.

The artificial island covers approximately 580 hectares and required around 16.7 million cubic meters of fill material. The passenger terminal sits along the northern edge of the island, with check-in halls, departure gates, a hotel, and a commercial complex integrated into a single continuous building. The design prioritizes a clear separation between departure and arrival flows while creating a relaxed, marketplace-style atmosphere in the landside areas.

## Connecting the Chubu Region

Centrair is directly linked to central Nagoya by the Meitetsu Airport Line, with the fastest trains reaching Meitetsu Nagoya Station in approximately 28 minutes. The airport also maintains a maritime access point on the southern side of the island, offering ferry connections to ports along the Ise Bay coast.

The 3,500-meter runway allows 24-hour operations — a significant advantage over the older Nagoya Airport (Komaki Airport), which had noise curfew restrictions. This around-the-clock capability supports overnight cargo flights and early morning departures that are essential for international business travel and logistics in the automotive and manufacturing industries concentrated in the Chūbu region.

## Summary

Chubu Centrair International Airport opened in 2005 on an artificial island in Ise Bay. Its single 3,500-meter runway operates 24 hours a day, and a direct rail link connects the terminal to central Nagoya in about 28 minutes. The airport functions as the Chūbu region's international gateway, and its distinctive setting above the sea — visible from the large windows of the observation deck — makes the journey itself worth the trip.
