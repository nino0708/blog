## A New Gateway Rising from Ise Bay

On 17 February 2005, Chubu Centrair International Airport (IATA: NGO) opened on a man-made island in Ise Bay, off the coast of Tokoname City in Aichi Prefecture. It was Japan's second offshore airport, following Kansai International Airport — itself built on a reclaimed island in Osaka Bay — which had opened a decade earlier in 1994.

The timing was deliberate: Central Japan International Airport Co., Ltd. (the developer and operator, 60% government-owned with Toyota Motor Corporation as the leading private shareholder) designed the opening to precede the 2005 Aichi World Exposition by just a few weeks, giving international visitors a modern air gateway to the Chubu region. The airport has a single runway stretching 3,500 metres. Terminal 1 integrates domestic and international services in a single compact building; a second, low-cost carrier-focused Terminal 2 (designed by Gensler) opened in September 2019.

## Infrastructure at Sea, with a Train to the Door

What distinguishes Centrair from many international airports is the seamlessness of its rail connection. The Meitetsu Centrair Line — an extension of the existing Tokoname Line — runs directly onto the artificial island and brings passengers from Nagoya Station in roughly 28 minutes. The absence of a bus transfer between city and terminal is a convenience that shapes the entire travel experience from the moment of departure.

The airport island itself was conceived as a destination, not merely a transit point. Amenities include an open-air observation deck overlooking the runway, a commercial zone called Sky Town, a traditional bathhouse (onsen), and Flight of Dreams — a facility built around a Boeing 787 Dreamliner, a nod to the aircraft that the nearby Mitsubishi and Spirit AeroSystems supply chain helped bring to production. For comparison, [Kansai International Airport](/en/buildings/kansai-international-airport/) in Osaka was similarly built offshore but on a larger scale; the two airports share the challenges and attractions of island-based infrastructure.

## Summary

Chubu Centrair International Airport opened in February 2005: a single-runway offshore airport on a man-made island in Ise Bay, served directly by the Meitetsu Centrair Line from Nagoya. It opened as the gateway for the 2005 Aichi Expo and remains the principal international airport for the Chubu region.
