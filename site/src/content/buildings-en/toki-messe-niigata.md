## The Tallest Tower Facing the Sea of Japan

On 1 May 2003, the skyline of Niigata's Bandaijima district gained its defining feature. The Toki Messe Bandaijima Building — 31 floors above ground, one below, reaching a height of approximately 140.5 metres — was designed and built by a joint venture led by Kajima Corporation. An observation room on the 31st floor, roughly 125 metres above street level, offers what is described as the highest public viewpoint on the Sea of Japan side of the Japanese archipelago.

Bandaijima is a landfill island near the mouth of the Shinano River, occupying a position between the commercial port and the city centre of Niigata. The tower's transparent metal-and-glass façade changes register through the day, turning pale silver in morning light and deep blue as afternoon clouds gather. From the water it reads as a clearly modern landmark against a skyline that otherwise runs low.

## Convention Hub for the Nihonkai Corridor

Toki Messe is not just a tower. The Bandaijima Building sits within a larger convention complex that includes a large exhibition hall and meeting rooms accommodating events from international conferences to trade fairs. The facility positions Niigata — historically the gateway city for trade across the Sea of Japan to the Asian mainland — as a convention destination for the wider Chubu-Hokuriku region.

The 2004 Chuetsu Earthquake tested Niigata's resilience, but the city rebuilt, and Toki Messe continued to operate as a convention and cultural venue. The view from the 31st floor — Sado Island visible on clear days, the Echigo plain stretching inland, and the Shinano River winding below — rewards visitors with a rare panorama of Japan's western coast.

## Summary

Toki Messe Bandaijima Building was completed in May 2003 — 31 floors, approximately 140 metres tall, with an observation room at 125 metres. Designed and built by Kajima Corporation's joint venture, it stands as the tallest structure on Japan's Sea of Japan coast and the convention centrepiece of Niigata City. On a clear day the view from the top takes in Sado Island across the water — a compelling reason to make the trip.
