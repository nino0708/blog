## A Tower Born from a Hot-Spring Expo

Beppu Tower stands at 3-10-2 Kitahama, Beppu City, Oita Prefecture, and was completed in May 1957. It was designed by Tachu Naito, the structural engineer known as the "Tower Doctor" for his series of iconic Japanese towers — a group that includes Tokyo Tower and Tsutenkaku in Osaka. The observation deck is located approximately 55 meters above ground, offering panoramic views of Beppu Bay and the city's steaming hot-spring district below.

The tower was originally planned as the centerpiece of the 1957 Beppu Hot-Spring Tourism and Industry Expo — a major local event intended to reinvent Beppu's image as a modern tourist destination. Known at first as the "Tourism Center TV Tower," it was renamed Beppu Tower in 1961. The red-and-white painted steel frame has been carefully maintained since completion, and the tower underwent renovation work in 2023 to welcome a new generation of visitors.

## A Modernist Icon Among the Steaming Vents

Beppu City is one of Japan's most celebrated hot-spring destinations, known for the highest concentration and volume of hot-spring output in the country. Against this backdrop of natural steam rising from the streets and hillsides, Beppu Tower's steel silhouette offers a striking counterpoint — the engineered and the geological side by side. The tower is situated in the central Kitahama district, within easy walking distance of the city's shopping streets and bathhouse clusters.

In 2007, Beppu Tower was designated a National Registered Tangible Cultural Property, recognizing its architectural and historical significance as one of Japan's postwar observation towers. It joins the broader family of Naito-designed towers — sometimes called the "Six Tower Siblings" — as a surviving example of mid-twentieth-century Japanese tower engineering. For visitors interested in the history of Japanese architecture alongside the pleasure of the region's natural hot springs, Beppu Tower is a compact but rewarding stop.

## Summary

Beppu Tower was completed in 1957 to a design by Tachu Naito. The observation deck sits at approximately 55 meters above ground in central Beppu, overlooking the bay and the hot-spring cityscape. Designated a National Registered Tangible Cultural Property in 2007, it remains one of the finest surviving examples of Japan's postwar tower-building era. Visiting Beppu without stepping inside the tower would mean missing a piece of the city's modern soul alongside its ancient waters.
