## 150 Metres Over a City Reborn

Rihga Royal Hotel Hiroshima opened in April 1994 in Naka Ward, Hiroshima City — 35 storeys above ground, 2 below, and 150 metres tall. Standing northwest of Hiroshima Castle, the tower ranks among the tallest structures in the city and can be seen from most of the central district. Its predecessor, the Shin-Hiroshima Hotel, opened in 1955, in the early years of the city's postwar reconstruction, and was eventually replaced by the current high-rise.

The building contains guest rooms, restaurants, banquet halls, and wedding facilities spread across its floors. The 150-metre height gives higher-floor rooms views across the Hiroshima delta plain and, on clear days, the islands of the Seto Inland Sea.

## A City Between History and the Present

Hiroshima's cityscape carries a particular weight of history. Rebuilt after 1945 according to a planned reconstruction layout, the city has wide boulevards and a regular street grid that give it a distinctive spaciousness. High-rise buildings appear as single accents against this open fabric rather than as dense clusters. Rihga Royal Hotel Hiroshima, with its 150-metre profile near the castle grounds, is one such accent.

The Peace Memorial Park and the Peace Memorial Museum are roughly a ten-minute walk from the hotel, as is the Atomic Bomb Dome (a UNESCO World Heritage Site). For international visitors, the proximity of a large hotel to these sites makes it a natural base for a Hiroshima stay.
