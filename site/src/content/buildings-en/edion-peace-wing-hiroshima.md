## A New Sporting Heart for the City of Peace

On the left bank of the Motoyasu River in Naka Ward, Hiroshima — just steps from the Peace Memorial Park — Edion Peace Wing Hiroshima opened on 1 February 2024. The football-only stadium holds approximately 28,520 spectators and stands on the former site of Hiroshima Municipal Stadium, the longtime home of the Hiroshima Toyo Carp baseball team. The venue was designed by a joint team of Higashihata Architects, the Institute of Environmental Design, and Taisei Corporation Architects & Engineers. Its arrival gave Hiroshima's urban core a new sporting focal point for the first time in decades.

The stadium's defining characteristic is the closeness of the stands to the pitch. Because the design omits an athletics track, the front rows press right up to the touchlines, creating an atmosphere in which supporters can almost feel the rhythm of play. For fans of the home club, Sanfrecce Hiroshima, this proximity amplifies every moment of a match. The design drew reference from modern European football stadiums where tight-fitting stands have become the standard.

## A Stadium Shaped by Its City

The choice of a city-centre location was deliberate. Placing the stadium within walking distance of central Hiroshima makes it accessible by transit and allows match days to energise nearby streets and restaurants. The site itself carries particular civic meaning: the former Municipal Stadium was a beloved piece of collective memory, and its transformation into a modern football ground was the result of years of community discussion.

The name "Peace Wing" (Heiwa no Tsubasa) acknowledges the history of Hiroshima — a city rebuilt in the shadow of the Peace Memorial. The building received attention at the JIA Grand Prize 2025 nominations for its architectural quality. On match days, supporters in purple Sanfrecce shirts fill the plaza outside, turning the stadium into a vivid centrepiece of the city.

## Summary

Edion Peace Wing Hiroshima opened in 2024 and holds around 28,520 spectators. Built on the storied ground of the former Hiroshima Municipal Stadium, it brings professional football to the heart of the city with stands close enough to the pitch that the game feels almost touchable. The stadium is an easy walk from the Peace Memorial and the A-Bomb Dome, making it a natural part of any visit to Hiroshima.
