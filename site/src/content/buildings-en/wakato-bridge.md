## A Red-Painted Span Over an Industrial Bay

Wakato Bridge crosses Dokai Bay in Kitakyushu City, Fukuoka Prefecture, connecting Tobata Ward to the south with Wakamatsu Ward to the north. Construction began in March 1959, and the bridge opened to traffic on 26 September 1962. It is a three-span two-hinge stiffened truss suspension bridge. The suspended span measures 627.3 meters, the main span is 367 meters, and the pylons rise approximately 84 meters above the water. Total bridge length including the approach spans reaches 2.1 kilometers. At the time of its completion, it was the longest suspension bridge in East Asia.

The bridge's red-painted pylons and truss girders stand in vivid contrast to the gray industrial backdrop of Dokai Bay, the waterway that for much of the twentieth century formed the core of Kitakyushu's heavy industry zone. That combination of bold engineering and industrial scenery has given Wakato Bridge an iconic place in the city's visual identity.

## From Tolled Crossing to Heritage

For decades the bridge operated as a toll road. In December 2018 the toll was lifted, making Wakato Bridge freely accessible to all users for the first time and increasing its daily traffic. Throughout its history it functioned alongside the Wakato Tunnel — another crossing of Dokai Bay — to handle the freight and commuter traffic flowing between the industrial districts on either shore.

In recognition of its technical achievement and historical significance, Wakato Bridge was designated a National Important Cultural Property (Structure) in February 2022. The designation reflects a broader Japanese movement to treat industrial and civil engineering infrastructure as cultural heritage — a parallel to the World Heritage listing of the Meiji Industrial Revolution sites that also cluster in Kyushu. For comparison, [Kanmon Bridge](/buildings/kanmon-bridge/) crosses the Kanmon Strait some twenty kilometers to the west, but Wakato Bridge predates it by roughly a decade.

## Summary

Wakato Bridge was completed in September 1962 as a 627.3-meter stiffened truss suspension bridge crossing Dokai Bay in Kitakyushu. After more than sixty years of carrying the industrial traffic of one of Japan's most productive port cities, it was designated a National Important Cultural Property in 2022. The bridge's red pylons reflected in Dokai Bay are a sight worth seeking out on any visit to Kitakyushu.
