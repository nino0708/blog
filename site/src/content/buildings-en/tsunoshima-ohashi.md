## A Bridge Over an Emerald Sea

Tsunoshima Bridge extends 1,780 metres from the northern tip of the Yamaguchi mainland out to Tsunoshima Island, crossing a strait of transparent water that shifts from emerald to cobalt blue depending on the light and the tides. The bridge opened on November 3, 2000, after seven years of construction, and has since drawn visitors from across Japan who come specifically to see—and drive across—a road that seems to float above the sea.

The bridge is free to use. There are no tolls, no special permits required—just open road across shallow water. At the time of its opening it was the longest toll-free bridge in Japan; it has since been surpassed by the Kouri Bridge in Okinawa, but its combination of length, setting, and open access keeps it among the most celebrated spans in the country.

## Designed Around the Landscape

Tsunoshima Bridge sits entirely within Kitanagato Quasi-National Park, and the restrictions that came with that designation shaped the finished structure. To preserve views of the sea from both the road and the surrounding coastline, the height of each bridge pier was capped at 18 metres. This low profile is part of what gives the crossing its particular quality: rather than arching over the water, the bridge slides across it at nearly sea level.

The gentle curve of the alignment is not merely aesthetic—it follows the need to bypass Hato Island, a small outcrop that lies in the direct path of a straight crossing. The result is a sweeping arc that photographers have been framing from the hillside lookout ever since the bridge opened.

Tsunoshima Island itself rewards a visit beyond the bridge. A lighthouse from 1876, a small fishing harbour, and a quiet stretch of beach make up most of the island's offerings. The bridge brought convenience to the islanders, but the pace of life on Tsunoshima has remained much its own.

## Summary

Tsunoshima Bridge, completed in 2000, is a 1,780-metre prestressed concrete viaduct managed by Yamaguchi Prefecture and open to all traffic without charge. Its low bridge piers were designed to protect the landscape of Kitanagato Quasi-National Park, and the crossing over the shallow, colourful sea has become one of the most photographed roadway scenes in Japan. If you are travelling along the San'in coast, a detour to drive this bridge is one worth building into the itinerary.
