## Japan's First National Performing Arts Complex

A short walk from Hatsudai Station on the Keio New Line, in Honmachi, Shibuya-ku, a low stone-clad complex stands alongside the soaring Tokyo Opera City Tower. This is the New National Theatre, Tokyo — completed in February 1997 and inaugurated with its first performance in October of the same year. It is Japan's first state-operated performing arts complex dedicated to contemporary opera, ballet, dance, and drama. The building rises five floors above ground and descends four below, with a total floor area of 68,879 m². It was designed by Takahiko Yanagisawa of TAK Architects & Urban Planners.

Before this theatre opened, Japan had no national performing arts venue of its own. European countries had established state opera houses by the nineteenth century, but Japan relied on NHK Hall and private venues. The concept gained momentum during the bubble era, and the Hatsudai site — on the western edge of Shinjuku — was chosen for its transport access and large footprint. The result is a complex that houses three distinct performance halls under one management.

## Three Halls, One Mission

The Opera House (the main hall) seats up to 1,814 and contains a fully equipped orchestra pit with hydraulic risers, a fly tower, and a wide stage suited to grand opera and large-scale ballet. The Playhouse seats approximately 1,038 and is configured for drama and contemporary dance, with flexible staging options. The Pit, the smallest hall at around 468 seats, is designed for experimental and studio-scale productions.

The exterior takes a horizontal, stone-faced approach that deliberately contrasts with the vertical glass tower beside it. This restraint allows the complex to read as a cultural ground plane against the commercial high-rise — an intentional urban pairing that defines the character of the Hatsudai precinct.

## Summary

The New National Theatre, Tokyo was completed in 1997, with five floors above ground and four below totaling 68,879 m². It was designed by Takahiko Yanagisawa and operates as Japan's first state-run performing arts venue, with three purpose-built halls serving opera, ballet, dance, and drama.
