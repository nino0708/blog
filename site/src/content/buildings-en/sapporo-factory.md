## A Meiji Brewery Reborn as a City Destination

In Kita 2-jo Higashi 4-chome, Chuo-ku, Sapporo — a short walk from subway stations Odori and Bus Center — a cluster of red brick and glass rises over a city block. This is Sapporo Factory, a commercial complex that opened in 1993 on the site of the Kaitakushi Beer Brewery, founded in 1876 by the Meiji government's Hokkaido Development Commission as Japan's first state-run brewery. The site spans seven interconnected buildings with a total floor area of 123,322 m² rising nine floors above ground.

The story of the site stretches back 150 years. The Kaitakushi commission brought German brewing expertise to Hokkaido and established the brewery as part of a broader colonization project. The enterprise passed into private hands and eventually became Sapporo Breweries. In 1892 a red-brick factory building was added to the complex, and the site continued as the heart of Sapporo's brewing industry for decades. When production shifted to a suburban plant, the urban site was cleared for redevelopment. Construction began in 1989 and the complex opened in 1993.

## Heritage and Modern Commerce Under One Roof

The defining design move was retaining the 1892 brick warehouse rather than demolishing it. That building now anchors the complex's interior, where a tall glass-vaulted atrium connects old masonry walls to contemporary retail floors. The brick's warm texture against the steel-and-glass atrium produces a layered sense of time that few shopping centers can match.

Practical considerations shaped the design as much as aesthetics. Hokkaido winters are long and severe, so an enclosed indoor circuit links all seven buildings, letting visitors move between shops, restaurants, and the atrium without stepping outside. In 1997 the Kaitakushi Brewery was revived inside the complex, adding a working craft brewery and guided tours that connect the commercial facility back to its industrial heritage.

## Summary

Sapporo Factory opened in 1993, integrating a preserved 1892 red-brick warehouse into a nine-floor, 123,322 m² commercial complex on the site of Japan's first government brewery. It stands as one of Japan's most cited examples of industrial heritage adaptive reuse.
