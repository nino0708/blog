## The World's First Driverless Railway in Commercial Service

On 5 February 1981, an automated transit line began running from Sannomiya station in central Kobe out to Port Island, a large artificial island reclaimed from Osaka Bay. The Kobe Port Liner — formally the Kobe New Transit Port Island Line — operates as an automated guideway transit (AGT) system: rubber-tyred vehicles on a dedicated elevated track, fully computer-controlled, with no driver on board. At the time of its opening it was the world's first automated guideway transit line to enter regular commercial service, making Kobe a global showcase for the technology.

The vehicles run smoothly and quietly on the concrete guideway, with wide front-facing windows that give an unobstructed view of the route ahead. From Sannomiya the line crosses onto the artificial island and loops around its interior before continuing to Kobe Airport station, a stop added in February 2006 when the line was extended to serve the new domestic airport built on a second artificial island off the coast of Port Island. The full route now spans 12 stations.

## Port Island's Innovation City and Its Railway

Port Island was created through a large-scale land reclamation project the City of Kobe began in the 1960s. The island's opening was marked in 1981 by Portopia '81 — the Kobe Port Island Exposition — and the Port Liner opened alongside the fair as a demonstration of Kobe's ambition to build a future-oriented city on the sea. After the exposition closed, the railway continued to serve the island as an everyday transit line, evolving in step with Port Island's development into a hub for medical research, international conventions, and university facilities.

Today the line is an essential service for the Kobe Medical Industry City cluster on Port Island, connecting hospitals, research institutes, and the convention centre to central Kobe without transfers. The 2006 extension to Kobe Airport brought the line an additional purpose: for travellers arriving at Kobe Airport on domestic flights, the Port Liner provides a direct, driverless connection to Sannomiya and onward to the broader Kobe and Osaka rail network.

## Summary

The Kobe Port Liner opened in February 1981 as the world's first commercially operated driverless transit system and has since grown to 12 stations connecting Sannomiya, Port Island, and Kobe Airport. It remains the lifeblood of Port Island's medical and research community, and its extension to Kobe Airport in 2006 added an international travel dimension. Board the front car from Sannomiya on a clear day, and the view across the bay and around the island's curved guideway is an experience the city still earns its reputation for.
