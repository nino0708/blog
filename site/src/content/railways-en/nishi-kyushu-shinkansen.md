## Japan's Newest Shinkansen Reaches Nagasaki

On 23 September 2022, the Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen opened for service between Takeo-Onsen and Nagasaki. The line covers approximately 66.0 kilometres and serves five stations: Takeo-Onsen, Ureshino-Onsen, Shin-Omura, Isahaya, and Nagasaki. Trains run as six-car sets of the N700S Series (8000 sub-series), operated by JR Kyushu with its own distinctive livery and interior design. At the time of opening it was described as one of the shortest shinkansen sections in Japan, drawing nationwide attention.

## A Relay System Across Kyushu

The Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen currently operates without through-running to the main Kyushu network. Passengers travelling from Hakata must ride the Relay Kamome limited express to Takeo-Onsen, then transfer across the same platform to board the shinkansen. The combined journey from Hakata to Nagasaki takes a minimum of 1 hour and 20 minutes. Extending the line northward through Saga Prefecture to connect with the existing Kyushu Shinkansen remains a subject of negotiation between JR Kyushu, Saga Prefecture, and the national government.

## A New Station at the Hot Springs

One of the most distinctive elements of the new line is Ureshino-Onsen Station — the first rail connection ever to reach this historic hot spring resort in Saga Prefecture. The new shinkansen station offers visitors a direct link between the hot springs and the broader rail network for the first time. Around Shin-Omura Station, residential and commercial development has advanced since opening, following a pattern of station-area growth seen along other recently opened lines.

The five-station route traverses the mountainous interior of western Kyushu, passing through terrain that had long complicated rail construction in the region. The name "Kamome" — the sea gull — that had belonged to the Hakata–Nagasaki limited express for decades carries forward in the new shinkansen service, linking old and new chapters in this route's history.

## Summary

The Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen opened in September 2022, connecting Takeo-Onsen and Nagasaki across 66 kilometres in five stations. Running as the N700S Series operated by JR Kyushu, the line shortens the Hakata-to-Nagasaki journey to 80 minutes via relay connection — bringing a new chapter in rail access to one of Japan's most historically significant cities.
