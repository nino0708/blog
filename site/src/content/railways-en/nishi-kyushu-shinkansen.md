## The Shinkansen Reaches Western Kyushu

The Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen opened on September 23, 2022, bringing high-speed rail to the western part of Kyushu for the first time. The line runs 69.6 kilometers from Takeō-Onsen Station in Saga Prefecture to Nagasaki Station, stopping at five stations: Takeō-Onsen, Ureshino-Onsen, Shin-Ōmura, Isahaya, and Nagasaki. Purpose-built N700S-8000 series trains operate under the "Kamome" (Seagull) brand name, covering the fastest Takeō-Onsen–Nagasaki trip in about 23 minutes.

The line was built to full shinkansen gauge (1,435 mm standard gauge) and design speed, following the Act for Development of Nationwide Shinkansen Railway Network. The tracks run mostly on elevated viaducts through the hilly terrain of western Kyushu, with stretches near Ōmura City offering views across Ariake Sea.

## The "Face-to-Face Transfer" Solution

The most distinctive feature of the Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen is its transfer arrangement at Takeō-Onsen. Because the Saga Prefecture section between Takeō-Onsen and Shin-Tosu has not been built as shinkansen track, there is no through service to Hakata. Instead, passengers transfer at Takeō-Onsen between the shinkansen "Kamome" and the conventional limited express "Relay Kamome," which runs to and from Hakata on existing tracks. The two trains wait at parallel platforms facing each other, making the exchange seamless and minimizing waiting time to a few minutes.

Ureshino-Onsen Station opened as one of the few shinkansen stations in Japan located within a traditional hot-spring resort, creating a direct boost to tourism along the line.

## Summary

The Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen, which opened in September 2022, links Takeō-Onsen and Nagasaki over 69.6 kilometers at a design speed of 260 km/h. Its face-to-face transfer arrangement at Takeō-Onsen represents a practical, if temporary, answer to the incomplete route through Saga Prefecture. With stops at two onsen resort towns, the line has already changed travel patterns across western Kyushu and will remain at the center of debates about future extensions.
