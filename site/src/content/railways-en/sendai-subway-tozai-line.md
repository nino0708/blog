## East Meets West Beneath the City

Sendai's terrain divides sharply between a range of hills and plateaus to the west — including Aobayama and Taihakusan — and the flat Sendai Plain stretching to the east. The Tozai Line, which opened on December 6, 2015, crosses this topographical divide over 13.9 kilometers and 13 stations, from Yagiyama-Dobutsukoen Station in the forested hills to Arai Station near the eastern edge of the urban area. The line uses linear-motor traction, which drives the train through electromagnetic force rather than rotating wheel motors. This technology allows a smaller tunnel diameter, reducing construction costs, and provides strong adhesion on steep slopes — essential here because the line's western section includes grades as steep as 55‰ (55 meters of rise per 1,000 meters of distance).

All 13 stations are equipped with platform screen doors, improving safety and reducing the draft created by passing trains.

## A Hub at Sendai Station

At Sendai Station, the Tozai Line intersects with the older Namboku Line (opened 1987), which runs north to south through the city. The two lines share the same central station but occupy separate underground levels, with concourses connecting them. This interchange makes Sendai Station a natural focal point for cross-city travel.

The western terminus, Yagiyama-Dobutsukoen, serves the Sendai City Zoo; Kawauchi Station is a short walk from Tohoku University's Kawauchi Campus; and Miyagino-dori Station provides access to the eastern commercial areas of the city. The line's opening completed a public transport axis across a city that previously had no east–west rail connection.

## Summary

The Sendai City Subway Tozai Line opened in December 2015, connecting the western hills to the eastern plain over 13.9 kilometers with 13 stations. Its linear-motor system was the key technical choice that made it possible to tackle the city's steep western terrain within a practical budget. Transferring to the Namboku Line at Sendai Station, it is easy to use the two subway lines together to reach most of the city's main attractions in a single underground journey.
