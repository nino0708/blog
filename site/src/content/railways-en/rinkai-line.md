## 12.2 Kilometres Through the Waterfront Sub-Centre

The Rinkai Line, run by Tokyo Waterfront Area Rapid Transit, spans 12.2 kilometres and 8 stations between Osaki and Shin-Kiba. It opened first between Shin-Kiba and Tokyo Teleport in March 1996 and was completed on 1 December 2002 with the extension to Osaki. Linking key facilities of the waterfront sub-centre such as Odaiba and Tokyo Big Sight, it has served as the feet of the new districts built on reclaimed land.

Most of the line runs in tunnel, boring beneath the bay-side ground and under the harbour. The Rinkai Line was born of a clear urban-planning aim: to support the development of the waterfront sub-centre from the transport side. Direct access from the city centre to the exhibition-thronged Big Sight and the shopping district of Odaiba carries real weight.

## Joined in One Line with the Saikyo Line

What defines the Rinkai Line's usefulness is its through service with JR's Saikyo and Kawagoe lines via Osaki. Rinkai Line trains run straight onto the Saikyo Line, passing Shibuya, Shinjuku, and Ikebukuro and reaching as far as Kawagoe. That ties the waterfront sub-centre to the western Yamanote terminals without a transfer, greatly improving movement around the bay area.

By connecting the waterfront directly to the centre, this line added a new axis to Tokyo's rail network — one that stitches diagonally across the inside and outside of the loop. Its operation is simple, with every train stopping at all stations, yet as part of the through-service network it covers a wide reach.

## Summary

The Rinkai Line, 12.2 kilometres between Osaki and Shin-Kiba, has carried access to the waterfront sub-centre of Odaiba and Tokyo Big Sight. With its full opening in 2002 and through service to the Saikyo and Kawagoe lines, the bay-side districts were joined to Shibuya, Shinjuku, and Ikebukuro on a single ride. Emerge from the tunnel near Shin-Kiba and the bay sky opens in the window, revealing the shape of a new Tokyo raised on reclaimed land.
