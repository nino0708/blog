## Shibuya to Motomachi-Chukagai — One Train, No Transfer

On 1 February 2004, the Yokohama Minatomirai Railway Minatomirai Line opened for service. The 4.1-kilometre underground line connects Yokohama Station to Motomachi-Chukagai Station across six stations, and it launched with simultaneous through-running service with the Tokyu Toyoko Line. For the first time, passengers could board a single train at Shibuya and step off at Yokohama's historic waterfront without changing.

The Minatomirai Line's opening came alongside a closure: the Toyoko Line's terminal stretch between Yokohama and Sakuragicho operated its last train on 30 January 2004 and was retired. The above-ground terminus that had long served as the gateway to the old port district gave way to the new underground connection at Yokohama Station. The line was planned in step with the ongoing redevelopment of the Minato Mirai 21 district, and each station along the new underground section received an individual architectural treatment — the brick-toned walls of Bashamichi Station, near the Red Brick Warehouse, being the most distinctive.

## Shibuya Goes Underground — 2013's Major Shift

On 16 March 2013, the Toyoko Line underwent its second major transformation. The approximately 1.3-kilometre section between Shibuya and Daikanyama was placed underground, and Shibuya Station relocated to a new subterranean platform. Simultaneously, through-running began with the Tokyo Metro Fukutoshin Line, extending the network to the Tobu Tojo and Seibu Ikebukuro lines. Shibuya had been the Toyoko Line's terminal; from that morning it became a through station.

The former elevated platforms at Shibuya were absorbed by the surrounding redevelopment — Shibuya Hikarie, Shibuya Scramble Square — and the neighbourhood's transformation accelerated. The ability to travel in one seat from Saitama to the Yokohama waterfront is now simply how the line works.

## Summary

The 2004 Minatomirai Line opening and the 2013 Shibuya underground switch are the two moments that made the modern Tokyu Toyoko Line. A local route once anchored at Shibuya's above-ground terminus became a metropolitan axis carrying through trains from the Kanto hinterland to Yokohama's seafront. Standing on the platform at Minatomirai Station and watching a train from Saitama emerge from the tunnel into harbour air is the clearest measure of what those two pivots built.
