## A Bridge of Two Facing Dinosaurs

The Tokyo Gate Bridge opened on 12 February 2012, a 2,618-metre truss bridge across Tokyo Bay. It links Wakasu in Koto Ward with the reclaimed Central Breakwater and forms part of the Tokyo Port Waterfront Road. Because its two central trusses look like a pair of dinosaurs facing each other with jaws outthrust, it is fondly known as the "Dinosaur Bridge." The roadway stands 54.6 metres above the sea.

This distinctive shape is no design whim but the solution to two strict constraints at once. Large ships passing beneath require ample clearance, while the airspace near nearby Haneda Airport imposes a height limit. A suspension or cable-stayed bridge with tall towers would breach that limit. And so a truss bridge — which secures strength without reaching high overhead — was chosen.

## A Waterfront Road for Bay-Area Logistics

The Tokyo Gate Bridge serves to make the traffic and logistics concentrated on the waterfront more efficient. It was built to ease chronic congestion and smooth movement around the bay area, where container terminals and distribution hubs cluster. The bridge includes a footway, and on opening day a long line of pedestrians formed in search of the view. The prospect over the central skyline and Tokyo Bay is another of the bridge's attractions.

The run of rugged truss steel is functional beauty stripped of ornament. A form arrived at inevitably from purpose and constraint has, in the end, produced a striking landmark on Tokyo Bay. Standing at the tip of reclaimed land, the bridge symbolises the leading edge of Tokyo's ever-expanding harbour infrastructure.

## Summary

The Tokyo Gate Bridge, a 2,618-metre truss bridge opened in 2012, has linked Wakasu with the Central Breakwater as part of the Tokyo Port Waterfront Road. Its "Dinosaur Bridge" form was inevitably drawn from two constraints: clearance for large ships and the height limit near Haneda Airport. Walk the footway and, through the rugged steel truss, Tokyo Bay and the central skyline open before you — the power of a shape born of function felt at close range.
